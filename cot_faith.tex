\begin{abstract}
Vision-language-action models that emit chain-of-thought are proposed as a route to interpretable robotics, on the premise that the emitted reasoning drives the action. This benchmark and the one prior line that also tests it score an edit by the \emph{magnitude} of the action change it induces. We show that this scoring rule's validity depends on which meaning-preserving edit is nominated as its floor, and that the more defensible choice does not hold up model by model. A meaning-preserving paraphrase moves the action \emph{at least as much} as semantic edits do on 11 of 11 configurations (9 significant, 7 Holm-corrected); against a second, length-exact floor the semantic mean is higher on 10 of 11 but only 3 reversals are significant (1 Holm-corrected), and the two floors disagree with each other more than either disagrees with the semantic mean on 10 of 11, so neither tested floor can be treated as canonical by the benchmark itself. We establish this with CoT-Faith: one paired-intervention protocol on 15 manipulation VLAs in four model families, ten edit families and three calibration nulls, 324 scored cells. The score is close to a decode-collision counter, and a threshold-free divergence confirms the same collapse. Requiring the action to point the \emph{implied} direction moves the top-ranked model from first of eight to seventh, and the two rankings agree only weakly. A rule-based oracle sanity check recovers high directional faithfulness ($\mathcal{F}_{\text{dir}}=0.925$), showing that the pipeline detects the constructed causal dependence, without validating the diagnostic's calibration on any learned policy. Any score of this kind should be read against a per-model null and an action-space diagnostic before it licenses a claim; ours admits none of our own eight submissions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Vision-language-action (VLA) models that emit chain-of-thought reasoning alongside their action tokens are widely proposed as a route toward interpretable robotics: the reasoning is expected to expose the model's plan for review, correction, or safety monitoring \citep{ecot}. This premise, that the emitted CoT \emph{drives} the subsequent action decode, underwrites CoT-based safety monitors, corrective interventions, and CoT-derived auditing, and has not been evaluated for manipulation VLAs under uniform conditions. Recent work evaluates CoT-faithfulness for autonomous-driving VLAs \citep{vladrivebench,pinocchio} and probes CoT-based safety monitors on a three-model manipulation cohort \citep{doubleedged}; a cross-model, cross-corpus, standardized manipulation benchmark is missing (related work: Appendix~\ref{sec:related}). We built one, applied it to 15 VLAs across 4 model families, and the result is one claim about the instrument rather than a set of findings about the models. The magnitude-based edit score that this benchmark and the one other perturbation-based precedent we know of is built on is not a calibrated measure of faithfulness: its floor-corrected interpretation is unstable, with all 11 configurations falling below the paraphrase floor while 10 of 11 reverse sign against a length-exact scramble floor (\S\ref{sec:floors}). Three further strands test that verdict, each a way it could fail instead: the score is close to a decode-collision counter (\S\ref{sec:collision}); a direction-aware diagnostic exposes a qualitatively different failure mode and disagrees at the top of the ranking (\S\ref{sec:directional}); and retraining one recipe unchanged moves the floor-corrected statistic by up to $0.068$, more than most between-model gaps (\S\ref{sec:variance}); the negative result is the safety-relevant contribution. This is not a narrow measurement quibble: as CoT-emitting VLAs move from research demonstrations toward settings where a monitor or a human overseer is meant to read the reasoning before trusting the action, the validity of the score used to certify that the reasoning is what moved the action is a precondition for any of those downstream uses, not an optional check on top of them. Figure~\ref{fig:overview} (\S\ref{sec:benchmark}) carries the claim and the first two strands.

\paragraph{Contributions.} The claim of \S\ref{sec:floors} is the contribution; \S\S\ref{sec:collision}--\ref{sec:variance} are the three strands that make it hard to escape. Two things support them. The benchmark (\S\ref{sec:benchmark}): ten edit families spanning six capability axes (Appendix~\ref{sec:taxonomy}) in a three-tier taxonomy plus three calibration nulls, on $\sim$100 LIBERO first-step samples per family per model, with a self-generated-CoT sweep on Bridge~V2 \citep{bridgev2} via Open X-Embodiment \citep{openx}, and a direction-aware score $\mathcal{F}_{\text{dir}}$, the one instrument here with a measured null, which motivates a six-stage admission protocol (Appendix~\ref{sec:eval_protocol}) rather than a validated replacement. A release built to be checked (\S\ref{sec:release}): all per-sample records, an audit script asserting 1,632 claims against them, and an admission rule that rejects all eight of our own submissions.

Figure~\ref{fig:overview}(a) shows this protocol on one released example first.

We take a VLA's emitted CoT to be faithful to the extent that the decoded action depends counterfactually on its semantic content, and only on that content, giving two testable model conditions and one instrument condition. \textbf{Selectivity (D1)}: a meaning-changing edit must move the action more reliably than a meaning-preserving one. \textbf{Sign-consistency (D2)}: an edit that reverses an implied direction must reverse the action, not merely displace it. \textbf{Calibration (D3)}: any score must be read against a per-model null, since the same score means different things on models with different decode geometry. Each strand previewed above addresses one of these three; \S\ref{sec:floors}'s is D1.

\subsection{Measurement Protocol}

For each sample $s$ and edit family $f$ we decode greedily twice (once on the unedited CoT, once on the edited CoT), holding the observation, the instruction and the decode fixed, and compare the two 7-DoF actions:
\begin{equation}
\Delta_\infty^{s,f} = \max_{i \in [7]}\bigl|\,a_i(s, f(\text{cot}_s)) - a_i(s, \text{cot}_s)\,\bigr|,
\label{eq:delta}
\end{equation}
with $a_i$ normalized to $[-1,1]$. We define the per-sample response indicator $\mathbf{1}[\Delta_\infty^{s,f} > \tau]$, $\tau = 0.05$, and summarize it as
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{F}(m,f) = \tfrac{1}{N}\textstyle\sum_s \mathbf{1}[\Delta_\infty^{s,f}(m) > \tau].
\label{eq:faith}
\end{equation}
$\mathcal{F}(m,f)$ is the response statistic this benchmark and its one precedent report as a faithfulness score. Whether that response rate is in fact a valid measure of faithfulness, rather than an assumption this paper makes, is exactly the question \S\ref{sec:floors} tests.
This is a paired, within-policy, within-observation intervention against the model's own null; it does not presuppose competence and licenses no claim about a policy that completes the task (\S\ref{sec:release}). Figure~\ref{fig:frames} (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix}) shows what one such edit does over a whole rollout, not one decode, and what the policy read while it did.

Eq.~\ref{eq:delta} credits movement in \emph{any} direction. For families whose semantics imply a signed consequence we require the sign. After \emph{direction\_flip} reverses left$\leftrightarrow$right / up$\leftrightarrow$down / in$\leftrightarrow$out,
\begin{align}
\mathcal{F}_{\text{dir}} &= \tfrac{1}{N}\textstyle\sum_s \mathbf{1}\!\left[c^f_s < -0.5\right], \label{eq:fdir}\\
c^f_s &= \cos\bigl(a_{1:3}(s,\text{cot}_s),\, a_{1:3}(s,f(\text{cot}_s))\bigr) \nonumber
\end{align}
with analogous criteria for \emph{gripper\_flip} and \emph{negation}.

The primary sweep uses teacher-forced CoTs to isolate the intervention instrument, holding the reasoning text itself fixed except for the edit under test; generated-CoT robustness is evaluated separately (\S\ref{sec:floors}).

\subsection{Edit Taxonomy}

Thirteen families: ten in three tiers and three calibration nulls, with a real example per family in Appendix~\ref{app:sec:benchmark}. \emph{paraphrase\_null}, \emph{bbox\_jitter\_null} and \emph{instr\_random\_sub} are the calibration nulls, the part of the design we would keep if we kept nothing else. \emph{selfsplice\_control} must score $0$ and does on 11/11 CoT-VLAs; the judge export names it \emph{identity\_control} and groups it with the nulls, so the judge scores 12 of the 13. Figure~\ref{fig:overview}(a)'s \texttt{MOVE} lines are the released \emph{direction\_flip} pair; full descriptions are in Appendix~\ref{app:sec:benchmark}.

A blind LLM judge (\texttt{Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct} \citep{qwen25}, in neither architecture family under test, following \citet{mtbench}'s protocol; full details in Appendix~\ref{sec:judge_edits}), gated on an identity control ($1.000$) and a pooled negative control ($0.175$), validates the generators over 437 pairs: \emph{paraphrase\_null} preserves meaning at $0.975$, \emph{bbox\_jitter\_null} at $1.000$ (both read against an $0.824$ order-flip agreement floor), so the meaning-preserving labels are supported by an independently gated judge rather than assumed, and one family's name oversells it: \emph{adversarial\_plausible} is judged plausible on only $0.125$ of pairs, corrected in the taxonomy (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix}). A second judge (\texttt{Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2}) fails its own negative-control gate (Appendix~\ref{sec:judge_edits}), so we do not treat its agreement with the first as corroboration.

\section{Results}
\subsection{The Magnitude Score's Floor Is Not Canonical}
\label{sec:floors}

\begin{figure*}[t]

Figure~\ref{fig:heatmap} is the full $\mathcal{F}$ surface behind this section: all eight leaderboard configurations $\times$ all 13 edit families. \texttt{no-CoT} is low across every semantic column, not just on average, and \emph{cross\_task\_swap}, the ceiling, is high for all eight; \texttt{ECoT-bridge}'s two missing cells (\emph{bbox\_jitter\_null}, \emph{instr\_random\_sub}) are inapplicable to its released norm-stats, not zero.

A faithful model should be \emph{insensitive} to an edit that preserves meaning, so $\mathcal{F}$ on \emph{paraphrase\_null} is a \emph{floor} a faithful model should stay \emph{under}, not a lower bound on anything good. Write $\bar{\mathcal{F}}$ for the mean of $\mathcal{F}$ over the seven non-control semantic families (\emph{direction\_flip}, \emph{gripper\_flip}, \emph{verb\_swap}, \emph{negation}, \emph{subject\_swap}, \emph{location\_swap}, \emph{adversarial\_plausible}); the quantity of interest is the floor-corrected $\bar{\mathcal{F}}_{\text{diff}} = \bar{\mathcal{F}} - \mathcal{F}(\text{floor})$, computed the one way, over these seven families only, everywhere in this paper. \emph{verb\_swap} is judged meaning-preserving on only $57.5\%$ of pairs, the least reliable of the seven (Appendix~\ref{sec:judge_edits}); we keep it in $\bar{\mathcal{F}}$ rather than drop the least favorable family. A \emph{ceiling} is always a maximum-effect reference an edit is compared up to: \emph{cross\_task\_swap} for $\mathcal{F}$, and for $\mathcal{F}_{\text{dir}}$ the largest score over the families with no direction to reverse (\S\ref{sec:directional}).
It is negative on 11 of 11 configurations (Table~\ref{tab:floors}), across both architecture families (2 base checkpoints, not 11 replications): 8 rows are \texttt{ECoT-bridge} or a LoRA adapter on it, 3 are \texttt{DeepThinkVLA-base} stages, with $\bar{\mathcal{F}}_{\text{diff}}$ from $-0.180$ to $-0.013$, and significant on 9 of 11 (7 of 8 ECoT-lineage, 2 of 3 DeepThinkVLA-lineage: two independent confirmations, not nine, once pseudo-replication is priced in) by a paired, observation-clustered bootstrap (20,000 resamples of the shared observation ids) that also holds against the stricter retraining bar of \S\ref{sec:variance}: a meaning-preserving synonym swap moves the action \emph{at least as reliably} as a semantic edit does. The two exceptions (\texttt{no-CoT} and \texttt{DT base}) are the two rows closest to their own floor, which is what a null control should look like. The one genuinely positive entry in our calibration set is off this table: the retrained no-CoT \emph{replicate} ($+0.014$), an independent second training of the action-only ablation, illustrates retraining variability rather than evidence of faithfulness, since a floor-corrected statistic \emph{should} return its own floor regardless of which replicate is drawn.

The relevant comparison is not whether semantic edits move the action by a large absolute amount, but whether they move it more than a meaning-preserving edit on the same model does; Figure~\ref{fig:calibration_gap} makes that comparison directly, across both checkpoint lineages, though as two confirmations rather than eleven, per the pseudo-replication accounting just above.

The effect is per-task, not an artifact of averaging, which Table~\ref{tab:floors}'s point estimates cannot show alone. Recomputing $\mathcal{F}_{\text{sem}}$ \emph{within} each of the $85$ LIBERO-90 \citep{libero} episode files every leaderboard row was scored on, with no new inference, the semantic mean is below that task's own paraphrase floor on more tasks than above it on all $8$ configurations that support the decomposition (Table~\ref{tab:per_task_headline}), $p$ from $9.0{\times}10^{-11}$ to $0.020$ on the seven full-CoT rows: all seven survive Holm correction across the eight tests at $\alpha{=}0.05$. The eighth, the no-CoT control, is null ($25$ below, $29$ above, $p = 0.683$), a coherence check, not a counterexample.

\paragraph{The obvious repair does not fully work, and the reason is itself informative.} \emph{paraphrase\_null} is the one family that changes sequence length (mean $+0.97$ words on 38/40 judged heads), so the floor may be partly a length effect. \emph{syntactic\_scramble} is the length-exact control: judged meaning-preserving at $1.000$, but its judged fluency is $4.00$ against $4.92$ and $5.00$ for the two true nulls (Appendix~\ref{sec:judge_edits}), sitting between them and the semantic families ($2.58$--$3.66$), and its same\_direction\_rate is the lowest of the three nulls ($0.925$ vs.\ $0.975$/$1.000$): fluency, not meaning, is where it departs. Across all thirteen families $\mathcal{F}$ correlates with judged fluency at Spearman $\rho = -0.60$. We cannot rule out that this floor tracks surface fluency rather than meaning, and flag it as unresolved in Limitations. Neither the policy's own log-likelihood of the edited CoT (partial $R^2 \in [0.001, 0.037]$ across all 6 Stage~1 checkpoints, beyond edit-distance and length) nor the judge's fluency rating itself (partial $R^2(\text{fluency}) = 0.056$ and the wrong sign, \texttt{r=32}, $n{=}236$) explains the gap between the two floors, and neither does length: \emph{paraphrase\_null\_lenexact}, meaning-preserving and length-exact like \emph{syntactic\_scramble} but lexical like \emph{paraphrase\_null}, floors at $\mathbf{0.560}$ on \texttt{r=32} ($n{=}300$), $0.007$ from \emph{paraphrase\_null} and $0.212$ from \emph{syntactic\_scramble} (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix} has all three checks in full). The remaining unresolved alternative is surface naturalness, not length: an automated judge alone cannot fully separate semantic preservation from human-perceived fluency. We therefore read this as a calibration failure under a tested operational null, not evidence that semantic content is causally irrelevant to the action.

Against the paraphrase floor, $\bar{\mathcal{F}}_{\text{diff}} < 0$ on all 11 calibrated configurations. Among the seven \texttt{Ours} configurations with a retraining replicate, 6 of 7 exceed \S\ref{sec:variance}'s $0.068$ deviation; for \texttt{ECoT-bridge}/DeepThinkVLA, which lack one, $0.068$ is a reference, not a measured bound (Limitations). Against the length-exact scramble floor, $\bar{\mathcal{F}}_{\text{diff}}$ is positive on 10 of 11, but only 3 of those 10 are individually significant (\texttt{r=32}, \texttt{DT SFT}, \texttt{DT RL}), and none of the seven exceeds it on this floor. We therefore report both floors everywhere rather than treat either as canonical: the magnitude metric cannot identify a stable semantic floor on its own, so swapping floors would trade a well-supported negative finding for a poorly-supported positive one, and we do not read the floors as interchangeable options. Threshold-free $\overline{\text{TV}}$ confirms the same collapse with no $\tau$ to sweep (\S\ref{sec:collision}); the $\tau$-sweep and the out-of-CoT \emph{instr\_random\_sub} control that bounds how much of any score is CoT-specific are in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix}. The pattern also replicates out of distribution: a Bridge~V2 follow-up that adds both calibration nulls to \texttt{ECoT-bridge}'s self-generated CoT on its own training corpus reproduces it (paraphrase floor above the semantic mean, the two floors disagreeing with each other more than either disagrees with the mean; full detail in Appendix~\ref{sec:cross_corpus}).

\paragraph{The floor-above-semantic pattern is not confined to teacher-forced reasoning.} Every result above edits a teacher-forced, ground-truth CoT annotation. On self-generated CoT, the same floor comparison replicates on \texttt{ECoT-bridge} and \texttt{DT-RL}, is directionally consistent on \texttt{DT-SFT} but not significant at this $n$, reverses on \texttt{r=32}, is mixed depending on which statistic is read on \texttt{r=64}, and is inconclusive on \texttt{no-CoT}; full per-checkpoint numbers are in Appendix~\ref{sec:limitations_full}. The calibration failure this section reports therefore extends beyond teacher-forced CoT, but is heterogeneous rather than universal.

\paragraph{A positive-control sanity check.} To verify that the benchmark can recover faithfulness when the causal dependence is explicit, we construct a rule-based oracle policy whose action is deterministically controlled by the edited directional content, applied to the released judge-validation pairs (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix}). On this known-faithful control, $\mathcal{F}_{\text{dir}}=0.925$, against $0.000$ on the matched non-faithful control (\emph{paraphrase\_null}). This establishes that the intervention-and-scoring pipeline can distinguish a known causal dependence from a matched null; it does not validate $\mathcal{F}_{\text{dir}}$ as a calibrated metric for learned VLAs.

\subsection{Mechanism: The Score Is a Decode-Collision Counter}
\label{sec:collision}

\begin{figure*}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=1.0\textwidth]{fig13_collision.pdf}
    \caption{$\mathcal{F}$ is close to a decode-collision counter. \emph{(a)} The one real camera frame all three rollout arms of Figure~\ref{fig:frames} share at $t{=}0$, with the two real MOVE phrases decoded from it: own CoT and its direction-flipped edit. \emph{(b)} The two decoded 7-DoF actions for that one frame: 5 of 7 dimensions tie exactly, so the whole $\Delta_\infty{=}1.741$ comes from translation-$y$ and -$z$ alone, an $n{=}1$ instance of the mechanism (c) shows at scale. \emph{(c)} The same fact stated as an identity: across the 324 (run, family) cells with $n \geq 20$, $\mathcal{F}$ at $\tau{=}0.05$ correlates with $1 - P(\Delta_\infty{=}0)$ at Pearson $R^2 = 0.926$; scored against the drawn $y{=}x$ line itself rather than the best-fit line, $R^2 = 0.893$. \emph{(d)} The resolution sweep: re-grouping the identical \texttt{ECoT-bridge} softmax into coarser action-token bins swings the collision rate $12.3\%\to2.9\%$ (8 to 256 bins) while the mean continuous $\Delta_\infty$ it is derived from barely moves ($0.656\to0.640$), so (c)'s correlation is not an artifact of this checkpoint's own 256-bin grid.}
    \label{fig:collision}
\end{figure*}

Were the floor failure an offset to correct, $\mathcal{F}$ would still be measuring displacement. It barely does. The per-sample $\Delta_\infty$ distribution is strongly bimodal: over the $28{,}443$ scored records of the 30 runs covered here (one seed per configuration) $46.2\%$ are \emph{exactly} zero (the argmax over action bins landed on the identical bin despite a different prompt) and $44.5\%$ exceed $0.10$. Of the records that move at all, only $9.8\%$ land below $\tau$, and if almost no mass lies near the threshold then $\mathcal{F}$ is close to $1 - P(\Delta_\infty = 0)$: not a measure of how far the action moved, but a count of how often the decode failed to collide, a symptom of the same per-bin argmax decode that action-tokenizer scaling work treats as a design axis in its own right \citep{fast2025}, not a defect specific to our instrument, so we would expect it on any de-quantized argmax action space, ours included (bin-width detail: Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix}).
That is measurable, so we measured it (Figure~\ref{fig:collision}(c); (a)--(b) show the same tie at the one frame Figure~\ref{fig:frames}'s three arms share). Across the 324 (run, family) cells with $n \geq 20$, $\mathcal{F}$ at $\tau{=}0.05$ tracks $1 - P(\Delta_\infty{=}0)$ at $r = 0.963$, $R^2 = 0.926$, with 80 cells identical to the last digit; the relation is one-sided, not a two-sided identity, since $\{\Delta_\infty{>}\tau\} \subseteq \{\Delta_\infty{\neq}0\}$ by construction (cell-by-cell count: Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix}). Threshold-insensitivity is a consequence of the metric being nearly a collision counter, not evidence that it is robust. This invalidates no comparison here (a collision rate is well-defined and self-consistent, and every $\mathcal{F}$ we report is computed the same way), but it changes what the quantity is called, and it is why \S\ref{sec:floors}'s floor collapse is not decisive on its own: a score whose dynamic range is dominated by argmax collisions cannot be told apart from one where two edit types simply collide at different rates for reasons that have nothing to do with meaning. \S\ref{sec:floors} rules out length and judged fluency as that reason; per-family collision-rate heterogeneity is part of it, and tracks which of the seven semantic families clear the paraphrase floor at all (Spearman $\rho=0.883$, Appendix~\ref{sec:family_heterogeneity}). We release $P(\Delta_\infty{=}0)$ beside $\mathcal{F}$ for all 324 cells in the release.

\paragraph{A threshold-free metric gives the same answer.} If $\mathcal{F}$'s dynamic range is dominated by decode collisions, the direct check is a statistic with no argmax and no threshold to collide: $D_{\text{TV}}^{s,f} = \tfrac{1}{7}\sum_{d=1}^{7}\mathrm{TV}\bigl(P_{\text{orig}}^d, P_{\text{edit}}^d\bigr)$, the total-variation distance between the policy's own softmax over action-token bins at each of the 7 action dimensions under the original vs.\ edited CoT, averaged over dimensions, continuous by construction, with nothing for a threshold to be one bin away from. Write $\overline{\text{TV}}$ for its mean over the same seven semantic families as $\bar{\mathcal{F}}$. On the six configurations we reran this way (Table~\ref{tab:tvfloors}, Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix}), $\overline{\text{TV}}$ is below the paraphrase floor on 6 of 6 configurations, all six significant by the same paired, observation-clustered bootstrap as \S\ref{sec:floors}, and above the scramble floor on 6 of 6, five of six significant: uncorrected, that is a larger fraction on both floors than $\mathcal{F}$'s eleven-configuration cohort ($9$/$11$, $3$/$11$, Table~\ref{tab:floors}); Holm-corrected (\S\ref{sec:variance}), it reverses on paraphrase, holds only on scramble. A metric with no collisions to count shows the same qualitative pattern on every configuration tested, and for the first time this cohort includes all three DeepThinkVLA checkpoints under a continuous instrument rather than only the thresholded one.

\paragraph{A resolution sweep links the instability directly to action-token granularity.} Re-deriving the collision rate and the mean $\Delta_\infty$ of the same softmax at coarser groupings of \texttt{ECoT-bridge}'s own 256 action-token bins (8/16/32/64/128/256, same forward pass, same four families, $n{=}171$ pooled; 256 bins reproduces this section's own numbers exactly, a regression check asserted in-script; full sweep: Figure~\ref{fig:collision}(d)): collision frequency changes substantially across resolutions ($12.3\%$ at 8 bins to $2.3\%$--$3.5\%$ from 32 bins on) while the underlying continuous displacement stays nearly constant ($0.640$--$0.656$). This directly demonstrates that the collision component of $\mathcal{F}$ is sensitive to action-grid granularity, even when the underlying continuous displacement is unchanged; it is one checkpoint's own softmax re-grouped, not a claim about models trained with different action tokenizers. Together with the collision analysis above, it indicates that a substantial part of $\mathcal{F}$'s dynamic range is set by the discrete action representation rather than by the semantic content of the intervention.

The LoRA variants it outscores by $1.2$--$1.5\times$ on magnitude genuinely reverse ($\cos$ from $-0.097$ to $-0.510$) and outscore it by $4.5$--$6.7\times$ on $\mathcal{F}_{\text{dir}}$.

What the two rules disagree about is one configuration, which shifts 6 rank positions while no other shifts more than 2 -- the row a reader would act on, which is why we publish none.
$\mathcal{F}_{\text{dir}}$ is also the one instrument here that clears a null; it is a proof of concept, not a validated replacement (confounds: this section and Limitations). $\mathcal{F}_{\text{dir}}$ tests directional consistency with the edited cue: necessary for faithfulness to signed content, not sufficient for the full reasoning trace. The $-0.5$ cosine threshold is a deliberately conservative directional criterion, not the loosest one that separates the two floors (threshold sweep: Appendix~\ref{sec:limitations_full}); $\mathcal{F}_{\text{dir}}$'s absolute value is therefore interpreted only relative to each model's own null and ceiling. We calibrate it against a \emph{ceiling}: the largest $\mathcal{F}_{\text{dir}}$ any of the eight families with no left$\leftrightarrow$right semantics reaches (named per row in Table~\ref{tab:fdirnull}); none gives a model any reason to \emph{reverse} a translation vector, so their maximum is a ceiling the treatment must clear.

Table~\ref{tab:fdirnull} is that calibration. On the six CoT-trained LoRA and data variants \emph{direction\_flip} clears the ceiling by $\mathbf{6.6}$--$\mathbf{11.1\times}$ and ECoT-bridge clears it at $2.5\times$, while the no-CoT control sits \emph{below} its own ceiling, as a calibrated instrument should. $\bar{\mathcal{F}}$ fails a comparable test (\S\ref{sec:floors}): on the no-CoT control it reads $0.166$ against its own $0.193$ floor, so the direction check distinguishes this action-only control from the CoT-trained models on this signed intervention, whereas magnitude scoring does not. The six clearing variants have a near-degenerate action space that cheapens, not manufactures, the clearance (permutation null still clears by $1.5$--$1.8\times$; Limitations). All three DeepThinkVLA checkpoints return $\mathcal{F}_{\text{dir}} \leq 0.010$ and do \emph{not} clear; on \texttt{DT SFT} and \texttt{DT RL} this is below their own permutation-null chance rate, but \texttt{DT base} is a harder case geometrically (Limitations). 7 of 11 configurations clear their own null. A candidate admission protocol for signed edits should therefore combine $\mathcal{F}_{\text{dir}}$ with its model-specific null and action-space diagnostic, not use $\mathcal{F}_{\text{mag}}$ alone, whose floor question \S\ref{sec:floors} shows to be unanswerable in either direction.

\paragraph{The diagnostic also recovers a constructed dependence in the oracle sanity check (\S\ref{sec:floors}).} A rule-based oracle reaches $\mathcal{F}_{\text{dir}}=0.925$ against $0.000$ on a matched non-faithful control, showing the pipeline detects this constructed causal dependence; this validates the pipeline's sensitivity, not $\mathcal{F}_{\text{dir}}$'s calibration on learned policies, which the confounds above still apply to in full.

We therefore recommend that any use of an edit-based faithfulness score report a model-specific null and, for signed families, an action-space diagnostic alongside $\mathcal{F}_{\text{dir}}$ (full admission rule: Appendix~\ref{sec:limitations_full}).

\subsection{Retraining Variance}
\label{sec:variance}

All leaderboard rows are 3-seed means with Wilson 95\% CIs (half-widths $\leq 0.067$), but sampling seeds does not measure the error bar that matters: seeds hold the checkpoint fixed, so we also retrained all seven configurations at a byte-identical recipe, scoring each replicate on the same 100 samples. In $\mathcal{F}$, per-family $\mathcal{F}$ moves across the six released pairs by up to $\mathbf{0.317}$, which is about $4.8\times$ that widest Wilson half-width (worst of $13$ families per pair; \emph{data-50A}'s is a control, so the bound is conservative), and $\bar{\mathcal{F}}$ by up to $0.106$, which is $1.6\times$ it. This bar (median $0.035$, max $0.068$) is what \S\ref{sec:floors}'s headline count is re-tested against (LoRA-rank non-monotonicity: Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix}, Figure~\ref{fig:ablation}); gaps below it should not be read as evidence of different faithfulness. \emph{Multiplicity.} Holm-correcting \S\ref{sec:floors}/\S\ref{sec:collision}'s $22$/$12$ tests lowers them to $7$/$11$, $1$/$11$, $2$/$6$, $5$/$6$ (Appendix~\ref{sec:multiplicity}); sign, not significance, is what \S\ref{sec:floors} turns on.

\enlargethispage{2\baselineskip}
\paragraph{What the benchmark establishes.} Single-step magnitude-based CoT edit scores require model-specific calibration and can be dominated by discrete action decoding, not semantic dependence. The direction-aware diagnostic remains confound-sensitive, and self-generated CoT extends the issue beyond teacher forcing, though not universally. Neither establishes rollout-level or cross-architecture generality.

\section{Conclusion}

CoT-Faith cannot rank manipulation VLAs by how much their reasoning drives their actions: magnitude-based edit scoring is not a calibrated measure of faithfulness under this protocol. Published gaps may not survive either floor. The direction-aware diagnostic (\S\ref{sec:directional}) is a starting point; more architectures and closed-loop rollouts are next.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{CoT faithfulness in language models.} Chain-of-thought prompting \citep{wei2022cot} is read as a window on the computation, on a definition of faithfulness that requires the stated reasoning to be the actual reason for the output \citep{jacovigoldberg2020}, and the literature that tests that reading finds it does not hold: stated reasoning often does not determine the output \citep{turpin2023,lanham2023,ye2022unreliability}, reasoning-trained models are no exception \citep{chen2025reasoning}, and unfaithfulness appears on natural rather than adversarially constructed prompts \citep{arcuschin2025}. The proposed repairs are architectural (decomposition \citep{radhakrishnan2023}, constrained decoding \citep{lyu2023faithful}) rather than metrological, and the field's own faithfulness tests are themselves contested \citep{atanasova2023,parcalabescu2024,lyu2024survey}. Our central empirical finding, that a meaning-preserving edit moves the output at least as reliably as a meaning-changing one, is that field's prompt-order and prompt-format sensitivity result \citep{lu2022order,sclar2024,mizrahi2024} transposed into a continuous action space rather than a new phenomenon. Our contribution is on the measurement side of that literature rather than the model side: we ask what an edit-magnitude score can license, not how to make a model more faithful.

\paragraph{The same question in VLAs.} Transferring the perturb-and-observe method to VLAs \citep{openvla,rt2,octo,ecot,cotvla,ecotlite} is attractive because the ``answer'' is a continuous action rather than a token, so the effect size is directly measurable. One line does this for autonomous driving with the same perturb-and-observe recipe \citep{vladrivebench}; it alone shares the specific flaw \S\ref{sec:floors} diagnoses, since it scores an edit by the \emph{magnitude} of the induced change, which is what makes our result a claim about a rule shared with it rather than about our implementation of it. \citet{doubleedged} probes manipulation safety monitors under perturbation on three models spanning a reasoning spectrum (no reasoning, text CoT, a latent iterative loop) across LIBERO and SimplerEnv, but its reasoning-stage entity-swap (the perturbation comparable to ours) is scored categorically (detection AUC, TPR@FPR, flag rate), not by magnitude; continuous displacement metrics in that paper apply only to its vision- and action-stage attacks, a different intervention class. \citet{pinocchio}, also driving, is a third measurement paradigm rather than a second instance of the same rule: a learned critic trained to classify edge-level consistency in a reasoning graph, used as a dense reward for RL post-training of the policy rather than a perturbation score on a frozen one, with a separate categorical (appropriate-response-or-not, not magnitude-of-change) counterfactual check for downstream robustness. Grouping either non-magnitude precedent in would overstate how much of the field shares the specific flaw \S\ref{sec:floors} diagnoses. The one magnitude-scoring line reports the same pattern in its own ablations, not framed as a critique of the scoring rule: \citet{vladrivebench}'s word-shuffled control on their highest-effect meaning-bearing prompt (\emph{origbrake}) produces, in their own words, ``an indistinguishable effect'' from the original ($-0.417$\,m vs.\ $-0.426$\,m mean displacement, their Table 13), and a control built from a repeated nonsense token produces ``nearly twice'' origbrake's effect ($-0.818$\,m): a meaning-destroying and a meaning-free edit both moving their continuous output at least as much as a meaningful one, in a driving VLA, under the same magnitude-only rule \S\ref{sec:floors} indicts here, reported as a property of their specific prompts rather than generalized into one. The manipulation benchmarks these models are graded on measure task success under distribution shift \citep{colosseum,vlabench}, in simulation standing in for hardware \citep{simplerenv}, or safety-relevant behaviour \citep{liberosafety}; none of them scores whether the emitted reasoning drove the action, which is the gap CoT-Faith is built for. \S\ref{sec:floors} and \S\ref{sec:collision} argue that this scoring rule needs a per-model null before any of its outputs can be read; \S\ref{sec:directional} says what to use instead.

\paragraph{Attention as a proxy, and why we do not use it.} Attention mass on the CoT segment is the obvious cheap proxy for this kind of interpretability claim, though no VLA work we know of has validated it as one. That attention weight is not by itself an explanation is argued widely in NLP \citep{jain2019attention,serrano2019}, as is the more general point that a plausible-looking attribution can survive controls it should fail \citep{adebayo2018,friedman2024}. We do not add a further measurement here, relying instead on the causal edit protocol \S\ref{sec:benchmark} defines directly. Causal intervention on the intermediate representation \citep{rome,zhang2024patching,geiger2022das} is the closer analogue of what we do, and our $\Delta_\infty$ is that intervention read out in action space rather than in a hidden representation.

\paragraph{Construct validity in benchmarks.} Our claim is of a kind that recurs: that a widely reported number does not measure the thing its name says. It has been made about fairness metrics \citep{jacobs2021}, about benchmark choice \citep{dehghani2021,raji2021}, and about experimental reporting in RL \citep{henderson2018,dodge2019,pineau2021}. The closest structural analogy is emergence: \citet{schaeffer2023} show that several claimed sudden capability jumps are an artifact of which metric, discontinuous or continuous, is used to read the same underlying model outputs, not a discontinuity in the outputs themselves. The diagnosis here is the same move with the floor in place of the metric: which edit is nominated as meaning-preserving, not anything about the model, decides whether a semantic edit "passes." Perturbation-based interpretability metrics carry the same problem and the same fix: comprehensiveness and sufficiency \citep{eraser} and retrain-and-evaluate calibration \citep{roar} pair a perturbation score with an explicit null, the design our two floors extend to a continuous action space, and that different faithfulness metrics disagree with each other by more than chance is itself a documented, general phenomenon \citep{krishna2022,chan2022faithfulness} rather than a defect specific to our instrument. The remedy in each case is the same one we adopt here: publish the null, the variance and the artifact rather than the ranking \citep{lipton2016}. This bears directly on the one-prior-instance count above: a magnitude score with no validated null is not a quirk of \citet{vladrivebench} or of manipulation VLAs, it is the specific failure mode ERASER and ROAR were introduced to correct in the much older, much more populated text- and vision-interpretability literatures they come from, now recurring in a model class young enough that only one paper has reached for this specific scoring convention. The modeling substrate that convention would be applied to is not similarly rare: our own citations span three distinct CoT-VLA lines \citep{ecot,cotvla,deepthinkvla}, released between July 2024 and November 2025, and one of them, DeepThinkVLA, is itself among the 15 models evaluated here, where the floor-collapse \S\ref{sec:floors} diagnoses is significant on $2$ of its $3$ configurations (\S\ref{sec:pseudoreplication}): not a hypothetical future risk but one already live inside this benchmark's own evaluation set. \citet{cotvla} is cited but not evaluated here: as of this submission its project page (\url{https://cot-vla.github.io/}) lists no code or checkpoint release, and neither its authors' GitHub organization nor Hugging Face host one, so it is not a lineage this release could add without training one from scratch. (\citet{ecotlite}, also cited above, is not a fourth line either: it shares \citet{ecot}'s ``Embodied-CoT'' template and two authors, and supplies the LIBERO-90 CoT annotations \S\ref{app:sec:benchmark} uses, so it is the same lineage under a different citation, not independent evidence of substrate breadth.) A field where the modeling substrate is this active but only one paper has reached for an unvalidated scoring convention is exactly where establishing the check is cheapest: fixing it now is a one-time cost against a still-small denominator, not a retrofit against a hardened convention later.

The mechanism is not specific to faithfulness scoring, either. \S\ref{sec:collision}'s argument is that any magnitude score computed by comparing two de-quantized, argmax-decoded continuous actions inherits the same collision floor, for the same reason \citep{fast2025}, regardless of what perturbation produced the two decodes being compared. A perturbation-based VLA robustness or out-of-distribution-sensitivity score built the same way (perturb the input, measure how far the action moved) would need the same per-model null this paper argues a faithfulness score needs, and for the same reason. We do not test this: no such score is in our release, and the claim is about the shared decode mechanism, not about any specific robustness benchmark's numbers.
