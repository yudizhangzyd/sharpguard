\title{CoT-Faith: A Benchmark for Chain-of-Thought Faithfulness\\
in Manipulation Vision-Language-Action Models}

\author{Anonymous ARR submission}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Vision-language-action models that emit chain-of-thought are proposed as a route to interpretable robotics, on the premise that the emitted reasoning drives the action. We present \textbf{CoT-Faith}, a benchmark that tests that premise uniformly across 15 manipulation VLAs in four architecture families, combining an attention decomposition with a paired-intervention edit protocol over ten edit families and three calibration nulls. Our headline results are negative and are about the protocol rather than the models. First, \textbf{magnitude-based edit scoring has no null it clears}: on 12 of 12 configurations a meaning-preserving paraphrase moves the action \emph{more} than semantic edits do. The obvious repair --- swapping to a length-exact meaning-preserving floor --- flips the sign on all 12, and the two floors disagree by more than either disagrees with the semantic families, so neither licenses a claim about meaning. Second, the metric is close to a decode-collision counter: it tracks $1 - P(\Delta{=}0)$ at $R^2{=}0.93$ over 324 cells. Third, requiring the action change to point the \emph{implied} direction inverts the ranking. Constructively, that direction-aware score is the one instrument here with a measured null: it clears its own floor on 7 of 11 configurations at $6.2$--$11.1\times$ and correctly fails on a no-CoT control. We release all records and an audit script that checks 999 claims and fails on any mismatch.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Vision-language-action (VLA) models that emit chain-of-thought reasoning alongside their action tokens are widely proposed as a route toward interpretable robotics: the reasoning is expected to expose the model's plan for review, correction, or safety monitoring \citep{ecot,cotvla,ecotlite,deepthinkvla}. This premise --- that the emitted CoT \emph{drives} the subsequent action decode --- underwrites CoT-based safety monitors, CoT-edit corrective interventions, and CoT-derived auditing. It has not been evaluated for manipulation VLAs under uniform conditions. Recent work evaluates CoT-faithfulness for driving VLAs \citep{vladrivebench,pinocchio} and probes CoT-based safety monitors in a small manipulation cohort \citep{doubleedged}; a cross-model, cross-corpus, standardized manipulation benchmark is missing.

We built one, applied it to 15 VLAs across 4 architecture families, and the most useful thing it produced is a set of results about the measurement instrument rather than about the models. We report those first, because they bound how every number in this paper --- including ours --- may be read.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{A benchmark and protocol.} Ten edit families in a three-tier taxonomy plus three calibration nulls (\S\ref{sec:benchmark}), a magnitude Faithfulness Score $\mathcal{F}$ and a direction-aware $\mathcal{F}_{\text{dir}}$, applied to $\sim$100 LIBERO first-step samples per family per model, with an $N{=}100$ cross-corpus sweep on Bridge~V2, RT-1/Fractal and BC-Z.
\item \textbf{A construct-validity failure, characterized rather than caveated} (\S\ref{sec:floors}). $\mathcal{F}$ sits below its own meaning-preserving floor on 12/12 configurations; the alternative floor flips the sign on 12/12; and the two floors disagree by more than either disagrees with the semantic mean, also on 12/12. We report both floors everywhere and treat $\mathcal{F}$ as uninformative about meaning in either direction.
\item \textbf{What $\mathcal{F}$ actually counts} (\S\ref{sec:collision}). It tracks $1 - P(\Delta_\infty{=}0)$ at $R^2 = 0.93$ over 324 cells. Its much-advertised insensitivity to the threshold follows from that, and is not evidence of robustness.
\item \textbf{One instrument that does clear a null} (\S\ref{sec:directional}). $\mathcal{F}_{\text{dir}}$ clears a ceiling built from eight non-directional families on 7 of 11 configurations, at $6.2$--$11.1\times$ on the CoT-trained variants, and fails on the no-CoT control as it should. This is what we recommend any admission rule be built on.
\item \textbf{A release built to be checked} (\S\ref{sec:release}). All per-sample records, an audit script asserting 999 claims against them --- field presence, not merely record counts --- and a leaderboard admission rule that rejects seven of our own eight submissions.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

CoT-faithfulness in language models is measured by perturbing the reasoning and observing the answer \citep{lanham2023,turpin2023}, and the field's central finding is that stated reasoning frequently does not determine the output. Transferring that method to VLAs is attractive because the ``answer'' is a continuous action rather than a token, so the effect size is directly measurable. Two lines do this for driving \citep{vladrivebench,pinocchio}, and \citet{doubleedged} probes manipulation safety monitors under perturbation. All of them score an edit by the \emph{magnitude} of the induced change. \S\ref{sec:floors} and \S\ref{sec:collision} are, among other things, an argument that this scoring rule needs a per-model null before any of its outputs can be read, and \S\ref{sec:directional} is an argument for what to use instead. Attention-based interpretability for VLAs \citep{ecot,cotvla} reports attention mass on the CoT segment; \S\ref{sec:dissociation} shows attention mass and edit response are dissociated, so neither substitutes for the other. Full discussion in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix}.

\section{The CoT-Faith benchmark}
\label{sec:benchmark}

\paragraph{Protocol.} For each sample $s$ and edit family $f$ we decode greedily twice --- once on the model's own CoT, once on the edited CoT --- holding the observation, the instruction and the decode fixed, and compare the two 7-DoF actions:
\begin{equation}
\Delta_\infty^{s,f} = \max_{i \in [7]}\bigl|\,a_i(s, f(\text{cot}_s)) - a_i(s, \text{cot}_s)\,\bigr|,
\label{eq:delta}
\end{equation}
with $a_i$ normalized to $[-1,1]$. A sample is faithful under $f$ iff $\Delta_\infty^{s,f} > \tau$, $\tau = 0.05$, and
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{F}(m,f) = \tfrac{1}{N}\textstyle\sum_s \mathbf{1}[\Delta_\infty^{s,f}(m) > \tau].
\label{eq:faith}
\end{equation}
This is a paired intervention: it references no ground-truth demonstration, so it is defined even on a policy that performs the task poorly --- a property that matters in \S\ref{sec:release}.

\paragraph{Edit families.} Ten families in three tiers (structural, word-level, object/causal) and three calibration nulls. The nulls are what make a score interpretable and are the part of the design we would keep if we kept nothing else: \emph{selfsplice\_control} (identity substitution, which must score $0$ and does, on 11/11 CoT-VLAs); \emph{paraphrase\_null} (meaning-preserving synonym swap); \emph{bbox\_jitter\_null} (meaning-preserving numeric perturbation); and an out-of-CoT control, \emph{instr\_random\_sub}, which corrupts five \emph{instruction} tokens and never touches the reasoning. A blind LLM judge, itself gated on an identity control ($1.000$) and a pooled negative control ($0.175$), validates the generators over 437 pairs: \emph{paraphrase\_null} preserves meaning at $0.975$ and \emph{bbox\_jitter\_null} at $1.000$, so the floors are validated rather than asserted. It also found that one family's name oversells it --- \emph{adversarial\_plausible} is judged plausible on only $0.125$ of pairs --- which we corrected in the taxonomy (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix}).

\paragraph{Direction-aware score.} Eq.~\ref{eq:delta} credits movement in \emph{any} direction. For families whose semantics imply a signed consequence we require the sign. After \emph{direction\_flip} reverses left$\leftrightarrow$right / up$\leftrightarrow$down / in$\leftrightarrow$out,
\begin{align}
\mathcal{F}_{\text{dir}} &= \tfrac{1}{N}\textstyle\sum_s \mathbf{1}\!\left[c^f_s < -0.5\right], \label{eq:fdir}\\
c^f_s &= \cos\bigl(a_{1:3}(s,\text{cot}_s),\, a_{1:3}(s,f(\text{cot}_s))\bigr) \nonumber
\end{align}
with analogous criteria for \emph{gripper\_flip} and \emph{negation}. It needs no new inference --- it is computed from action pairs we already release.

\paragraph{Models.} 15 VLAs: 4 OpenVLA non-CoT baselines, 3 DeepThinkVLA variants (base/SFT/RL, a PaliGemma-based second architecture family with a different reasoning format and action tokenizer), 1 public ECoT-bridge, and 7 LoRA fine-tunes of our own isolating rank, data quantity, and reasoning-target training. Every trained configuration was trained \emph{twice} at a byte-identical recipe, which supplies the error bar that matters (\S\ref{sec:variance}).

\section{The magnitude score has no null it clears}
\label{sec:floors}

A faithful model should be \emph{insensitive} to an edit that preserves meaning. So $\mathcal{F}$ on \emph{paraphrase\_null} is a floor, and the quantity of interest is the floor-corrected $\bar{\mathcal{F}}_{\text{diff}} = \bar{\mathcal{F}}(\text{semantic}) - \mathcal{F}(\text{floor})$.

\textbf{It is negative on 12 of 12 configurations} (Table~\ref{tab:floors}), across both architecture families, with $\bar{\mathcal{F}}_{\text{diff}}$ from $-0.179$ to $+0.014$. A meaning-preserving synonym swap moves the action \emph{more reliably} than a semantic edit does, on every full-CoT model we measured. On our strongest model a paraphrase changes the action $94.7\%$ of the time against $96.3\%$ for replacing the entire trace with an unrelated task's reasoning --- our designated maximum-effect control. The single positive entry is the independent retraining of the no-CoT variant ($+0.127$), the model trained on an action-only target, which is where a working floor-corrected statistic \emph{should} return its own floor. We report it as the protocol's clean positive control, not as a model that passes.

\paragraph{The obvious repair does not work, and why it fails is the real result.} \emph{paraphrase\_null} is the one family in our taxonomy that changes sequence length: its synonym table contains multi-word expansions (\emph{release} $\to$ ``let go of''), and it changes word count on 38/40 judged heads by a mean of $+0.97$ words, while \emph{direction\_flip}, \emph{negation}, \emph{syntactic\_scramble} and \emph{bbox\_jitter\_null} change it on 0/40. So the floor may be subtracting a length effect. \textbf{We did not test this before submission.} The control that settles it is \emph{syntactic\_scramble}: the same judge scores it meaning-preserving at $1.000$, and it is length-exact by construction.

Against that floor, $\bar{\mathcal{F}}_{\text{diff}}$ is \emph{positive} on 12/12. \textbf{The sign flips on every single configuration.} That alone would license switching floors and reporting the favourable sign. What forbids it is the third statistic: \textbf{on 12/12 configurations the gap between the two floors exceeds the gap between the semantic mean and \emph{either} floor.} At $r{=}32$ the floors are $0.567$ and $0.348$, a spread of $0.219$, while the semantic mean $0.438$ lies \emph{between} them, $0.129$ and $0.090$ away. Two edits our validated judge certifies as meaning the same thing disagree with each other more than either disagrees with edits that change meaning.

\textbf{We therefore report both floors everywhere and treat the magnitude metric as uninformative about meaning in either direction} --- a weaker claim than our submitted one, and the one the data supports. Swapping to the length-exact floor would trade one unlicensed sign for its opposite.

\section{What the magnitude score counts}
\label{sec:collision}

The per-sample $\Delta_\infty$ distribution is strongly bimodal: over all $28{,}443$ scored records, $46.2\%$ are \emph{exactly} zero (the argmax over action bins landed on the identical bin despite a different prompt) and $44.5\%$ exceed $0.10$. We previously drew the flattering conclusion from this --- that $\mathcal{F}$ is stable under $\tau$, which it is. The unflattering one is stronger. Of the records that move at all, only $9.8\%$ land below $\tau$; if almost no mass lies near the threshold, then $\mathcal{F}$ is close to $1 - P(\Delta_\infty = 0)$: not a measure of how far the action moved, but a count of how often the decode failed to collide.

That is measurable, so we measured it. Across the 324 (run, family) cells with $n \geq 20$, $\mathcal{F}$ at $\tau{=}0.05$ tracks $1 - P(\Delta_\infty{=}0)$ at $r = 0.963$, $R^2 = 0.926$, with 80 cells identical to the last digit; per configuration the agreement is $R^2 = 0.972$. \textbf{Threshold-insensitivity is a consequence of the metric being nearly a collision counter, not evidence that it is robust.} This invalidates no comparison in the paper --- a collision rate is well-defined and self-consistent, and every $\mathcal{F}$ we report is computed the same way --- but it changes what the quantity is called, and it is the mechanism behind \S\ref{sec:floors}: a score whose dynamic range is dominated by argmax collisions has no particular reason to separate meaning-changing from meaning-preserving edits, and it does not. We report $P(\Delta_\infty{=}0)$ as a first-class column (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix}).

\section{Direction-aware scoring: the ranking inverts, and this instrument has a null}
\label{sec:directional}

\textbf{The ranking reverses.} ECoT-bridge --- numerically highest in every magnitude column --- has $\mathcal{F}_{\text{mag}} = 0.963 \pm 0.006$ on \emph{direction\_flip} but $\mathcal{F}_{\text{dir}} = 0.120 \pm 0.043$, with mean translation cosine $+0.415$: \textbf{it moves in substantially the \emph{same} direction after the direction is reversed}. The LoRA variants it outscores by $1.2$--$1.5\times$ on magnitude genuinely reverse ($\cos$ from $-0.111$ to $-0.509$) and outscore it by $4.5$--$6.5\times$ on $\mathcal{F}_{\text{dir}}$. The inversion is far larger than the seed noise producing it: ECoT-bridge's deficit against $r{=}64$ is $0.659$ against per-row seed stds of $0.043$ and $0.021$. This is why we publish no ranking --- two defensible scoring rules disagree about who is best, so publishing either would be a choice of conclusion rather than a measurement.

\textbf{$\mathcal{F}_{\text{dir}}$ is also the one instrument here that clears a null}, and this is the constructive half of the paper. Our own critique of the field is that edit sensitivity gets reported without a null; $\mathcal{F}_{\text{dir}}$ was introduced to show the ranking inverts and was never itself calibrated. So we calibrated it against the \emph{ceiling} over eight non-directional families --- both meaning-preserving floors, both structural nulls, and the semantic families whose edits are not directional. None of them has any reason to \emph{reverse} a translation vector, so their maximum is the floor.

On the six CoT-trained LoRA and data variants, \emph{direction\_flip} clears that ceiling by $\mathbf{6.2}$--$\mathbf{11.1\times}$ ($r{=}64$: $0.779$ vs.\ $0.070$; $r{=}32$: $0.589$ vs.\ $0.077$), \textbf{while the no-CoT control sits \emph{below} its own floor} ($0.087$ vs.\ $0.113$) --- the one configuration where the treatment does not clear, and exactly the behaviour a calibrated instrument should show. $\mathcal{F}_{\text{mag}}$ fails that same test: on the no-CoT control it reads $0.187$ against a $0.193$ floor, so magnitude scoring cannot distinguish the ablation from the models it ablates, and the direction check can. ECoT-bridge clears at $3.8\times$ ($0.150$ vs.\ $0.040$), which is the $0.96 \to 0.12$ collapse seen from the null side. All three DeepThinkVLA checkpoints return $\mathcal{F}_{\text{dir}} \leq 0.010$ and do \emph{not} clear, so the second architecture family fails the direction check outright; we report that as a negative row, not as coverage. \textbf{7 of 11 configurations clear their own null.} An admission rule should be built on this, not on $\mathcal{F}_{\text{mag}}$, whose floor question \S\ref{sec:floors} shows to be unanswerable in either direction.

\section{Attention and causal effect are dissociated}
\label{sec:dissociation}

All 8 ECoT-family CoT-VLAs cluster tightly at $(\text{visual}, \text{instr}, \text{CoT}, \text{prev}) \approx (0.29, 0.30, 0.34, 0.07)$ regardless of training regime, while OpenVLA non-CoT baselines sit at $(0.52, 0.42, 0.00, 0.05)$: \textbf{attention on the CoT segment is a floor set by architecture, and reasoning-target training moves it by little}. Causal effect is not so distributed --- $\bar{\mathcal{F}}_{\text{mag}}$ spans $5.2\times$ across the same models. Attention mass therefore does not predict causal effect, and a monitor reading the first is not reading the second.

Two limits, both measured. The attention claim is an \emph{early-layer} claim: a 5-layer-set $\times$ 3-seed sweep shows $\alpha(\text{cot})$ leads in exactly 1 of the 4 four-layer blocks probed --- the one we report --- and swings $18.7$\,pp across depth against a worst-case sampling $\sigma$ of $0.26$\,pp. No bucket ordering should be reported without its layer set. And on the second architecture family $\alpha(\text{cot})$ is \emph{never} the largest bucket on any checkpoint, so ``the CoT bucket is largest'' is specific to both the layer set and the family.

We also tested whether attention predicts \emph{failure}. It does not: re-run in-domain and in robot units after we withdrew a cross-domain version for a protocol error, $\alpha(\text{cot})$ gives AUROC $0.596$ with a CI spanning chance ($N{=}153$, one model). Only $\alpha(\text{instruction})$ excludes chance ($0.362$), in the direction that inattention to the command predicts error --- read off the same data rather than predicted in advance. We do not generalize it.

\section{The error bar that matters is the retraining, not the seed}
\label{sec:variance}

All leaderboard rows are 3-sampling-seed means with Wilson 95\% CIs (half-widths $\leq 0.067$). \textbf{This does not fix the problem it appears to fix.} Sampling seeds hold the checkpoint fixed. So we trained all seven configurations a second time at a byte-identical recipe and scored each replicate on the same 100 samples with byte-identical interventions. Each pair is genuinely two different models --- on $r{=}32$ the \emph{unedited} action differs on 66 of 100 samples. Across the pairs, per-family $\mathcal{F}$ moves by up to $\mathbf{0.260}$ and $\bar{\mathcal{F}}$ by up to $0.092$: about $3.9\times$ the widest Wilson interval, and larger than most between-model differences a reader would want to read off the table. Single-family differences below ${\sim}0.26$ are not model differences. The controls behave throughout (\emph{selfsplice\_control} is exactly $0.000$ in all 14 runs), so this is variance in the measured quantity, not in the harness.

\section{Release and audit}
\label{sec:release}

We release $52{,}338$ per-sample edit records ($45{,}989$ scored), $3{,}620$ attention records, the decoder audits, and every script, at $51.6$\,MB of JSON requiring no network access to re-derive any number here. \texttt{scripts/verify\_paper\_numbers.py} asserts \textbf{999 claims} against the artifacts, treats a missing artifact as a failure rather than a skip, and exits non-zero on any mismatch.

Two disclosures the audit forced, both of which we would rather not make. \textbf{First, it now checks what records \emph{contain}, not only how many there are} --- because the count-only version passed $869/869$ while the release paragraph made a false claim about its own schema. The new check immediately found a second instance: 438 scored records (the three cross-corpus runs) carry deltas without the action pair or edit metadata, so those runs are reproducible at aggregate level only. \textbf{Second, six of the eight leaderboard rows come from a policy whose single-step open-loop prediction does not beat a constant} --- its L$_1$ is $0.0486$ against $0.0457$ for predicting the per-dimension dataset mean, in every frame the checkpoint ships. This does not invalidate $\Delta_\infty$, which is a self-comparison referencing no ground truth. It does mean those rows are edit-sensitivity measurements on weak policies, and it is the direct cause of the missing rollout evidence: at 40 episodes per arm, both the clean-CoT and no-CoT arms score $0/40$, so an edit-induced $\Delta$SR is undefined rather than zero.

Our leaderboard's admission rule requires a per-model paraphrase floor and a direction check. \textbf{It rejects seven of our own eight submissions}, and the eighth has a floor at the same height as its ceiling. That rule, rather than any score it admits, is the part of this work we expect to outlast the rest.

\section{Conclusion}

CoT-Faith set out to rank manipulation VLAs by how much their reasoning drives their actions. It cannot, and the reason is not a shortage of models or samples: the magnitude-based edit score that this benchmark and its precedents are built on has no null it clears, its sign is determined by which meaning-preserving family is nominated as the floor, and it is largely a decode-collision counter. Direction-aware scoring does clear a null, on 7 of 11 configurations, and inverts the magnitude ranking. Published faithfulness gaps obtained by magnitude scoring may survive neither check, and we think measuring the floor should precede reporting the score.

\section*{Limitations}

The full list is Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix}; the load-bearing items are these. \emph{Construct validity fails on 11 of 12 calibration entries} and the exception is a negative control, so no absolute $\mathcal{F}$ in this paper should be read as a quantity of faithfulness. \emph{No number here is rollout-conditioned.} The leaderboard is first-step; the paired rollout returns $0/40$ in both arms, and we state plainly that this is a checkpoint-competence problem rather than a compute or suite-availability problem --- four earlier versions of this item framed it as the latter. \emph{P3 is one model with a negative result} and supports no cross-model comparison. \emph{Attention claims are early-layer claims} and do not survive the depth sweep. \emph{Per-family $N$ varies from 60 to 100} with a visibility gate, so we compare each model only against its own floor and ceiling. \emph{$\mathcal{F}_{\text{dir}}$ does not transfer to the second architecture family}: all three DeepThinkVLA checkpoints fail it. \emph{One scoring convention is a choice we make and disclose}: DeepThinkVLA emits a $(10,7)$ action chunk and we score step 0, the only convention under which the two families measure the same event; under the chunk convention the paraphrase floor saturates at $1.000$ on all three and one of our two cross-family results does not hold. \emph{OpenVLA-OFT is excluded} after failing to load at its own authors' declared pins.

\section*{Ethics Statement}

This work evaluates whether the reasoning text emitted by manipulation policies actually drives their actions. Its motivation is a safety concern: CoT-based monitors and CoT-edit interventions are being proposed for deployed robots on the assumption that the reasoning is load-bearing, and our results indicate that the standard way of testing that assumption does not measure what it claims to. We consider the negative result the safety-relevant contribution, and we have tried to state it in a form that does not overclaim in the other direction either --- \S\ref{sec:floors} withdraws a headline we submitted.

All experiments are in simulation (LIBERO) or on public offline corpora; no physical robot was operated and no human subjects or personal data are involved. We release measurement records, not new capability. Upstream licenses are resolved from the Hugging Face Hub API rather than written from memory and released machine-readably; 3 of the 15 assets we depend on declare no license upstream, and we disclose that and grant no rights to them rather than assign a license we cannot verify. The release states the uses it does not support, including that no number in it is rollout-conditioned and that the leaderboard is a diagnostic rather than a ranking.
