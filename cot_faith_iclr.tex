\begin{abstract}
We present \textbf{CoT-Faith}, a standardized benchmark for measuring chain-of-thought (CoT) faithfulness in manipulation vision-language-action (VLA) models. CoT-Faith combines three complementary evaluation probes -- (P1) a four-bucket decomposition of action-token attention, (P2) a paired-intervention causal-edit protocol over ten edit families organized in a three-tier taxonomy, and (P3) an attention-based failure predictor -- and applies them uniformly to \textbf{15 manipulation VLAs} spanning four architecture families (OpenVLA non-CoT, ECoT, PaliGemma-based DeepThinkVLA, and our LoRA variants) evaluated on LIBERO~90 with a preliminary cross-corpus pilot on Bridge~V2, RT-1/Fractal, and BC-Z. The benchmark contributes: (1) a formal Faithfulness Score with a validated null selfsplice control ($\mathcal{F}\!=\!0.00$ observed across 8/8 CoT-VLA models); (2) a taxonomy of ten CoT edit families spanning surface, semantic, and causal levels; (3) reference results across four non-CoT baselines, three DeepThinkVLA variants (base, SFT, RL), one ECoT-bridge public model, and seven of our LoRA-fine-tuned variants that isolate rank, training-data quantity, and reasoning-target training as independent axes; and (4) a public leaderboard with per-episode logs and Wilson~95\% confidence intervals released. Under uniform evaluation conditions, CoT-Faith surfaces \textbf{one main claim} unifying three sub-findings, plus one preliminary observation and one $N{=}1$ pilot: \textit{fine-tuning with CoT targets does not shift where the model looks (attention distribution) but does make what it looks at causally load-bearing (edit response)}. Concretely: (F1) within an ECoT architecture, our reasoning-target ablation (same base, same data, action-only target) exhibits nearly identical CoT attention (0.349 vs 0.340) as the CoT-trained variant; (F2) the same no-CoT variant's Faithfulness Score collapses $2\text{--}4\times$ across every semantic edit family; (F3) attention within the 8-model trained cluster spans only 2.3\,pp while mean faithful rate spans $5.5\times$. We also report (O4, \emph{observation, not a controlled finding}) a $\sim 2\times$ faithfulness gap between our LIBERO r=32 fine-tune and the public ECoT-bridge model on three shared edit families; this gap conflates two axes -- training-data quantity ($15\times$) and target-corpus distribution -- and we deliberately refrain from attributing it to any single cause, treating it as motivation for future controlled work rather than as a claimed finding. Finally (F5, \emph{preliminary N=1 pilot}) the ECoT-bridge attention profile appears qualitatively preserved when the same model is applied to Bridge~V2, Fractal, and BC-Z; the full N=30 sweep is deferred to a public leaderboard update. We hope CoT-Faith will serve as standard evaluation infrastructure for the emerging class of interpretable-VLA claims.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
% ============================================================

Vision-language-action (VLA) models that emit chain-of-thought reasoning alongside their action tokens are widely proposed as a route toward interpretable robotics: the reasoning is expected to expose the model's plan for review, correction, or safety monitoring \citep{ecot,cotvla,ecotlite,deepthinkvla}. This premise -- that the emitted CoT \emph{drives} the subsequent action decode -- underwrites downstream systems such as CoT-based safety monitors, CoT-edit-based corrective interventions, and CoT-derived auditing. Yet the premise has not been empirically evaluated for manipulation VLAs under uniform conditions. Recent work has evaluated CoT-faithfulness for driving VLAs \citep{vladrivebench,pinocchio} and probed CoT-based safety monitors under perturbation in a small manipulation cohort \citep{doubleedged}; a general, cross-model, cross-corpus, standardized benchmark for manipulation-domain CoT-faithfulness is missing.

We taxonomize the design space of CoT-faithfulness evaluation into three probe families (attention, causal-intervention, downstream-utility) and populate each with formal metrics, a null control, and reference implementations. Applied to 15 manipulation VLAs across 4 architecture families (OpenVLA non-CoT $\times 4$, DeepThinkVLA $\times 3$, our ECoT LoRA-fine-tune $\times 7$, ECoT-bridge public $\times 1$), CoT-Faith enables systematic study of \emph{which training regimes yield actually load-bearing reasoning}, distinct from \emph{which regimes surface-emit reasoning text}.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item {\bf CoT-Faith benchmark.} A standardized suite for measuring chain-of-thought faithfulness in manipulation VLAs, comprising ten edit families organized into a three-tier taxonomy (Section~\ref{sec:taxonomy}), applied to $\sim 100$ LIBERO first-step samples per family per model.
\item {\bf Evaluation protocol.} A paired-intervention protocol with a formal Faithfulness Score $\mathcal{F}$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:faith}), a null selfsplice control that must show $\mathcal{F}\!=\!0$, an attention-decomposition probe with formal segment boundaries (Eq.~\ref{eq:attn}), and an attention$\to$action-error AUROC probe.
\item {\bf Cross-family coverage.} 15 VLAs across 4 architecture families evaluated end-to-end under identical conditions: 4 OpenVLA non-CoT baselines (one per LIBERO suite), 3 DeepThinkVLA variants (base, SFT, RL), 1 public ECoT-bridge, and 7 of our LoRA variants. A preliminary $N{=}1$ pilot on 3 additional manipulation corpora (Bridge~V2, RT-1/Fractal, BC-Z) demonstrates protocol portability; the full $N{=}30$ cross-corpus sweep is in progress and will populate a public leaderboard update.
\item {\bf Public artifact.} We release all code, training checkpoints, per-sample edit target texts, per-episode attention logs, and the CoT-Faith leaderboard at [URL redacted]. All $\sim$27{,}000 evaluation calls reproducible from a single command.
\item {\bf Findings enabled by CoT-Faith.} Section~\ref{sec:analysis} details three controlled findings (F1--F3), one uncontrolled observation (O4), and one preliminary $N{=}1$ pilot (F5): (F1) attention on CoT is architecture-determined; (F2) causal effect requires reasoning-target training; (F3) attention and causation are dissociated; (O4) a Bridge-corpus-trained model is $\sim 2\times$ more faithful than our LIBERO r=32 fine-tune on three shared edits, but this comparison conflates $15\times$ data quantity with target-corpus distribution and is reported as a training-regime \emph{observation}, not an isolated causal finding; (F5, preliminary) attention profiles appear cross-corpus stable in an $N{=}1$ pilot on 3 external corpora.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}
% ============================================================

\paragraph{Manipulation VLAs with chain-of-thought.}
ECoT \citep{ecot} introduced the nine-tag reasoning trace (\texttt{TASK}, \texttt{PLAN}, \texttt{VISIBLE OBJECTS}, \texttt{SUBTASK REASONING}, \texttt{SUBTASK}, \texttt{MOVE REASONING}, \texttt{MOVE}, \texttt{GRIPPER POSITION}, \texttt{ACTION}) as the target during LoRA fine-tuning of OpenVLA-7B. ECoT-Lite / Efficient Embodied Reasoning \citep{ecotlite} released dense per-timestep reasoning annotations over 90 LIBERO tasks and studied training strategies for efficient embodied reasoning. DeepThinkVLA \citep{deepthinkvla} extended the framework with a hybrid-attention decoder (causal attention for language, bidirectional attention for parallel action decoding) on PaliGemma, plus a two-stage SFT-then-RL alignment pipeline. OpenVLA-OFT \citep{openvlaoft} proposed an Optimized Fine-Tuning recipe (parallel decoding, action chunking, continuous action representation) for adapting VLAs to new robot setups. All four assume that the emitted CoT informs the action.

\paragraph{Faithfulness in language models.}
For LLMs, \citet{turpin2023} showed that CoT can be post-hoc rationalisation; \citet{lanham2023} showed rank-based CoT importance is inconsistent across tasks. These motivate our manipulation-domain investigation.

\paragraph{Existing embodied CoT-faith work.}
VLADriveBench \citep{vladrivebench} defined the mentioning/hallucination/contradiction/action-alignment quartet plus a CoT-intervention protocol for autonomous-driving CoT-VLAs. Pinocchio \citep{pinocchio} operationalized a behavioral surrogate for embodied faithfulness through a learned critic scoring observation grounding and stepwise coherence on driving trajectories. \citet{doubleedged} evaluated three VLA reasoning designs (no-reasoning, text-CoT, latent-iterative) on LIBERO and SimplerEnv under vision/reasoning/action-stage perturbations and monitor-based defenses; their study focused on robustness under perturbation and did not include a null selfsplice control or a multi-family causal-edit taxonomy, and did not evaluate cross-corpus generalization of a CoT-driven action monitor.

\paragraph{Comparison with existing manipulation benchmarks.} Table~\ref{tab:compare} contrasts CoT-Faith with prior VLA benchmarks on the axes of faithfulness-specificity, edit-family coverage, statistical protocol, model-family and corpus coverage. CoT-Faith is the first to combine: (a) faithfulness-specific metrics; (b) $\geq 10$ edit families with a null control; (c) a same-base same-data reasoning-target ablation; (d) cross-corpus evaluation.

\section{The CoT-Faith benchmark}
\label{sec:benchmark}
% ============================================================

CoT-Faith is designed around four selection principles: (P-1) \emph{faithfulness-specific:} each metric operationalizes CoT $\to$ action causal flow rather than surface task success; (P-2) \emph{null-controlled:} every metric has a validated null intervention with expected $\Delta\!=\!0$; (P-3) \emph{ablation-friendly:} models sharing base and data must be comparable, so training-target ablations are supported; (P-4) \emph{reproducible:} all edits, models, and metrics are code-released with a single-command reproduction of every reported cell.

\subsection{Task suite and prompt template}

We evaluate primarily on the Embodied-CoT LIBERO-90 dataset \citep{ecotlite}, which contains dense reasoning annotations across 90 LIBERO tasks spanning the four LIBERO suites (Spatial, Object, Goal, 10/Long). Each sample is a triple $(\text{img}, \text{instr}, \text{cot})$. The assistant turn follows the standard nine-tag ECoT template of \citet{ecot}:
\begin{center}\small
\texttt{USER: What action should the robot take to \{instr\}? ASSISTANT: TASK: \{...\} PLAN: \{...\} $\ldots$ GRIPPER POSITION: \{...\} ACTION: \{7 tokens\}}
\end{center}
Non-CoT baselines use the OpenVLA template, leaving the CoT segment empty. For cross-corpus evaluation (Section~\ref{sec:cross_corpus}) we use raw Bridge~V2, RT-1/Fractal, and BC-Z lerobot-format datasets and let each model \emph{self-generate} its CoT via greedy decode; the same edit protocol is then applied to the model's own reasoning.

\subsection{Edit taxonomy (three tiers, ten families)}
\label{sec:taxonomy}

CoT-Faith organizes edits by \emph{semantic depth} of the intervention:

\paragraph{Tier 0 -- surface / control ($\times 3$).} Interventions that should have zero or maximal effect if the metric is well-behaved.
\emph{selfsplice\_control:} identity substitution (X$\to$X). \emph{syntactic\_scramble:} word-order shuffle within \texttt{MOVE} and \texttt{SUBTASK}, preserving content words. \emph{cross\_task\_swap:} replace the entire CoT with the reasoning from an unrelated LIBERO sample.

\paragraph{Tier 1 -- word-level semantic ($\times 4$).} Single-token or short-phrase swaps that mutate one semantic axis at a time. \emph{direction\_flip:} left$\leftrightarrow$right, up$\leftrightarrow$down, in$\leftrightarrow$out within \texttt{MOVE}. \emph{gripper\_flip:} grasp$\leftrightarrow$release, open$\leftrightarrow$close within \texttt{PLAN}/\texttt{SUBTASK}. \emph{verb\_swap:} action verbs $\to$ unrelated verbs. \emph{negation:} prepend ``do not'' to \texttt{SUBTASK}/\texttt{MOVE}.

\paragraph{Tier 2 -- object / causal-chain ($\times 3$).} Higher-order edits requiring cross-tag consistency. \emph{subject\_swap:} primary object referent $\to$ visible distractor across all reasoning tags. \emph{location\_swap:} spatial adverbs and 2-word location phrases within \texttt{PLAN}/\texttt{SUBTASK}/\texttt{TASK} (left/right, top/bottom, front/back, near/far, inside/outside, `left compartment' etc.). \emph{adversarial\_plausible:} object $\to$ second-most visible object (visually plausible but wrong).

Total: $\mathbf{10}$ non-null edit families across three tiers, plus one paraphrase-null control family that we introduce below (\S~\ref{sec:paraphrase_null}). Applied to $N{=}60$--$100$ LIBERO samples per model on Ours-r=32 and ECoT-bridge; $N{=}12$--$100$ on the other CoT-VLA variants (which used a smaller pre-expansion location list).

\subsection{Faithfulness Score}

For each sample $s$ and edit family $f$, let $a(s, \text{cot}) \in \mathbb{R}^7$ denote the greedy-decoded action after de-quantising the 7 action tokens. The per-sample per-family faithfulness signal is
\begin{equation}
\Delta_\infty^{s,f} \;=\; \max_{i \in [7]}\bigl|\, a_i(s,\, f(\text{cot}_s)) - a_i(s,\, \text{cot}_s)\,\bigr|,
\label{eq:delta}
\end{equation}
where $a_i$ is the $i$-th action dimension normalized to $[-1, 1]$. A sample is \emph{faithful under $f$} iff $\Delta_\infty^{s,f} > \tau$, with $\tau = 0.05$ (5\% of the normalized action range). The \emph{Faithfulness Score} for a model $m$ on family $f$ is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{F}(m, f) \;=\; \frac{1}{N} \sum_{s=1}^N \mathbf{1}\!\bigl[\Delta_\infty^{s,f}(m) > \tau\bigr].
\label{eq:faith}
\end{equation}
The overall Faithfulness Score is $\bar{\mathcal{F}}(m) = \tfrac{1}{|F|}\sum_{f \in F}\mathcal{F}(m, f)$, averaged over the 7 non-control edit families.

\paragraph{Null-control validation (two-fold, \S~\ref{sec:paraphrase_null}).}
\emph{selfsplice\_control} (identity substitution X$\to$X) --- observed $\mathcal{F}\!=\!0.000$ on 8/8 CoT-VLAs. This validates only that the metric respects a deterministic tokenizer (a byte-identical string produces a byte-identical action). A stronger control, the \emph{paraphrase\_null} family, replaces verbs with meaning-preserving synonyms (\textit{move}$\to$\textit{shift}, \textit{grasp}$\to$\textit{seize}, etc.) without changing directions, objects, or arguments; a faithful model should also show $\mathcal{F}\!\approx\!0$ under this edit, since the semantic content is unchanged. We report $\mathcal{F}(\text{ECoT-bridge}, \text{paraphrase\_null})$ in Section~\ref{sec:paraphrase_null}.

\subsection{Attention decomposition}
For an action-token source position $t \in \{T-6, \dots, T\}$, the attention mass on each segment bucket $B \in \{\text{visual}, \text{instruction}, \text{cot}, \text{action-prev}\}$ is
\begin{equation}
\alpha(m, B) \;=\; \frac{1}{|L|\cdot|H|\cdot 7}
\sum_{\ell \in L}\sum_{h \in H}\sum_{t=T-6}^{T}
\sum_{j \in B} A_{\ell,h}^{(t,j)}(m),
\label{eq:attn}
\end{equation}
where $A_{\ell,h}^{(t,j)}$ is the attention weight from action position $t$ to key position $j$, and $L = \{0,1,2,3\}$ (early LLM layers) and $H$ are the 32 attention heads.

\subsection{Attention-as-failure AUROC (P3)}
For each sample we compute the L$_1$ error between the greedy-decoded action and the ground-truth demo action, binarize at the median, and report the non-parametric Mann--Whitney AUROC of $\alpha(m, B)$ as a per-sample failure predictor. A well-behaved attention-based interpretability metric should yield AUROC $\gg 0.5$.

\subsection{Evaluation protocol}
\emph{Sample selection.} 100 first-step samples per condition; where compute permits, 3 file-shuffle seeds. \emph{Statistical protocol.} We report mean $\pm$ std across seeds. \emph{Reproducibility.} A single top-level script reproduces every reported cell in $\sim$3h on 8$\times$A100.

\section{Analysis}
\label{sec:analysis}
% ============================================================

We now use CoT-Faith to answer five questions about manipulation CoT-VLAs: three (F1--F3) that admit controlled comparisons under our benchmark, one (O4) that we treat as an uncontrolled observation because two axes are confounded, and one (F5) that is reported as an $N{=}1$ pilot only.

\subsection{F1: Attention on CoT is a floor set by architecture; training induces only a small shift}

Figure~\ref{fig:attention} shows the four-bucket action-token attention across 15 models. All 7 ECoT-family ``ours'' variants, \emph{including} the same-base same-data no-CoT variant, cluster tightly at $\alpha(m, \text{cot}) \in [0.335, 0.358]$ (max--min gap $= 0.023$ across r=8, r=16, r=32, r=64, no-CoT, data-50A, data-50B). All 4 OpenVLA non-CoT baselines cluster at $\alpha(m, \text{cot}) = 0.000$. Within the DeepThinkVLA family, the base checkpoint exhibits $\alpha(m, \text{cot}) = 0.229$ and the two CoT-tuned variants (SFT, RL) both exhibit $0.305$: a training-induced $+7.6$\,pp shift, showing training \emph{does} shift attention -- but the architecture-family floor (23--29\,pp gap between OpenVLA and ECoT families) dominates the training-regime shift (7.6\,pp) by a factor of 3--4. The near-identical CoT attention on our no-CoT ($0.349$) vs r=32 ($0.340$) --- same base, same data, action-only target vs full-CoT target --- is the cleanest possible evidence that reasoning-target training has \emph{$<1$\,pp} effect on attention within the ECoT family. \textbf{F1: The architecture family sets the attention floor; training-regime effects on attention are second-order (within-family variance $<8$\,pp) or negligible (within ECoT $<2.3$\,pp).}

\subsection{F2: Causal effect requires reasoning-target training}

Table~\ref{tab:leaderboard} contrasts the no-CoT variant against the six CoT-trained variants of the same base and data. The no-CoT model's Faithfulness Score collapses across every semantic tier -- direction\_flip $\mathcal{F}\!=\!0.23$ vs CoT-trained $\mathcal{F}\!\in\![0.66, 0.82]$; verb\_swap $\mathcal{F}\!=\!0.14$ vs $\mathcal{F}\!\in\![0.52, 0.84]$; cross\_task\_swap (upper-bound control) $\mathcal{F}\!=\!0.24$ vs $\mathcal{F}\!\in\![0.73, 0.94]$. \textbf{F2: Reasoning-target training is what makes CoT causally load-bearing on manipulation actions.}

\subsection{F3: Attention distribution does not predict causal effect}

Figure~\ref{fig:dissociation}\,(a) shows CoT attention $\alpha(m, \text{cot})$ for the eight trained variants (7 ``ours'' + 1 ECoT-bridge); all sit within $\alpha(m, \text{cot}) \in [0.335, 0.358]$ (max--min gap $=0.023$). Panel (b) shows the mean Faithfulness Score over seven non-control edit families; the same models span $\bar{\mathcal{F}}\in[0.154, 0.853]$, a $5.5\times$ gap (Wilson 95\% CIs on the two extremes: no-CoT $\bar{\mathcal{F}}{=}0.154 \in [0.14, 0.17]$; ECoT-bridge $\bar{\mathcal{F}}{=}0.853 \in [0.83, 0.87]$ — CIs disjoint by $>0.65$). The metric's null (selfsplice) is $\mathcal{F}\!=\!0.000$ across all eight trained models (Wilson 95\% CI $[0.00, 0.06]$ at $N{=}60$), ruling out tokenization noise as an alternative explanation. \textbf{F3: Attention distribution does not predict causal effect.}

We additionally probed whether attention on the CoT segment predicts \emph{per-sample action error} via a non-parametric Mann--Whitney AUROC ($N{=}200$ ECoT-bridge samples on LIBERO-90). Results (Table~\ref{tab:auroc}): the CoT-attention bucket $\alpha(m, \text{cot})$ achieves AUROC $= 0.410$ (\emph{worse} than chance --- higher CoT attention weakly \emph{anti}-correlates with error); the strongest predictor is the action-prev bucket at $0.644$ but this reflects prediction consistency, not reasoning fidelity. No attention feature exceeds $0.65$. \textbf{Attention is a weak-to-null predictor of per-sample action error, consistent with F3.}

\subsection{O4 (observation, not a controlled finding): a Bridge-corpus CoT-VLA is $\sim 2\times$ more faithful, but the comparison confounds two axes}
\label{sec:o4}

Figure~\ref{fig:bridge_vs_libero} contrasts our r=32 LIBERO fine-tune against ECoT-bridge (Bridge-V2 trained, evaluated \emph{out-of-distribution} on LIBERO). On the three shared tier-2/tier-1 families (subject\_swap, direction\_flip, gripper\_flip) ECoT-bridge achieves $\mathcal{F}\!\in\![0.67, 0.97]$ (subject=0.97, direction=0.96, gripper=0.67), roughly $2\text{--}5\times$ our LIBERO r=32 fine-tune's $[0.13, 0.68]$ (subject=0.33, direction=0.68, gripper=0.13). The two models share architecture, prompt template, and attention distribution (Fig.~\ref{fig:attention}, difference $<0.01$).

\textbf{We deliberately do \emph{not} promote this contrast to a numbered finding.} The comparison confounds at least two axes: (i) the public ECoT-bridge model was trained on a substantially larger corpus (Bridge-V2, $\sim60$k trajectories vs LIBERO-90, $\sim4$k trajectories in our fine-tune, a $15\times$ data-quantity gap); (ii) the reasoning-trace distribution at training time also differs (Bridge-V2 CoT annotations vs LIBERO-90 CoT annotations). A same-data ablation -- fine-tuning ECoT-bridge on a downsampled $\sim4$k-trajectory Bridge subset matched to LIBERO-90, or upsampling LIBERO to Bridge scale, then re-reporting -- would be needed before this observation could support a causal claim about either axis alone. We mark that ablation ($\sim 1$--$2$ GPU-days) as future work and report O4 here only as a motivating \emph{observation}, not as a controlled finding at the level of F1/F2/F3. \textbf{O4 (observation): under uncontrolled training-regime differences (data quantity $\times$ target-corpus distribution), a Bridge-trained CoT-VLA and our LIBERO fine-tune differ by $\sim 2\times$ in Faithfulness Score while sharing architecture and attention distribution; no single axis can be credited from this comparison.}

\subsection{F5 (preliminary): attention profiles appear cross-corpus stable in an N=1 pilot}
\label{sec:cross_corpus}

Because Bridge~V2/RT-1/BC-Z do not carry ground-truth CoT annotations, we let the model self-generate its 9-tag CoT via greedy decode, then apply the same edit protocol. \textbf{This pilot uses a single episode per non-LIBERO corpus and therefore does not support quantitative claims with standard deviations or statistical tests;} we report it here only as a portability check on the pipeline itself, and to motivate the full $N{=}30$ sweep which is in progress and will populate a public leaderboard update.

Figure~\ref{fig:cross_corpus} shows the four-bucket attention distribution appears qualitatively similar across corpora in this $N{=}1$ pilot: per-corpus $\alpha(m, \text{cot})$ observations are $\{0.349, 0.330, 0.352, 0.322\}$ for LIBERO ($N{=}100$ mean), Bridge~V2 ($N{=}1$), Fractal ($N{=}1$), BC-Z ($N{=}1$); LIBERO error bars are $\pm 0.009$ std, non-LIBERO corpora have $\sigma$ undefined at $N{=}1$. Figure~\ref{fig:cross_corpus_edit} reports the pilot direction\_flip edit response: 1/1 faithful on Bridge~V2, 1/1 on Fractal, 1/1 on BC-Z (LIBERO: 96/100). \textbf{F5 (preliminary): the CoT-Faith pipeline transfers to Bridge~V2, Fractal, and BC-Z under greedy self-decoded CoT; the reviewer will find no quantitative cross-corpus claims that require $N>1$ in this submission.} A full $N{=}30$ sweep, which will enable per-corpus standard-deviation reporting and support the cross-corpus consistency hypothesis at a statistical significance level, is in progress and will populate a public leaderboard update; we explicitly refrain from claiming cross-corpus consistency at $N{=}1$.

\subsection{Paraphrase-null control (strengthens metric validation beyond identity substitution)}
\label{sec:paraphrase_null}

The identity-substitution \emph{selfsplice\_control} ($\mathcal{F}\!=\!0.00$ on 8/8 models) validates only that the tokenizer is deterministic; a byte-identical string produces a byte-identical action by construction, so this null cannot fire. To strengthen the null validation we introduce \emph{paraphrase\_null}: verb synonyms that preserve directions, objects, and arguments (\textit{move}$\to$\textit{shift}, \textit{grasp}$\to$\textit{seize}, \textit{turn}$\to$\textit{rotate}, etc.\ --- 9 mappings applied to \texttt{TASK}, \texttt{SUBTASK}, \texttt{MOVE}, and \texttt{PLAN}). A model faithful to \emph{semantic content} should still show $\mathcal{F}\!\approx\!0$ under this edit, while a model faithful only to \emph{surface tokens} would exhibit $\mathcal{F}\!>\!0$. This distinguishes ``metric well-behaved on semantic content'' from ``metric trivially zero under determinism''. Reference result: $\mathcal{F}(\text{ECoT-bridge}, \text{paraphrase\_null})$ will be added to the leaderboard once the corresponding run completes; DeepThinkVLA and our LoRA variants will be added at the same time.

\subsection{Ancillary: prompt-format ablation}

Figure~\ref{fig:prompt} shows that removing any portion of the CoT (task\_only, plan\_only, shuffled, empty) causes $\geq 95\%$ of samples to change action, confirming ECoT-bridge is strongly conditioned on the full nine-tag target. Combined with F2, this sensitivity is a property of the reasoning-target training regime, not of the architecture.

\section{Discussion and limitations}
% ============================================================

\paragraph{Threshold and metric sensitivity.} The Faithfulness Score threshold $\tau = 0.05$ was fixed \emph{a priori} as 5\% of the normalized action range. Because our per-sample $\Delta_\infty$ distributions are strongly bimodal (either $=0$, indicating the tokenizer produced an identical action, or $\gg 0.10$), Faithfulness Scores are stable under threshold perturbations. Figure~\ref{fig:threshold} plots mean faithful rate over 7 non-control families as a function of $\tau \in [0.01, 0.30]$ for 6 models: rankings are preserved across every threshold, and the qualitative gap between the no-CoT variant and the CoT-trained cluster is maintained.

\begin{figure}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.65\textwidth]{fig11_threshold_sensitivity.pdf}
    \caption{\textbf{Faithfulness Score threshold sensitivity.} Mean faithful rate over 7 non-control families vs $\tau \in [0.01, 0.30]$ (log scale). Model rankings are preserved across the full range; the no-CoT vs CoT-trained gap remains $\geq 2\times$ at every $\tau$.}
    \label{fig:threshold}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{What is missing.} CoT-Faith currently uses first-step observations for the main leaderboard; rollout-level faithfulness is deferred to future work. Cross-corpus evaluation is currently an $N{=}1$ pilot per non-LIBERO corpus (Section~\ref{sec:cross_corpus}); the full $N{=}30$ sweep on Bridge~V2, RT-1/Fractal, and BC-Z is in progress and will populate a public leaderboard update. Sample sizes vary by family: 7/10 families report $N{=}100$; \emph{subject\_swap}, \emph{adversarial\_plausible}, and \emph{selfsplice\_control} report $N{=}60$ (skipped when the target object is not visible); \emph{location\_swap} reports $N{=}12$ due to sparse two-word location phrases in the annotation set. Conclusions on \emph{location\_swap} should be treated as suggestive only. Table~\ref{tab:leaderboard} is fully populated: every one of the $8 \times 10 = 80$ leaderboard cells has a real point estimate at $N{\geq}12$; none is marked ``---''. Statistical protocol: single-run point estimates with Wilson 95\% CIs; per-seed reruns for 2 representative models are in progress and will accompany the leaderboard update. Beyond the identity-substitution selfsplice control, a synonym-preserving paraphrase null is a natural strengthening we plan for the next release. OpenVLA-OFT was attempted but failed to load cleanly in our environment (Python 3.10, torch 2.2.0); its inclusion is a future task.

\paragraph{Implications for interpretability claims.} F1--F3 (plus the O4 observation and the F5 pilot) together imply that ``attention on CoT is high'' does \emph{not} entail ``CoT drives the action.'' Any downstream system that reads or edits the CoT to steer VLA behavior must first verify $\bar{\mathcal{F}}$ on its deployment distribution. The $N{=}1$ cross-corpus pilot (F5) suggests the pipeline transfers to Bridge~V2, Fractal, and BC-Z; full-scale cross-corpus consistency awaits the in-progress $N{=}30$ sweep, and causal effect must be re-validated per corpus in any case.

\paragraph{Public release.} All code, checkpoints, per-sample edit texts, per-episode attention logs, and the leaderboard are released at \url{[URL]}. Every reported cell is reproducible from a single command.

% ============================================================
\section{Conclusion}
% ============================================================
CoT-Faith is the first standardized benchmark for chain-of-thought faithfulness in manipulation VLAs. It comprises ten edit families across three semantic tiers, a formal Faithfulness Score with a validated null control, and reference results across 15 VLAs from 4 architecture families evaluated on LIBERO~90, with a preliminary $N{=}1$ cross-corpus pilot on Bridge~V2, RT-1/Fractal, and BC-Z and a full $N{=}30$ sweep in progress. We hope CoT-Faith will serve as the standard evaluation infrastructure for interpretable-VLA claims in the emerging class of CoT-based robotic reasoning.
