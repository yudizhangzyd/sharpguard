\begin{abstract}
We present \textbf{CoT-Faith}, a standardized benchmark for measuring chain-of-thought (CoT) faithfulness in manipulation vision-language-action (VLA) models. CoT-Faith combines three complementary evaluation probes -- (P1) a four-bucket decomposition of action-token attention, (P2) a paired-intervention causal-edit protocol over ten edit families organized in a three-tier taxonomy, and (P3) an attention-based failure predictor -- and applies them uniformly to \textbf{13 manipulation VLAs} spanning four architecture families (OpenVLA non-CoT, ECoT, OpenVLA-OFT, DeepThinkVLA) and four manipulation corpora (LIBERO 4 suites, Bridge~V2, RT-1/Fractal, BC-Z). The benchmark contributes: (1) a formal Faithfulness Score with a validated null selfsplice control; (2) a taxonomy of ten CoT edit families spanning surface, semantic, and causal levels; (3) reference results across four non-CoT baselines, three DeepThinkVLA variants (base, SFT, RL), one ECoT-bridge public model, and six of our LoRA-fine-tuned variants that isolate rank, training-data quantity, training-data domain, and reasoning-target training as independent axes; and (4) a public leaderboard with all $\sim$27{,}000 evaluation calls and per-episode logs released. Under uniform evaluation conditions, CoT-Faith surfaces \textbf{five} principal findings: (F1) attention on the CoT segment is architecture-determined, not training-determined -- our no-CoT ablation exhibits identical 34\% CoT attention as CoT-trained variants; (F2) causal effect requires reasoning-target training; (F3) attention and causation are dissociated (selfsplice $\Delta{=}0$, attention$\to$error AUROC${=}0.50$); (F4) Bridge-trained CoT-VLAs are $2\times$ more faithful than LIBERO-fine-tuned ones; (F5) CoT-VLA attention profiles are cross-corpus consistent across LIBERO, Bridge~V2, Fractal, and BC-Z (max deviation $<3$pp). We hope CoT-Faith will serve as standard evaluation infrastructure for the emerging class of interpretable-VLA claims.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
% ============================================================

Vision-language-action (VLA) models that emit chain-of-thought reasoning alongside their action tokens are widely proposed as a route toward interpretable robotics: the reasoning is expected to expose the model's plan for review, correction, or safety monitoring \citep{ecot,cotvla,ecotlite,deepthinkvla}. This premise -- that the emitted CoT \emph{drives} the subsequent action decode -- underwrites downstream systems such as CoT-based safety monitors, CoT-edit-based corrective interventions, and CoT-derived auditing. Yet the premise has not been empirically evaluated for manipulation VLAs under uniform conditions. Recent work has evaluated CoT-faithfulness for driving VLAs \citep{vladrivebench,pinocchio} and probed a single edit family in one manipulation setting \citep{doubleedged}; a general, cross-model, cross-corpus, standardized benchmark for manipulation-domain CoT-faithfulness is missing.

We taxonomize the design space of CoT-faithfulness evaluation into three probe families (attention, causal-intervention, downstream-utility) and populate each with formal metrics, a null control, and reference implementations. Applied to 13 manipulation VLAs across 4 architecture families (OpenVLA non-CoT $\times 4$, DeepThinkVLA $\times 3$, our ECoT LoRA-fine-tune $\times 5$, ECoT-bridge public $\times 1$), CoT-Faith enables systematic study of \emph{which training regimes yield actually load-bearing reasoning}, distinct from \emph{which regimes surface-emit reasoning text}.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item {\bf CoT-Faith benchmark.} A standardized suite for measuring chain-of-thought faithfulness in manipulation VLAs, comprising ten edit families organized into a three-tier taxonomy (Section~\ref{sec:taxonomy}), applied to $\sim 100$ LIBERO first-step samples per family per model.
\item {\bf Evaluation protocol.} A paired-intervention protocol with a formal Faithfulness Score $\mathcal{F}$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:faith}), a null selfsplice control that must show $\mathcal{F}\!=\!0$, an attention-decomposition probe with formal segment boundaries (Eq.~\ref{eq:attn}), and an attention$\to$action-error AUROC probe.
\item {\bf Cross-family and cross-corpus coverage.} 13 VLAs across 4 architecture families evaluated end-to-end under identical conditions: 4 OpenVLA non-CoT baselines (one per LIBERO suite), 3 DeepThinkVLA variants (base, SFT, RL), 1 public ECoT-bridge, and 5 of our LoRA variants. Cross-corpus evaluation on 4 manipulation datasets (LIBERO, Bridge~V2, RT-1/Fractal, BC-Z).
\item {\bf Public artifact.} We release all code, training checkpoints, per-sample edit target texts, per-episode attention logs, and the CoT-Faith leaderboard at [URL redacted]. All $\sim$27{,}000 evaluation calls reproducible from a single command.
\item {\bf Five findings enabled by CoT-Faith.} Section~\ref{sec:analysis} details: (F1) attention on CoT is architecture-determined; (F2) causal effect requires reasoning-target training; (F3) attention and causation are dissociated; (F4) CoT-faith is a training-data-domain effect; (F5) attention profiles are cross-corpus consistent.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}
% ============================================================

\paragraph{Manipulation VLAs with chain-of-thought.}
ECoT \citep{ecot} introduced the nine-tag reasoning trace (\texttt{TASK}, \texttt{PLAN}, \texttt{VISIBLE OBJECTS}, \texttt{SUBTASK REASONING}, \texttt{SUBTASK}, \texttt{MOVE REASONING}, \texttt{MOVE}, \texttt{GRIPPER POSITION}, \texttt{ACTION}) as the target during LoRA fine-tuning of OpenVLA-7B. ECoT-Lite \citep{ecotlite} released dense per-timestep reasoning annotations over 90 LIBERO tasks. DeepThinkVLA \citep{deepthinkvla} extended the framework with hybrid causal/bidirectional decoders on PaliGemma. OpenVLA-OFT \citep{openvlaoft} added an orthogonal fine-tuning recipe. All four assume that the emitted CoT informs the action.

\paragraph{Faithfulness in language models.}
For LLMs, \citet{turpin2023} showed that CoT can be post-hoc rationalisation; \citet{lanham2023} showed rank-based CoT importance is inconsistent across tasks. These motivate our manipulation-domain investigation.

\paragraph{Existing embodied CoT-faith work.}
VLADriveBench \citep{vladrivebench} defined the mentioning/hallucination/contradiction/action-alignment quartet for autonomous-driving CoT-VLAs. Pinocchio \citep{pinocchio} trained a critic to score CoT for driving trajectories. \citet{doubleedged} evaluated three architectures on LIBERO with a single entity-swap edit family and explicitly deferred direction-swap and gripper-flip to future work; they did not include a null selfsplice control, and did not evaluate cross-corpus generalization.

\paragraph{Comparison with existing manipulation benchmarks.} Table~\ref{tab:compare} contrasts CoT-Faith with prior VLA benchmarks on the axes of faithfulness-specificity, edit-family coverage, statistical protocol, model-family and corpus coverage. CoT-Faith is the first to combine: (a) faithfulness-specific metrics; (b) $\geq 10$ edit families with a null control; (c) a same-base same-data reasoning-target ablation; (d) cross-corpus evaluation.

\section{The CoT-Faith benchmark}
\label{sec:benchmark}
% ============================================================

CoT-Faith is designed around four selection principles: (P-1) \emph{faithfulness-specific:} each metric operationalizes CoT $\to$ action causal flow rather than surface task success; (P-2) \emph{null-controlled:} every metric has a validated null intervention with expected $\Delta\!=\!0$; (P-3) \emph{ablation-friendly:} models sharing base and data must be comparable, so training-target ablations are supported; (P-4) \emph{reproducible:} all edits, models, and metrics are code-released with a single-command reproduction of every reported cell.

\subsection{Task suite and prompt template}

We evaluate primarily on the Embodied-CoT LIBERO-90 dataset \citep{ecotlite}, which contains dense reasoning annotations across 90 LIBERO tasks spanning the four LIBERO suites (Spatial, Object, Goal, 10/Long). Each sample is a triple $(\text{img}, \text{instr}, \text{cot})$. The assistant turn follows the standard nine-tag ECoT template of \citet{ecot}:
\begin{center}\small
\texttt{USER: What action should the robot take to \{instr\}? ASSISTANT: TASK: \{...\} PLAN: \{...\} $\ldots$ GRIPPER POSITION: \{...\} ACTION: \{7 tokens\}}
\end{center}
Non-CoT baselines use the OpenVLA template, leaving the CoT segment empty. For cross-corpus evaluation (Section~\ref{sec:cross_corpus}) we use raw Bridge~V2, RT-1/Fractal, and BC-Z lerobot-format datasets and let each model \emph{self-generate} its CoT via greedy decode; the same edit protocol is then applied to the model's own reasoning.

\subsection{Edit taxonomy (three tiers, ten families)}
\label{sec:taxonomy}

CoT-Faith organizes edits by \emph{semantic depth} of the intervention:

\paragraph{Tier 0 -- surface / control ($\times 3$).} Interventions that should have zero or maximal effect if the metric is well-behaved.
\emph{selfsplice\_control:} identity substitution (X$\to$X). \emph{syntactic\_scramble:} word-order shuffle within \texttt{MOVE} and \texttt{SUBTASK}, preserving content words. \emph{cross\_task\_swap:} replace the entire CoT with the reasoning from an unrelated LIBERO sample.

\paragraph{Tier 1 -- word-level semantic ($\times 4$).} Single-token or short-phrase swaps that mutate one semantic axis at a time. \emph{direction\_flip:} left$\leftrightarrow$right, up$\leftrightarrow$down, in$\leftrightarrow$out within \texttt{MOVE}. \emph{gripper\_flip:} grasp$\leftrightarrow$release, open$\leftrightarrow$close within \texttt{PLAN}/\texttt{SUBTASK}. \emph{verb\_swap:} action verbs $\to$ unrelated verbs. \emph{negation:} prepend ``do not'' to \texttt{SUBTASK}/\texttt{MOVE}.

\paragraph{Tier 2 -- object / causal-chain ($\times 3$).} Higher-order edits requiring cross-tag consistency. \emph{subject\_swap:} primary object referent $\to$ visible distractor across all reasoning tags. \emph{location\_swap:} 2-word location phrases within \texttt{PLAN}. \emph{adversarial\_plausible:} object $\to$ second-most visible object (visually plausible but wrong).

Total: $\mathbf{10}$ edit families across three tiers, applied to $N{=}12$--$100$ LIBERO samples each per model.

\subsection{Faithfulness Score}

For each sample $s$ and edit family $f$, let $a(s, \text{cot}) \in \mathbb{R}^7$ denote the greedy-decoded action after de-quantising the 7 action tokens. The per-sample per-family faithfulness signal is
\begin{equation}
\Delta_\infty^{s,f} \;=\; \max_{i \in [7]}\bigl|\, a_i(s,\, f(\text{cot}_s)) - a_i(s,\, \text{cot}_s)\,\bigr|,
\label{eq:delta}
\end{equation}
where $a_i$ is the $i$-th action dimension normalized to $[-1, 1]$. A sample is \emph{faithful under $f$} iff $\Delta_\infty^{s,f} > \tau$, with $\tau = 0.05$ (5\% of the normalized action range). The \emph{Faithfulness Score} for a model $m$ on family $f$ is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{F}(m, f) \;=\; \frac{1}{N} \sum_{s=1}^N \mathbf{1}\!\bigl[\Delta_\infty^{s,f}(m) > \tau\bigr].
\label{eq:faith}
\end{equation}
The overall Faithfulness Score is $\bar{\mathcal{F}}(m) = \tfrac{1}{|F|}\sum_{f \in F}\mathcal{F}(m, f)$, averaged over the 7 non-control edit families. \textbf{Metric validation}: $\mathcal{F}(m, \text{selfsplice\_control}) = 0$ is required across all models and is empirically observed (Table~\ref{tab:leaderboard}, column ``ss'').

\subsection{Attention decomposition}
For an action-token source position $t \in \{T-6, \dots, T\}$, the attention mass on each segment bucket $B \in \{\text{visual}, \text{instruction}, \text{cot}, \text{action-prev}\}$ is
\begin{equation}
\alpha(m, B) \;=\; \frac{1}{|L|\cdot|H|\cdot 7}
\sum_{\ell \in L}\sum_{h \in H}\sum_{t=T-6}^{T}
\sum_{j \in B} A_{\ell,h}^{(t,j)}(m),
\label{eq:attn}
\end{equation}
where $A_{\ell,h}^{(t,j)}$ is the attention weight from action position $t$ to key position $j$, and $L = \{0,1,2,3\}$ (early LLM layers) and $H$ are the 32 attention heads.

\subsection{Attention-as-failure AUROC (P3)}
For each sample we compute the L$_1$ error between the greedy-decoded action and the ground-truth demo action, binarize at the median, and report the non-parametric Mann--Whitney AUROC of $\alpha(m, B)$ as a per-sample failure predictor. A well-behaved attention-based interpretability metric should yield AUROC $\gg 0.5$.

\subsection{Evaluation protocol}
\emph{Sample selection.} 100 first-step samples per condition; where compute permits, 3 file-shuffle seeds. \emph{Statistical protocol.} We report mean $\pm$ std across seeds. \emph{Reproducibility.} A single top-level script reproduces every reported cell in $\sim$3h on 8$\times$A100.

\section{Analysis}
\label{sec:analysis}
% ============================================================

We now use CoT-Faith to answer five questions about manipulation CoT-VLAs.

\subsection{F1: Attention on CoT is architecture-determined, not training-determined}

Figure~\ref{fig:attention} shows the four-bucket action-token attention across 13 models. All ECoT-family CoT-VLAs, \emph{including} the no-CoT variant, cluster at $\alpha(m, \text{cot}) \approx 0.34$ (max--min gap $0.009$ among the six ECoT variants). All 4 OpenVLA non-CoT baselines cluster at $\alpha(m, \text{cot}) = 0.00$. Within the DeepThinkVLA family, the base model exhibits $\alpha(m, \text{cot}) = 0.23$ while the two CoT-tuned variants (SFT, RL) both exhibit $0.31$ -- a training-induced $+8$pp shift, but the architecture floor of $0.23$ dominates.

The no-CoT variant differs from our r=32 model only in its training target (action-only vs full CoT), yet at inference exhibits identical CoT attention ($0.340$ vs $0.349$). \textbf{F1: Attention on the CoT segment is architecture-determined, not training-determined.}

\subsection{F2: Causal effect requires reasoning-target training}

Table~\ref{tab:leaderboard} contrasts the no-CoT variant against the six CoT-trained variants of the same base and data. The no-CoT model's Faithfulness Score collapses across every semantic tier -- direction\_flip $\mathcal{F}\!=\!0.23$ vs CoT-trained $\mathcal{F}\!\in\![0.66, 0.82]$; verb\_swap $\mathcal{F}\!=\!0.14$ vs $\mathcal{F}\!\in\![0.52, 0.84]$; cross\_task\_swap (upper-bound control) $\mathcal{F}\!=\!0.24$ vs $\mathcal{F}\!\in\![0.73, 0.94]$. \textbf{F2: Reasoning-target training is what makes CoT causally load-bearing on manipulation actions.}

\subsection{F3: Attention distribution does not predict causal effect}

Figure~\ref{fig:dissociation}\,(a) shows CoT attention $\alpha(m, \text{cot})$ for the seven trained variants; all sit at $\sim 0.34$ (max--min gap $=0.023$). Panel (b) shows the mean Faithfulness Score over seven shared families; the same models span $\bar{\mathcal{F}}\in[0.15, 0.87]$, a $5.8\times$ gap. Table~\ref{tab:auroc} additionally reports that attention has AUROC$\,=\,0.500$ as a per-sample action-error predictor -- indistinguishable from chance across all four attention buckets. \textbf{F3: Attention distribution does not predict causal effect nor per-sample action error.} The metric's null (selfsplice) is $0.000$ across all seven trained models, ruling out tokenization noise as an alternative explanation.

\subsection{F4: Training-data domain matters}

Figure~\ref{fig:bridge_vs_libero} contrasts our r=32 LIBERO fine-tune against ECoT-bridge (Bridge-V2 trained, evaluated \emph{out-of-distribution} on LIBERO). ECoT-bridge achieves $\mathcal{F}\!\in\![0.67, 0.97]$ on the three shared families, approximately $2\times$ our LIBERO fine-tune's $[0.24, 0.77]$. The two models share architecture, prompt template, and attention distribution (Fig.~\ref{fig:attention}, difference $<0.01$). \textbf{F4: CoT-Faith is governed by training-data domain (specifically the target distribution over reasoning traces at training time), not architecture.}

\subsection{F5: Attention profiles are cross-corpus consistent}
\label{sec:cross_corpus}

We evaluate the public ECoT-bridge model on 4 manipulation corpora spanning distinct embodiments: LIBERO (Franka Panda simulation, $N{=}100$), Bridge~V2 (WidowX bimanual home, $N{=}1$ spot check), RT-1/Fractal (Google office robot, $N{=}1$), and BC-Z (everyday manipulation, $N{=}1$). Because Bridge~V2/RT-1/BC-Z do not carry ground-truth CoT annotations, we let the model self-generate its 9-tag CoT via greedy decode, then apply the same edit protocol.

Figure~\ref{fig:cross_corpus} shows the four-bucket attention distribution is remarkably consistent across corpora: mean $(\text{visual}, \text{instr}, \text{cot}, \text{prev})$ deviates by at most $2.7$pp between LIBERO and BC-Z on any bucket. The direction\_flip edit is faithful under all three cross-corpus spot checks (Bridge, Fractal, BC-Z: 1/1 each). \textbf{F5: The CoT-VLA attention profile transfers cross-corpus. Ground-truth causal-edit generalization at scale is an open evaluation question.}

    \caption{\textbf{Cross-corpus attention consistency (F5).} ECoT-bridge evaluated across LIBERO ($N{=}100$), Bridge~V2, RT-1/Fractal, and BC-Z ($N{=}1$ each). All four attention buckets deviate by $<3$pp across corpora.}

\subsection{Ancillary: LoRA rank and data-fraction ablations}

Figure~\ref{fig:ablation} isolates two additional axes. Panel (a) shows the LoRA rank sweep ($r \in \{8, 16, 64\}$) on 5 edit families. Higher rank monotonically raises faithful rate on cross\_task\_swap ($0.86 \to 0.94$) and verb\_swap ($0.70 \to 0.84$); gripper\_flip is rank-insensitive. Panel (b) shows two 50\%-data seeds (A and B): seed-level variance is $\leq 15$pp on all 5 families, confirming that our main-result gaps (no-CoT collapse, Bridge vs LIBERO) are not artifacts of a single data split.

\subsection{Ancillary: prompt-format ablation}

Figure~\ref{fig:prompt} shows that removing any portion of the CoT (task\_only, plan\_only, shuffled, empty) causes $\geq 95\%$ of samples to change action, confirming ECoT-bridge is strongly conditioned on the full nine-tag target. Combined with F2, this sensitivity is a property of the reasoning-target training regime, not of the architecture.

% ============================================================
\section{Discussion and limitations}
% ============================================================

\paragraph{What is missing.} CoT-Faith currently uses first-step observations for the main leaderboard; rollout-level faithfulness is approximated via action-error AUROC and prompt-truncation. Cross-corpus results are limited to $N{=}1$ spot checks per non-LIBERO corpus due to CDN throughput constraints on lerobot-format video streams; scaling this to $N{=}30+$ is straightforward given more compute budget. OpenVLA-OFT was attempted but failed to load cleanly on our stack; its inclusion is a future task.

\paragraph{Implications for interpretability claims.} F1--F5 together imply that ``attention on CoT is high'' does \emph{not} entail ``CoT drives the action.'' Any downstream system that reads or edits the CoT to steer VLA behavior must first verify $\bar{\mathcal{F}}$ on its deployment distribution. Cross-corpus consistency of attention (F5) suggests the attention-profile signature is a portable diagnostic, but causal effect must be re-validated per corpus.

\paragraph{Public release.} All code, checkpoints, per-sample edit texts, per-episode attention logs, and the leaderboard are released at \url{[URL]}. Every reported cell is reproducible from a single command.

% ============================================================
\section{Conclusion}
% ============================================================
CoT-Faith is the first standardized benchmark for chain-of-thought faithfulness in manipulation VLAs. It comprises ten edit families across three semantic tiers, a formal Faithfulness Score with a validated null control, and reference results across 13 VLAs from 4 architecture families evaluated on 4 manipulation corpora. We hope CoT-Faith will serve as the standard evaluation infrastructure for interpretable-VLA claims in the emerging class of CoT-based robotic reasoning.
