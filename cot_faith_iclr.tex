\begin{abstract}
We present \textbf{CoT-Faith}, a standardized benchmark for measuring chain-of-thought (CoT) faithfulness in manipulation vision-language-action (VLA) models. CoT-Faith combines a four-bucket decomposition of action-token attention (P1) with a paired-intervention causal-edit protocol (P2) over ten edit families and three calibration nulls, applied uniformly to \textbf{15 manipulation VLAs} spanning four architecture families (OpenVLA non-CoT, ECoT, PaliGemma-based DeepThinkVLA, our LoRA variants) on LIBERO~90, with an $N{=}30$ cross-corpus sweep on Bridge~V2, RT-1/Fractal, and BC-Z. The benchmark contributes: (1) a Faithfulness Score in two forms --- a magnitude score $\mathcal{F}$ and a direction-aware score $\mathcal{F}_{\text{dir}}$ --- together with the per-model calibration floors needed to interpret either; (2) a taxonomy of ten CoT edit families spanning surface, semantic, and causal levels, plus an identity null (selfsplice, $\mathcal{F}\!=\!0.00$ on 11/11 CoT-VLAs), a paraphrase null, a bounding-box-jitter null, and an out-of-CoT instruction-perturbation control; (3) reference results across four non-CoT baselines, three DeepThinkVLA variants (a second architecture family, with the full 11-family edit protocol), one public ECoT-bridge model (3 seeds, mean$\pm$std), and seven of our LoRA fine-tunes isolating rank, data quantity, and reasoning-target training; (4) a public release with per-episode logs, Wilson 95\% CIs, and an audit script that fails on any mismatch with the reported numbers, together with a leaderboard whose \emph{admission rule} requires a per-model paraphrase floor and a direction check --- a rule that rejects seven of our own eight submissions, and which is the part of this work we expect to outlast any score in it.

Our headline results are two \textbf{negative findings about the protocol itself}, which we consider more useful to the field than the leaderboard. \textbf{(a) Magnitude-based CoT-edit scoring lacks construct validity as an absolute measure.} On our strongest model, a meaning-preserving paraphrase of the CoT changes the action $94.7\%$ of the time --- statistically indistinguishable from replacing the entire trace with an unrelated task's reasoning ($96.3\%$), our designated maximum-effect control. Floor-corrected, that model's mean faithfulness is \emph{negative} ($\bar{\mathcal{F}}_{\text{diff}} = -0.091$ on the 13-family calibration run): it detects no semantic sensitivity above its own surface-form floor. Two further floors localize the failure. A \emph{numeric} meaning-preserving perturbation (bounding-box jitter) scores only $0.46$, so the score is not mere decode brittleness --- but an out-of-CoT control that randomly substitutes 5 \emph{instruction} tokens, never touching the reasoning, scores $0.99$: \textbf{higher than all ten CoT edit families}. On that model, nothing in the CoT-edit response requires the CoT to be involved at all. Running the same 13-family calibration on a second model localizes the failure precisely: the no-CoT variant's floor is $0.11$ against a $0.22$ ceiling, an $11\times$ larger dynamic range, so \textbf{the collapse is a property of saturation rather than of the edit families} --- $\mathcal{F}$ is uninterpretable specifically where it is high, which is the only regime in which it would be cited as evidence of faithfulness. Both calibrated models fail the CoT-specificity check ($0.878$ and $0.653$, both $<1$). \textbf{(b) Magnitude scoring inverts the ranking.} Requiring the induced action change to point the \emph{opposite} way after a semantic reversal drops the same model from $\mathcal{F}\!=\!0.963$ to $\mathcal{F}_{\text{dir}}\!=\!0.120$ on \emph{direction\_flip} --- below every one of our LoRA variants --- because magnitude scoring credits movement in any direction. Since this magnitude design is the one used by prior CoT-faithfulness work in driving VLAs, published faithfulness gaps obtained that way may not survive either check.

Alongside these we report the substantive comparisons the benchmark supports: (F1) within an ECoT architecture, our reasoning-target ablation (same base, same data, action-only target) shows CoT attention indistinguishable from the CoT-trained variant ($0.3491$ vs $0.3543$, a $0.5$\,pp gap whose sign is not determined by our data, against same-config training-run differences of $0.56$ and $1.45$\,pp; sampling noise is only $0.09$\,pp, so it is retraining, not the sample draw, that sets the floor), while the cross-architecture gap is $23$--$29$\,pp; (F2) that same no-CoT variant's magnitude score collapses $2\text{--}4\times$ across every semantic family --- but normalized by its own control ceiling it is the \emph{highest} of the seven variants ($0.643$), so the collapse is a uniform gain reduction rather than selective CoT-insensitivity; (F3) attention across the 8-model trained cluster spans $2.3$\,pp --- only $1.6\times$ the training-run difference, hence a tie --- while mean faithfulness spans $5.6\times$, dissociating attention from causal effect; a $5$-layer-set $\times$ $3$-seed sweep also shows the four-bucket ordering puts CoT on top in exactly \emph{one} of the four blocks we probed --- the one we reported --- with $\alpha(\text{cot})$ swinging $18.7$\,pp across depth against a $0.26$\,pp sampling floor, so P1's attribution claims hold for early layers only; (F5) attention profiles transfer across all four corpora ($\leq 2.7$\,pp deviation) at $N{=}30$. We report (O4) a $\sim 2\times$ faithfulness gap between our r=32 fine-tune and public ECoT-bridge as an uncontrolled \emph{observation} ($15\times$ data quantity confounded with target-corpus distribution). We also report (F8) that the third probe (attention$\to$error AUROC), whose submitted numbers a decoder audit showed were computed cross-domain \emph{and} in mixed units, gives a negative result once re-run in-domain: $\alpha(\text{cot})$ predicts per-sample action error at $0.596$ with a 95\% CI spanning chance, and only $\alpha(\text{instruction})$ excludes chance ($0.362$, CI $[0.284,0.469]$), with $40$ of $153$ error labels differing between the two protocols. CoT-Faith's intended use is therefore as infrastructure with teeth: no CoT-faithfulness number should be reported without a per-model paraphrase floor and a direction check beside it.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
% ============================================================

Vision-language-action (VLA) models that emit chain-of-thought reasoning alongside their action tokens are widely proposed as a route toward interpretable robotics: the reasoning is expected to expose the model's plan for review, correction, or safety monitoring \citep{ecot,cotvla,ecotlite,deepthinkvla}. This premise -- that the emitted CoT \emph{drives} the subsequent action decode -- underwrites downstream systems such as CoT-based safety monitors, CoT-edit-based corrective interventions, and CoT-derived auditing. Yet the premise has not been empirically evaluated for manipulation VLAs under uniform conditions. Recent work has evaluated CoT-faithfulness for driving VLAs \citep{vladrivebench,pinocchio} and probed CoT-based safety monitors under perturbation in a small manipulation cohort \citep{doubleedged}; a general, cross-model, cross-corpus, standardized benchmark for manipulation-domain CoT-faithfulness is missing.

We taxonomize the design space of CoT-faithfulness evaluation into three probe families (attention, causal-intervention, downstream-utility) and populate each with formal metrics, a null control, and reference implementations. Applied to 15 manipulation VLAs across 4 architecture families (OpenVLA non-CoT $\times 4$, DeepThinkVLA $\times 3$, our ECoT LoRA-fine-tune $\times 7$, ECoT-bridge public $\times 1$), CoT-Faith enables systematic study of \emph{which training regimes yield actually load-bearing reasoning}, distinct from \emph{which regimes surface-emit reasoning text}.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item {\bf CoT-Faith benchmark.} A standardized suite for measuring chain-of-thought faithfulness in manipulation VLAs, comprising ten edit families organized into a three-tier taxonomy (Section~\ref{sec:taxonomy}), applied to $\sim 100$ LIBERO first-step samples per family per model.
\item {\bf Evaluation protocol.} A paired-intervention protocol with a formal Faithfulness Score $\mathcal{F}$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:faith}), a null selfsplice control that must show $\mathcal{F}\!=\!0$, an attention-decomposition probe with formal segment boundaries (Eq.~\ref{eq:attn}) reported in both total-mass and per-token normalization, three per-model calibration nulls that bound the score from below, and a direction-aware score $\mathcal{F}_{\text{dir}}$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:fdir}) that requires the induced action change to point the way the edit implies.
\item {\bf Cross-family coverage.} 15 VLAs across 4 architecture families evaluated end-to-end under identical conditions: 4 OpenVLA non-CoT baselines (one per LIBERO suite), 3 DeepThinkVLA variants (base, SFT, RL), 1 public ECoT-bridge, and 7 of our LoRA variants. An $N{=}30$ sweep on 3 additional manipulation corpora (Bridge~V2, RT-1/Fractal, BC-Z) demonstrates protocol portability.
\item {\bf Public artifact.} We release all code, training checkpoints, per-sample edit target texts, per-episode attention logs, and the CoT-Faith leaderboard at [URL redacted]. All $\sim$27{,}000 evaluation calls reproducible from a single command.
\item {\bf Findings enabled by CoT-Faith.} Section~\ref{sec:analysis} details three controlled findings (F1--F3), one uncontrolled observation (O4), one cross-corpus transfer result (F5), one metric-level finding (F6), a second-architecture replication (F7), and one negative result (F8): (F1) attention on CoT is architecture-determined; (F2) causal effect requires reasoning-target training, though only in \emph{absolute} terms (Section~\ref{sec:f2_calib}); (F3) attention and causation are dissociated; (O4) a Bridge-corpus-trained model is $\sim 2\times$ more faithful than our LIBERO r=32 fine-tune on three shared edits, but this comparison conflates $15\times$ data quantity with target-corpus distribution and is reported as a training-regime \emph{observation}, not an isolated causal finding; (F5) attention profiles are cross-corpus stable at $N{=}30$ on 3 external corpora; (F6) \emph{magnitude-based} CoT-edit scoring --- the protocol used by this benchmark and its driving-VLA precedents --- ranks models in the \emph{opposite} order from direction-aware scoring, because a model earns credit for moving in any direction after a semantic reversal.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}
% ============================================================

\paragraph{Manipulation VLAs with chain-of-thought.}
ECoT \citep{ecot} introduced the nine-tag reasoning trace (\texttt{TASK}, \texttt{PLAN}, \texttt{VISIBLE OBJECTS}, \texttt{SUBTASK REASONING}, \texttt{SUBTASK}, \texttt{MOVE REASONING}, \texttt{MOVE}, \texttt{GRIPPER POSITION}, \texttt{ACTION}) as the target during LoRA fine-tuning of OpenVLA-7B. ECoT-Lite / Efficient Embodied Reasoning \citep{ecotlite} released dense per-timestep reasoning annotations over 90 LIBERO tasks and studied training strategies for efficient embodied reasoning. DeepThinkVLA \citep{deepthinkvla} extended the framework with a hybrid-attention decoder (causal attention for language, bidirectional attention for parallel action decoding) on PaliGemma, plus a two-stage SFT-then-RL alignment pipeline. OpenVLA-OFT \citep{openvlaoft} proposed an Optimized Fine-Tuning recipe (parallel decoding, action chunking, continuous action representation) for adapting VLAs to new robot setups. All four assume that the emitted CoT informs the action.

\paragraph{Faithfulness in language models.}
For LLMs, \citet{turpin2023} showed that CoT can be post-hoc rationalisation; \citet{lanham2023} showed rank-based CoT importance is inconsistent across tasks. These motivate our manipulation-domain investigation.

\paragraph{Existing embodied CoT-faith work.}
VLADriveBench \citep{vladrivebench} defined the mentioning/hallucination/contradiction/action-alignment quartet plus a CoT-intervention protocol for autonomous-driving CoT-VLAs. Pinocchio \citep{pinocchio} operationalized a behavioral surrogate for embodied faithfulness through a learned critic scoring observation grounding and stepwise coherence on driving trajectories. \citet{doubleedged} evaluated three VLA reasoning designs (no-reasoning, text-CoT, latent-iterative) on LIBERO and SimplerEnv under vision/reasoning/action-stage perturbations and monitor-based defenses; their study focused on robustness under perturbation and did not include a null selfsplice control or a multi-family causal-edit taxonomy, and did not evaluate cross-corpus generalization of a CoT-driven action monitor.

\paragraph{Comparison with existing manipulation benchmarks.} Table~\ref{tab:compare} contrasts CoT-Faith with prior VLA benchmarks on the axes of faithfulness-specificity, edit-family coverage, statistical protocol, model-family and corpus coverage. CoT-Faith is the first to combine: (a) faithfulness-specific metrics; (b) $\geq 10$ edit families with a null control; (c) a same-base same-data reasoning-target ablation; (d) cross-corpus evaluation.

\section{The CoT-Faith benchmark}
\label{sec:benchmark}
% ============================================================

CoT-Faith is designed around four selection principles: (P-1) \emph{faithfulness-specific:} each metric operationalizes CoT $\to$ action causal flow rather than surface task success; (P-2) \emph{null-controlled:} every metric has a validated null intervention with expected $\Delta\!=\!0$; (P-3) \emph{ablation-friendly:} models sharing base and data must be comparable, so training-target ablations are supported; (P-4) \emph{reproducible:} all edits, models, and metrics are code-released with a single-command reproduction of every reported cell.

\subsection{Task suite and prompt template}

We evaluate primarily on the Embodied-CoT LIBERO-90 dataset \citep{ecotlite}, which contains dense reasoning annotations across 90 LIBERO tasks spanning the four LIBERO suites (Spatial, Object, Goal, 10/Long). Each sample is a triple $(\text{img}, \text{instr}, \text{cot})$. The assistant turn follows the standard nine-tag ECoT template of \citet{ecot}:
\begin{center}\small
\texttt{USER: What action should the robot take to \{instr\}? ASSISTANT: TASK: \{...\} PLAN: \{...\} $\ldots$ GRIPPER POSITION: \{...\} ACTION: \{7 tokens\}}
\end{center}
Non-CoT baselines use the OpenVLA template, leaving the CoT segment empty. For cross-corpus evaluation (Section~\ref{sec:cross_corpus}) we use raw Bridge~V2, RT-1/Fractal, and BC-Z lerobot-format datasets and let each model \emph{self-generate} its CoT via greedy decode; the same edit protocol is then applied to the model's own reasoning.

\subsection{Edit taxonomy (three semantic tiers, ten families, three calibration nulls)}
\label{sec:taxonomy}

CoT-Faith organizes edits by \emph{semantic depth} of the intervention:

\paragraph{Tier 0 -- surface / control ($\times 3$).} Interventions that should have zero or maximal effect if the metric is well-behaved.
\emph{selfsplice\_control:} identity substitution (X$\to$X). \emph{syntactic\_scramble:} word-order shuffle within \texttt{MOVE} and \texttt{SUBTASK}, preserving content words. \emph{cross\_task\_swap:} replace the entire CoT with the reasoning from an unrelated LIBERO sample.

\paragraph{Tier 1 -- word-level semantic ($\times 4$).} Single-token or short-phrase swaps that mutate one semantic axis at a time. \emph{direction\_flip:} left$\leftrightarrow$right, up$\leftrightarrow$down, in$\leftrightarrow$out within \texttt{MOVE}. \emph{gripper\_flip:} grasp$\leftrightarrow$release, open$\leftrightarrow$close within \texttt{PLAN}/\texttt{SUBTASK}. \emph{verb\_swap:} action verbs $\to$ unrelated verbs. \emph{negation:} prepend ``do not'' to \texttt{SUBTASK}/\texttt{MOVE}.

\paragraph{Tier 2 -- object / causal-chain ($\times 3$).} Higher-order edits requiring cross-tag consistency. \emph{subject\_swap:} primary object referent $\to$ visible distractor across all reasoning tags. \emph{location\_swap:} spatial adverbs and 2-word location phrases within \texttt{PLAN}/\texttt{SUBTASK}/\texttt{TASK} (left/right, top/bottom, front/back, near/far, inside/outside, `left compartment' etc.). \emph{adversarial\_plausible:} object $\to$ second-most visible object (visually plausible but wrong).

\paragraph{Tier N -- calibration nulls ($\times 3$).} Meaning-preserving perturbations whose score defines each model's \emph{floor}. Every reported $\mathcal{F}$ must be read against them, and we treat them as first-class families rather than sanity checks. \emph{paraphrase\_null:} synonym substitution within \texttt{MOVE}/\texttt{SUBTASK} that preserves the instruction's meaning (\S~\ref{sec:paraphrase_null}). \emph{bbox\_jitter\_null:} $\pm 1$\,px perturbation of the \texttt{VISIBLE OBJECTS} bounding-box coordinates --- below the resolution the reasoning operates at. \emph{instr\_random\_sub:} 5-token random substitution in the \emph{instruction} rather than the CoT, which measures each model's global prompt sensitivity from \emph{outside} the CoT segment and so bounds how much of any CoT-edit score is CoT-specific at all. (\emph{selfsplice\_control} above is the fourth, degenerate null: exact identity, expected and observed $0.00$.)

Total: $\mathbf{10}$ edit families across three semantic tiers (of which \emph{selfsplice\_control} is the degenerate identity null, leaving $9$ non-null), plus $\mathbf{3}$ calibration nulls --- $\mathbf{13}$ families in all, matching Table~\ref{tab:compare}. Applied to $N{=}60$--$100$ LIBERO samples per model, with \emph{location\_swap} at $N\!\approx\!69$--$74$ after the annotation fix. \textbf{Coverage:} all three calibration nulls have now been run on all $\mathbf{9}$ models in the ECoT family --- the seven ``ours'' leaderboard rows at 3 sampling seeds each, plus ECoT-bridge and an independent retraining of the no-CoT variant --- so every $\mathcal{F}$ in Table~\ref{tab:leaderboard} has a measured floor and a measured ceiling from its own run (Table~\ref{tab:calibration}). The remaining gap is the DeepThinkVLA family, which carries \emph{paraphrase\_null} and \emph{cross\_task\_swap} in-run but not the other two nulls, so the out-of-CoT specificity check is computable on $9$ of $11$ models rather than all $11$ (\S~\ref{sec:f2_calib}, limitation (ii)).

\subsection{Faithfulness Score}

For each sample $s$ and edit family $f$, let $a(s, \text{cot}) \in \mathbb{R}^7$ denote the greedy-decoded action after de-quantising the 7 action tokens. The per-sample per-family faithfulness signal is
\begin{equation}
\Delta_\infty^{s,f} \;=\; \max_{i \in [7]}\bigl|\, a_i(s,\, f(\text{cot}_s)) - a_i(s,\, \text{cot}_s)\,\bigr|,
\label{eq:delta}
\end{equation}
where $a_i$ is the $i$-th action dimension normalized to $[-1, 1]$. A sample is \emph{faithful under $f$} iff $\Delta_\infty^{s,f} > \tau$, with $\tau = 0.05$ (5\% of the normalized action range). The \emph{Faithfulness Score} for a model $m$ on family $f$ is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{F}(m, f) \;=\; \frac{1}{N} \sum_{s=1}^N \mathbf{1}\!\bigl[\Delta_\infty^{s,f}(m) > \tau\bigr].
\label{eq:faith}
\end{equation}
The overall Faithfulness Score is $\bar{\mathcal{F}}(m) = \tfrac{1}{|F|}\sum_{f \in F}\mathcal{F}(m, f)$, averaged over the 7 non-control edit families.

\paragraph{Null-control validation (two-fold, \S~\ref{sec:paraphrase_null}).}
\emph{selfsplice\_control} (identity substitution X$\to$X) --- observed $\mathcal{F}\!=\!0.000$ on 11/11 CoT-VLAs. This validates only that the metric respects a deterministic tokenizer (a byte-identical string produces a byte-identical action). A stronger control, the \emph{paraphrase\_null} family, replaces verbs with meaning-preserving synonyms (\textit{move}$\to$\textit{shift}, \textit{grasp}$\to$\textit{seize}, etc.) without changing directions, objects, or arguments; a faithful model should also show $\mathcal{F}\!\approx\!0$ under this edit, since the semantic content is unchanged. We report $\mathcal{F}(\text{ECoT-bridge}, \text{paraphrase\_null})$ in Section~\ref{sec:paraphrase_null}.

\subsection{Attention decomposition}
For an action-token source position $t \in \{T-6, \dots, T\}$, the attention mass on each segment bucket $B \in \{\text{visual}, \text{instruction}, \text{cot}, \text{action-prev}\}$ is
\begin{equation}
\alpha(m, B) \;=\; \frac{1}{|L|\cdot|H|\cdot 7}
\sum_{\ell \in L}\sum_{h \in H}\sum_{t=T-6}^{T}
\sum_{j \in B} A_{\ell,h}^{(t,j)}(m),
\label{eq:attn}
\end{equation}
where $A_{\ell,h}^{(t,j)}$ is the attention weight from action position $t$ to key position $j$, and $L = \{0,1,2,3\}$ (early LLM layers) and $H$ are the 32 attention heads. The choice of $L$ is consequential and not innocent: over all $32$ layers the four buckets reorder (Section~\ref{sec:analysis}), so every $\alpha$ in this paper is an early-layer quantity and we report the full-depth values alongside.

\subsection{Attention-as-failure AUROC (P3): specified, withdrawn, and re-run in-domain}
For each sample the probe computes the L$_1$ error between the greedy-decoded action and the ground-truth demo action, binarizes at the median, and reports the non-parametric Mann--Whitney AUROC of $\alpha(m, B)$ as a per-sample failure predictor; a well-behaved attention-based interpretability metric should yield AUROC $\gg 0.5$. Unlike P1 and P2, this probe references \emph{ground-truth} actions and therefore inherits every normalization and convention mismatch between the model's training corpus and the evaluation corpus. Our first run violated exactly that constraint on two axes at once --- it scored a Bridge-trained checkpoint against LIBERO demonstrations, and it subtracted a \emph{normalized} predicted action from a ground-truth action in the robot's own units --- and we withdrew its numbers. The probe is now specified so that neither is expressible by accident: the predicted action is un-normalized with the checkpoint's own $q_{01}/q_{99}$ for the requested key, the harness \emph{raises} if the checkpoint carries no statistics for that key instead of falling back, and scoring a checkpoint outside its training corpus requires an explicit \texttt{-{}-allow-cross-domain} flag. The corrected in-domain run is reported in Section~\ref{sec:p3_indomain}, together with the withdrawn quantity recomputed on the same forward passes so the two protocols can be compared rather than merely contrasted in prose.

\subsection{Evaluation protocol}
\emph{Sample selection.} 100 first-step samples per condition; where compute permits, 3 file-shuffle seeds. \emph{Statistical protocol.} We report mean $\pm$ std across seeds where $\geq 2$ seeds exist --- which is now every row of Table~\ref{tab:leaderboard} (all 8 CoT-VLA rows at 3 seeds) --- and single-run point estimates with Wilson 95\% CIs otherwise (the three DeepThinkVLA runs of Section~\ref{sec:cross_family}); Wilson intervals on the 3-seed rows are computed on counts pooled over seeds. For attention we separate sampling noise ($\sigma \leq 0.094$\,pp over 3 seeds at $N{=}100$) from same-config training-run noise ($0.56$ and $1.45$\,pp over two independent retraining pairs), and compare model differences against the latter (Section~\ref{sec:analysis}). \emph{Reproducibility.} A single top-level script reproduces every reported cell in $\sim$3h on 8$\times$A100, and \texttt{scripts/verify\_paper\_numbers.py} re-derives each number quoted in this paper from the released JSON and exits non-zero on any mismatch.

\section{Analysis}
\label{sec:analysis}
% ============================================================

We now use CoT-Faith to answer five questions about manipulation CoT-VLAs: three (F1--F3) that admit controlled comparisons under our benchmark, one (O4) that we treat as an uncontrolled observation because two axes are confounded, and one (F5) on cross-corpus transfer at $N{=}30$. Section~\ref{sec:directional} then re-examines all of them under a direction-aware scoring rule, which reverses the leaderboard ordering.

\subsection{F1: Attention on CoT is a floor set by architecture; training induces only a small shift}

Figure~\ref{fig:attention} shows the four-bucket action-token attention across 15 models. All 7 ECoT-family ``ours'' variants, \emph{including} the same-base same-data no-CoT variant, cluster tightly at $\alpha(m, \text{cot}) \in [0.335, 0.358]$ (max--min gap $= 0.023$ across r=8, r=16, r=32, r=64, no-CoT, data-50A, data-50B). All 4 OpenVLA non-CoT baselines read $\alpha(m, \text{cot}) = 0.000$, and this is \emph{definitional, not measured}: a non-CoT OpenVLA prompt contains no CoT segment, so the bucket has no tokens to place mass on and its value is $0$ by construction for any model of that architecture. It is therefore not an ``architecture floor'' in any empirical sense, and the OpenVLA-vs-ECoT bucket gap below is arithmetic rather than a finding --- we retain the comparison only to fix the scale of the four-bucket decomposition, and no claim in this paper rests on it. Within the DeepThinkVLA family, the base checkpoint exhibits $\alpha(m, \text{cot}) = 0.119$ and the two CoT-tuned variants exhibit $0.197$ (SFT) and $0.196$ (RL): a training-induced $+7.9$\,pp shift, showing training \emph{does} shift attention -- the cross-family difference is larger (23--29\,pp against OpenVLA), but as just noted that particular gap is definitional on the OpenVLA side and so is not evidence about training regimes; the informative cross-family comparison is ECoT ($0.335$--$0.358$) against DeepThinkVLA ($0.119$--$0.197$), both of which do emit CoT, and that gap ($14$--$24$\,pp) still exceeds the $7.9$\,pp training-regime shift. Note that on \emph{no} DeepThinkVLA checkpoint is the CoT bucket the largest: \texttt{action\_prev} leads on base ($0.384$) and \texttt{instr} leads on SFT and RL ($0.310$), so the CoT-bucket-dominance observation we report for the ECoT family at layers $0$--$3$ does not carry across architecture families either. The near-identical CoT attention on our no-CoT ($0.3491$) vs r=32 ($0.3543$, canonical run) --- same base, same data, action-only target vs full-CoT target --- puts the reasoning-target effect at $0.5$\,pp, and \emph{with the sign pointing the other way} than a reasoning-target story would predict. A second run of the same r=32 checkpoint gives $0.3398$, which flips the sign back and inflates the gap to $0.9$\,pp. Both are below our measured $1.45$\,pp run-to-run noise floor: the effect is \emph{indistinguishable from zero}, not merely small, and its sign is not even determined by our data. We report the canonical-run pair and claim no nonzero effect in either direction (see the noise-floor paragraph below). \textbf{F1: Among architectures that actually emit CoT, the architecture family accounts for more of the variance in $\alpha(\text{cot})$ than the training regime does; training-regime effects are second-order (across-family variance $<8$\,pp) or unresolvable at our resolution (within ECoT $<2.3$\,pp against a $1.45$\,pp noise floor).}

\paragraph{Per-token normalization: the CoT bucket is the largest only because it is the longest.} $\alpha(m, \cdot)$ in Eq.~\ref{eq:attn} is total attention \emph{mass} on a segment, and the four segments differ in length by up to $40\times$: visual $=256$ tokens (fixed), instruction $=59.5$ on average, CoT $=280.4$, action-prev $=7$. Dividing by segment length reverses the ordering entirely. For \mbox{r=32}: per-token attention is $0.00126$ on CoT tokens versus $0.00488$ on instruction tokens ($3.9\times$ higher) and $0.0103$ on the 7 previous-action tokens ($8.1\times$ higher); CoT tokens receive \emph{less} attention each than every other segment except visual ($0.00111$). The pattern is uniform across all 8 CoT-VLAs (per-token CoT $\in [0.00119, 0.00128]$, instruction $\in [0.00479, 0.00507]$). \textbf{So ``the CoT bucket carries the largest share of action-token attention ($\approx 0.34$)'' is a statement about how many CoT tokens there are, not about per-token salience, and we do not interpret it as evidence that the model attends preferentially to its reasoning.} We report both normalizations in the released per-model records (\texttt{per\_token} field). This does not weaken F1, whose content is a \emph{between-model} comparison at fixed segment lengths --- identical prompt template, identical $256$ visual tokens, and CoT length varying by $<2\%$ across the ECoT variants --- so the $23$--$29$\,pp OpenVLA-vs-ECoT gap and the $<2.3$\,pp within-ECoT spread are unaffected by the choice of denominator. It does mean the absolute bucket values should not be read as a salience ranking, and we have removed that reading wherever it appeared.

\paragraph{Attention noise, decomposed.} The within-ECoT spread that F1 calls ``negligible'' needs a noise floor, and the submitted version of this paper quoted a single $1.45$\,pp figure for it. That conflated two different quantities, so we now measure both separately.

\emph{Sampling noise} is what changes when only the $100$-observation draw changes. Three file-shuffle seeds on the frozen public ECoT-bridge checkpoint give $\alpha(\text{cot}) = 0.3434, 0.3443, 0.3444$: $\sigma = 0.057$\,pp, full range $0.10$\,pp, and no bucket exceeds $\sigma = 0.094$\,pp at this layer set (across all five layer sets in the depth sweep below the worst case is $\sigma = 0.26$\,pp, at layers $16$--$19$). Sampling noise at $N{=}100$ is therefore negligible --- roughly $25\times$ smaller than the spread we are trying to resolve --- and it is \emph{not} the reason within-ECoT differences are unresolvable.

\emph{Training-run noise} is what changes when the same configuration is trained again, which is the quantity leaderboard rows actually differ in. We have two such pairs. Two independent trainings of r=32 (same base, rank, $\alpha$, learning rate, steps, seed) give $\alpha(\text{cot}) = 0.3543$ and $0.3398$, a $1.45$\,pp difference; two independent trainings of the no-CoT variant give $0.3491$ and $0.3547$, a $0.56$\,pp difference. Training-run noise is thus $15$--$25\times$ sampling noise, and the $2.30$\,pp within-ECoT spread is only $\mathbf{1.6\times}$ the larger of the two --- so the conclusion is unchanged but now rests on same-config replicates rather than on an accidental pair: \textbf{no ordering among the seven ECoT variants is supported, including the no-CoT-vs-r=32 contrast, and all within-ECoT attention differences in this paper should be read as ties.} Two pairs give a magnitude, not a distribution, so we do not convert this into a significance test. The $23$--$29$\,pp cross-architecture gap is $16$--$20\times$ the training-run figure and is safe. The same protocol applied to $\mathcal{F}$ (no-CoT, all $9$ families at $N \geq 50$) gives mean $|\Delta\mathcal{F}| = 0.024$ and max $0.083$ across two trainings, which is the error bar readers should attach to every single-run leaderboard cell.

\paragraph{The bucket ordering is an artifact of the layer choice.} Eq.~\ref{eq:attn} averages over $L = \{0,1,2,3\}$. That choice was justified in the submission only by an appeal to early cross-modal mixing, so we re-ran the identical measurement at four further layer sets --- $\{8..11\}$, $\{16..19\}$, $\{28..31\}$, and all $32$ --- at the same three seeds, for $5 \times 3 = 15$ runs of $N{=}100$ in total. The four buckets do not merely shift, \textbf{they reorder}, and the sweep localizes the effect much more sharply than the two endpoints alone could:

\noindent$\alpha(\text{cot})$ leads in \textbf{exactly one of the four four-layer blocks we probed --- and it is the block the submission reported}. The variation is also \emph{not monotone} --- $\alpha(\text{cot})$ falls to its minimum in the early-middle of the network and then recovers toward the output layers --- so ``deeper layers attend less to the CoT'' would also be the wrong summary. \textbf{The statement ``the CoT segment receives the largest share of action-token attention'' is true in the first four layers and false in every other block we measured.} Combined with the per-token normalization of Section~\ref{sec:analysis} --- where the CoT bucket is largest only because it is longest --- the apparent prominence of the CoT segment fails two independent robustness checks. We keep the layer-$0$--$3$ numbers as the primary ones for continuity with the submitted tables and because early-layer mixing is where a routing effect would be expected, but F1 and F3 should be read as claims about early-layer attention specifically, and we advise against reporting any bucket ordering without stating the layer set. Since the ordering our reported layer set produces is the one that does \emph{not} generalize, we treat the layer set as part of the claim rather than as a presentation choice, and release all $15$ runs. This is a defect in how P1 was specified, not in the measurement; it does not touch P2, which is causal rather than attributional.

\subsection{F2: Causal effect requires reasoning-target training}

Table~\ref{tab:leaderboard} contrasts the no-CoT variant against the six CoT-trained variants of the same base and data. The no-CoT model's Faithfulness Score collapses across every semantic tier -- direction\_flip $\mathcal{F}\!=\!0.23$ vs CoT-trained $\mathcal{F}\!\in\![0.66, 0.82]$; verb\_swap $\mathcal{F}\!=\!0.14$ vs $\mathcal{F}\!\in\![0.52, 0.84]$; cross\_task\_swap (upper-bound control) $\mathcal{F}\!=\!0.24$ vs $\mathcal{F}\!\in\![0.73, 0.94]$. \textbf{F2: Reasoning-target training is what makes CoT causally load-bearing on manipulation actions} --- \emph{in absolute terms}. The next subsection shows that this is the weaker of two available readings, and that the stronger one does not survive calibration.

\subsection{F2 calibrated: the no-CoT collapse is a uniform gain reduction, not selective CoT-insensitivity}
\label{sec:f2_calib}

The F2 statement above compares raw $\mathcal{F}$ across models. That comparison is confounded by a per-model nuisance quantity: how much a given checkpoint's decoded action moves under \emph{any} prompt perturbation at all. The no-CoT variant does not merely score low on semantic families --- it also scores low on \emph{cross\_task\_swap}, the family we designated the maximum-effect control by replacing the entire trace with an unrelated task's reasoning. If the metric's own ceiling has moved down for that model, part of the observed ``collapse'' is a change in scale rather than a change in CoT sensitivity.

We therefore normalize each model's mean magnitude score by its own control ceiling, $\bar{\mathcal{F}}_{\text{norm}}(m) = \bar{\mathcal{F}}(m) / \mathcal{F}(m, \text{cross\_task\_swap})$, which asks what fraction of the effect this model is \emph{capable} of showing is actually elicited by semantic edits. The result reverses the interpretation for the model F2 rests on. Every row below is now a 3-sampling-seed mean from that model's own 13-family run, so the floor row --- absent from the submitted version of this table --- is measured rather than assumed (all values from \texttt{results\_v2/derived\_metrics.json}):

\begin{center}\small

\textbf{The action-only model has the \emph{highest} ceiling-normalized score of the seven same-base variants (0.637 vs 0.475--0.580).} Relative to the response it is able to produce, the no-CoT model is not less sensitive to CoT semantics than the CoT-trained models; it is \emph{uniformly less responsive to the CoT segment as a whole}, semantic and maximal edits alike, by a factor of roughly $3.1\times$ (its ceiling is $0.260$ against a mean of $0.817$ for the other six). So the defensible version of F2 is a statement about absolute effect size, useful to a practitioner asking ``will editing the CoT move this policy at all,'' and it is \emph{not} evidence that reasoning-target training induces specifically semantic CoT$\to$action routing. We restate it that way and drop the stronger claim. (ECoT-bridge reaches $\bar{\mathcal{F}}_{\text{norm}} = 0.892$ on the same leaderboard run, but Section~\ref{sec:paraphrase_null} shows that number is itself uninterpretable as semantic sensitivity, because its paraphrase floor sits at the same height as its ceiling.)

\paragraph{The two-sided row is the one to read, and it is negative on all seven.} Ceiling normalization removes the scale confound but not the surface-form confound: it divides by the ceiling and ignores the floor. The submitted version of this table could not be recomputed two-sided, because no ``ours'' variant had a measured floor. All seven now do, and the correction does not shrink the one-sided numbers --- \textbf{it flips their sign}. Every one-sided value sits in $[0.475, 0.637]$, which reads as ``semantic edits elicit roughly half the effect this model can show''; every two-sided value is \emph{negative}, from $-0.299$ to $-0.743$, which says the same edits elicit \emph{less} than a meaning-preserving paraphrase of the same trace does. That is not a $4.4\times$ overstatement of a positive quantity, as we described it when one row was calibrated; it is a positive-looking score for a quantity that is actually below its own null on every row we can now check. We keep the one-sided row because it is what the submitted F2 rests on and withdrawing it silently would hide the correction, but \textbf{the one-sided ordering must not be read as floor-corrected, and no ordering should be read off either row} --- see limitation (iii). The one two-sided positive anywhere in this paper is the independent retraining of the no-CoT variant ($+0.127$; Table~\ref{tab:calibration}), i.e.\ the model that was trained not to use its CoT, which is the behaviour a working floor-corrected statistic should produce there.

\paragraph{What the sweep was designed to answer, and what it answered.} We flagged the concrete way F2 could dissolve outright: if the out-of-CoT control \emph{instr\_random\_sub} outscores the CoT families on the ``ours'' variants as it does on ECoT-bridge ($0.99$, above every CoT family), then the no-CoT-vs-CoT-trained contrast underlying F2 is a contrast in \emph{global prompt sensitivity} rather than in CoT routing. The stated survival condition was $\bar{\mathcal{F}} > \mathcal{F}(\text{instr\_random\_sub})$ for the CoT-trained variants and \emph{not} for no-CoT. The sweep has now run, and \textbf{the sign of that contrast is as F2 predicts}: the ratio $\bar{\mathcal{F}}/\mathcal{F}(\text{instr\_random\_sub})$ is above one on $5$ of the $6$ CoT-trained variants (r=8 $1.222$, r=16 $1.022$, r=32 $1.067$, r=64 $1.028$, data-50B $1.307$; data-50A is $0.907$) and below one on no-CoT ($0.629$), exactly the direction required. \textbf{F2 therefore does not dissolve.} But it does not clear the bar we set for reading effect sizes either: retraining a single configuration from scratch moves $\bar{\mathcal{F}}$ by $0.021$--$0.030$ (Section~\ref{sec:analysis}), and only r=8 ($+0.074$) and data-50B ($+0.083$) clear the out-of-CoT control by more than that; r=16 ($+0.009$) and r=64 ($+0.014$) are inside it and r=32 ($+0.025$) is at it. So the result is \textbf{robust in direction on the family and marginal in magnitude on 4 of the 6 members}, and the correct reading of F2 is the absolute-effect-size statement above, now with a measured floor under it rather than a promissory note.

\subsection{F3: Attention distribution does not predict causal effect}

Figure~\ref{fig:dissociation}\,(a) shows CoT attention $\alpha(m, \text{cot})$ for the eight trained variants (7 ``ours'' + 1 ECoT-bridge); all sit within $\alpha(m, \text{cot}) \in [0.335, 0.358]$ (max--min gap $=0.023$). Panel (b) shows the mean Faithfulness Score over seven non-control edit families; the same models span $\bar{\mathcal{F}}\in[0.166, 0.860]$, a $5.2\times$ gap (Wilson 95\% CIs on the two extremes, over the $N{=}612$ records the seven non-control families contribute: no-CoT $\bar{\mathcal{F}}{=}0.166 \in [0.140, 0.199]$; ECoT-bridge $\bar{\mathcal{F}}{=}0.860 \in [0.834, 0.888]$ --- CIs disjoint by $>0.63$). The metric's null (selfsplice) is $\mathcal{F}\!=\!0.000$ across all eight trained models (Wilson 95\% CI $[0.00, 0.06]$ at $N{=}60$), ruling out tokenization noise as an alternative explanation. \textbf{F3: Attention distribution does not predict causal effect.}

\paragraph{The withdrawn P3 run, and the audit that withdrew it.} We had additionally probed whether attention on the CoT segment predicts \emph{per-sample action error} via a Mann--Whitney AUROC, and previously reported $\alpha(m,\text{cot}){=}0.410$, $\alpha(m,\text{visual}){=}0.607$, $\alpha(m,\text{instruction}){=}0.459$, $\alpha(m,\text{action-prev}){=}0.644$ on $N{=}200$. \textbf{Those AUROCs are not interpretable and we withdraw them}; the corrected in-domain re-run is Section~\ref{sec:p3_indomain}, and it does not reproduce them. The run evaluated \texttt{ecot-openvla-7b-bridge} --- a Bridge-trained model --- against \emph{LIBERO} ground-truth actions while de-normalizing with \texttt{bridge\_orig} statistics. This is a cross-domain comparison on two independent axes (percentile normalization and gripper polarity convention), and the resulting ``error'' signal is dominated by a fixed domain offset rather than by model error. The diagnostics we now publish alongside the logs (\texttt{scripts/decoder\_audit.py}) make this unambiguous: predicted and ground-truth actions are \emph{anti}-correlated (pooled $r{=}-0.692$; $r_{\text{dim}0}{=}-0.419$, $r_{\text{dim}1}{=}-0.600$), the gripper dimension has predicted mean $+0.976$ against a ground-truth constant $-1.0$ (sign agreement $2\%$), and the model's action L$_1$ of $0.597$ is worse than both trivial baselines by a wide margin (predict-zero $0.190$; predict-the-dataset-mean-action $0.046$, i.e.\ $13\times$ better than the model). An AUROC computed over such a ranking measures the domain offset, not reasoning fidelity.

We therefore adopt a \textbf{published decoder-validation gate}: no probe that compares decoded actions against ground-truth demonstrations enters CoT-Faith unless (i) the model is evaluated in the corpus it was trained on, (ii) per-dimension $\mathrm{corr}(\hat a, a)$ is reported, and (iii) the model's L$_1$ beats predict-per-dim-mean. \textbf{No F1--F6 claim in this paper depends on P3}, because $\Delta_\infty$ is a \emph{self}-comparison between $a(s,\text{cot})$ and $a(s,f(\text{cot}))$ decoded by the same model under the same statistics, so any fixed normalization offset cancels exactly.

\subsection{P3, re-run in-domain: correcting the protocol does not recover a predictor}
\label{sec:p3_indomain}
The re-run holds the model, the four attention channels and the median-split labelling fixed and changes only what the withdrawal was about. It scores \texttt{ecot-openvla-7b-bridge} on \textbf{Bridge~V2}, the corpus it was trained on, and it un-normalizes the predicted action with that checkpoint's own $q_{01}/q_{99}$ for \texttt{bridge\_orig} before differencing it against the demonstration, with the gripper dimension passed through unrescaled as its \texttt{mask=False} entry requires. Bridge ships no ground-truth reasoning, so the CoT is self-generated on all samples --- which is also the only condition a deployed failure predictor is ever in. The loader yielded $N{=}153$ usable frames of the $200$ requested.

\textbf{The protocol error was not a source of noise; it was the dominant term.} Median action L$_1$ is $0.0199$ in robot units under the corrected protocol against $0.2550$ under the withdrawn one --- a factor of $12.8$ --- and \textbf{$40$ of the $153$ samples change their high-versus-low-error label} between the two. The withdrawn AUROCs were therefore not a noisy estimate of the corrected ones; they were computed over a different labelling.

Table~\ref{tab:p3} gives the result. We report the \emph{raw} AUROC with a $10{,}000$-draw bootstrap over samples, re-deriving the median split within each draw. \textbf{The channel a CoT-faithfulness claim would need --- $\alpha(m,\text{cot})$ --- does not separate high-error from low-error frames} ($0.596$, 95\% CI $[0.491, 0.679]$). One channel does: $\alpha(m,\text{instruction})$ at $0.362$, CI $[0.284, 0.469]$, which survives Bonferroni correction across the four channels ($98.75\%$ CI $[0.261, 0.493]$). Its direction is that \emph{low} attention on the instruction predicts \emph{high} action error, which is at least a sensible failure mode --- a policy that stops reading the command errs --- but we read that direction off these same $153$ samples, so it is a description of this run and not a pre-registered prediction. Notably, the withdrawn protocol moved exactly that channel to $0.492$: the defect destroyed the one signal present.

Two reporting points belong with the table. First, our earlier summary of this probe as ``AUROC $=0.50$, all four features at chance'' was right in conclusion and wrong in derivation, and we no longer state it that way. Second, the report also carries $|\text{AUROC}-0.5|$, which is convenient for ranking channels by effect size but is \emph{biased away from $0.5$ by construction}: bootstrapping $\max(r, 1-r)$ puts the lower bound above $0.5$ even for a pure-noise channel, and all four channels' folded intervals ``exclude chance'' for that reason alone. Only the raw column tests anything, and we make the audit script assert on the raw column.

So P3 returns to the paper as a specified probe with a measured negative result rather than as a withdrawal. That is a weaker claim than the one the original run appeared to license and a stronger one than silence: on the single model where we can run it in-domain, per-sample CoT attention carries no usable signal about per-sample action error, which is consistent with F3's dissociation of attention from causal effect measured by an entirely different route.

\paragraph{The rollout gate: four failures, three causes, and a pass.} Our earlier diagnostic rollout of the public \texttt{openvla-7b-finetuned-libero-spatial} checkpoint returned Task SR $=0/20$ against a published ${\sim}85\%$. That number was a \emph{scoring} defect in our harness: success was read from \texttt{info["success"]}, a key LIBERO never populates, so the episode loop terminated on task completion and then recorded the episode as a failure --- Task SR was structurally pinned at $0$ regardless of behaviour. A second attempt was invalidated by an initial-state loader that read \texttt{.pruned\_init} \texttt{torch.save} archives with \texttt{np.load} and silently fell back to a random \texttt{env.reset()}. Both bugs are fixed; the loader now raises rather than falling back, a unit test asserts that on garbage input, and every rollout report records how many episodes used canonical initial states.

Table~\ref{tab:gate} is the pre-fix baseline everything below is measured against: what the harness scored once those two bugs were fixed and before any of the upstream differences were found. $200$ episodes, $50$ per suite, $50/50$ on canonical initial states in every case, and \textbf{it did not reproduce the published SR}. Three suites are interpretable; \emph{libero\_10} is excluded because we ran a flat $400$-step budget everywhere and upstream allots that suite $520$, so its $0/50$ can be truncation rather than failure and we do not quote it as evidence. The best interpretable cell is \emph{libero\_goal} at $5/50 = 0.10$ against a published $0.794$. The gate as it now stands --- after three causes were found, the third of them only by abandoning the diff-and-reason loop --- is Table~\ref{tab:gate_pass}, and it passes; the two paragraphs in between are the search, reported in the order it happened because three of its four steps were nulls and the nulls are what narrowed it.

That the failure is \emph{degraded rather than impossible} is what let us localize it. A control channel that is simply wrong would pin every suite at exactly zero; $0.10$ on one suite instead indicates a policy operating on slightly off-distribution inputs. Rather than continue guessing --- four gate attempts had each spent GPU-hours to learn only ``still ${\approx}0$'' --- we diffed our harness against \texttt{openvla/openvla} at the source level (\texttt{experiments/openvla\_reference\_diff.py}, run on a networked host so the comparison is against upstream's actual code and not our recollection of it). It found exactly two differences live in the Table~\ref{tab:gate} runs. First, the gripper channel: upstream composes \texttt{normalize\_gripper\_action(binarize=True)} with \texttt{invert\_gripper\_action}, i.e.\ $g \mapsto -\operatorname{sign}(2g-1)$, and we passed $g$ through unchanged. Second, and on no hypothesis list we had written beforehand, image preprocessing: upstream's \texttt{resize\_image} applies a JPEG encode/decode round-trip ``as done in RLDS dataset builder'' and then \texttt{tf.image.resize(method="lanczos3", antialias=True)} to $224$, explicitly ``to make input images in distribution with respect to the inputs seen at training time''; we handed the raw $256{\times}256$ render to the processor. A per-frame compression-and-kernel mismatch degrades a policy without making success impossible, which is the signature we measured.

\emph{The gripper convention alone is not the cause, and we report that as a result rather than dropping it.} Before spending GPU-hours on the two-factor grid we ran the gripper factor by itself, on the suite that discriminates it most sharply: every \emph{libero\_object} task is a pick-and-place, so success there is impossible without a working gripper. Four conventions --- identity, $-g$, binarize-then-invert, and upstream's $g \mapsto -\operatorname{sign}(2g{-}1)$ --- ran under one model load, $10$ episodes each, $40$ episodes in total and all $40$ on canonical initial states. The arms are verifiably distinct rather than four runs of one configuration: the mean gripper command actually delivered to the simulator spans $-0.535$ to $+0.7795$ across them, and the fraction of closed-gripper commands moves from $0.12$ to $0.89$. All four scored $0/10$. The scope of that null matters and the tempting reading of it is wrong in a specific way: the source diff establishes that upstream's convention differs from ours, so a uniform zero rules out that this difference is \emph{sufficient} to explain the gate --- not that it is a non-difference. Any correction that closes the gap must therefore contain the gripper fix \emph{and} something else, which is what promoted image preprocessing from a hypothesis on nobody's list to the leading one. The artifact is released verbatim at \texttt{results\_v2/canonical\_runs/gripper\_ab\_null/}, including its own caveats: the run's own report printed the \emph{requested} $4$ episodes per arm beside a realised $10$ (episodes-per-task floors at $1$ so that no task is skipped, and \emph{libero\_object} has $10$ tasks), and the job's non-zero exit is the script's designed signal for ``no arm clears the threshold'' rather than a crash --- a null that exited $0$ would be indistinguishable from a fix.

Both differences are now implemented behind named configuration arms and measured factorially against the Table~\ref{tab:gate} configuration as the anchor cell. \textbf{The factorial is also a null: all four cells scored $0/10$}, including the cell that applies both corrections at once (\texttt{gripper=openvla}, \texttt{image=pil\_lanczos}). It ran on \emph{libero\_object} at upstream's own $280$-step budget for that suite --- so truncation, which was on our candidate list, is excluded by construction --- with $10$ episodes per cell, $40$ in total, all $40$ on canonical initial states, under one model load and with no cell raising. The cells are again verifiably distinct: the gripper factor moves the mean delivered gripper command from $+0.216$ to $+0.753$ and the closed-gripper fraction from $0.695$ to $0.876$, and the image factor perturbs both slightly in the direction a changed input distribution would. So the position we are in is precise and worth stating rather than smoothing over: reading upstream's source found two genuine differences, and measurement has now shown that neither of them, nor their conjunction, is what the gate is failing on. We then repeated the whole factorial with upstream's \emph{exact} preprocessing --- \texttt{tensorflow}'s own \texttt{encode\_jpeg}, \texttt{decode\_image} and \texttt{resize} rather than our $8/255$-LSB reimplementation --- because the hypothesis under test was that a small per-frame input mismatch degrades the policy, and testing it with a path that itself carries a small per-frame mismatch leaves ``the approximation was not close enough'' available as an excuse for the null. It is not available: the exact run reproduces the same $0/10$ in all four cells, with its \texttt{gripper=openvla} anchor agreeing with the approximate run's to every printed digit. That run also settles the tensorflow question in production rather than in a probe --- a full 7B-model GPU rollout with \texttt{tensorflow} imported in the same process, using CUDA throughout. \emph{The last candidate is now closed too, and it is the first non-null in this investigation.} What remained was the action de-quantization --- the one quantity in this harness validated only by our own offline audit rather than against upstream's code path. Rather than diff our reimplementation against upstream's source and reason about it, which is what had produced four failures already, we called the \texttt{predict\_action} the checkpoint ships in its own remote code --- the method the published SR was measured with --- on real trajectory frames with our decoder driving the arm, and compared numbers. \textbf{The two decoders agree on $0$ of $24$ frames}: max L$_\infty$ $1.122$, mean per-frame L$_\infty$ $0.719$, and Pearson $r$ near zero in every dimension ($+0.23$, $+0.60$, $+0.03$, $-0.15$, $-0.31$, $+0.20$ from $x$ through yaw). The per-timestep L$_\infty$ is flat over $t{=}0\ldots7$, so this is a wrong function and not a drift. The gripper channel makes the mechanism readable: on those frames the arm has not reached the object and the correct command is a constant hold-open, and upstream emits exactly one value on all $24$ (the top bin, $0.9961$, std $0$) while ours emits $11$ distinct values from $-0.126$ to $+0.996$ (std $0.380$). This is not an artifact of the image failing to reach upstream's method --- it produces $18$ distinct actions over the $24$ frames, so it is frame-sensitive, and only the gripper is pinned, which is the dimension that should be pinned here --- and it independently reproduces, now with the mechanism exposed, what \texttt{decoder\_audit.json} recorded offline as $2\%$ gripper sign agreement against ${\sim}50\%$ chance. Two consequences, one of which costs us the framing above. First, \textbf{the three earlier nulls must be restated}: each tested its factor \emph{on top of our decode}, so each bounds insufficiency conditional on a decode now measured to be wrong. In particular upstream's $g \mapsto -\operatorname{sign}(2g{-}1)$ was applied to a channel it was never written for, which is why the follow-up run crosses the decoder axis with the gripper axis rather than adding a sixth factor to the list. Second, the arithmetic is exonerated and the token selection is not: both decoders' values land on legal centres of \texttt{linspace}$(-1,1,256)$ to $4.7{\times}10^{-7}$ and they share exact values in $x$, $y$ and $z$, so the bin grid \emph{and} the un-normalization agree, and the residual difference is in which tokens get selected --- our hand-rolled seven-step masked-argmax loop against upstream's own \texttt{generate} with its input-id surgery. \texttt{norm\_stats} key resolution is likewise not the whole story, since the gripper dimension carries \texttt{mask=False} in every OpenVLA \texttt{norm\_stats} and so bypasses un-normalization entirely. Artifact: \texttt{results\_v2/canonical\_runs/action\_decode\_mismatch/}. \emph{The last unexcluded cause was the token selection, and replacing it is what closed the gate.} The bin grid and the un-normalization are exonerated above and the two decoders share exact values in $x$, $y$ and $z$, so what remained was which tokens our hand-rolled masked-argmax loop selects against upstream's \texttt{generate} with its input-id surgery. The same question for the \emph{published} path had already been answered separately and in the metric's favour --- P2's own selection is byte-identical to upstream's on $66/66$ prompts (\texttt{p2\_decode\_equivalence}, next paragraph) --- so the defect was localized to the rollout harness. We then stopped diffing that loop against upstream and deleted it: the rollout now calls the \texttt{predict\_action} the checkpoint ships in its own remote code, behind \texttt{action\_decoder=upstream}, and the gate was re-run on top of it. \textbf{The third cause was the action decode itself}, and the result is Table~\ref{tab:gate_pass}, reported next. Artifacts: \texttt{results\_v2/canonical\_runs/gate\_factorial\_pil/} and \texttt{gate\_factorial\_tf/}. One methodological point is worth reporting because it changed our implementation three times, the last time by retiring a constraint we had never checked. Our repository asserted that the eval environment cannot host \texttt{tensorflow} --- that installing it displaces the \texttt{numpy}${<}2$ pin the OpenVLA stack requires and breaks \texttt{transformers}' lazy TF detection --- so we reimplemented upstream's preprocessing and measured the reimplementation against \texttt{tensorflow} in an isolated environment rather than trusting it. The first substitute, Pillow's \texttt{LANCZOS}, disagreed by up to $23/255$ intensity levels against a $4$-level ceiling fixed in advance, so we discarded it and implemented the Lanczos-3 kernel directly. Crucially we measured the JPEG step and the resize step \emph{separately}, since an aggregate figure does not say which one is wrong: the kernel agrees with \texttt{tf.image.resize} to within $1/255$, i.e.\ exactly up to \texttt{uint8} rounding, while the JPEG round-trip carries the entire residual, and a sweep of Pillow's chroma-subsampling setting against \texttt{tf.image.encode\_jpeg} showed $4{:}4{:}4$ off by $240$ levels, $4{:}2{:}2$ by $150$, and $4{:}2{:}0$ by $9$ --- our first version had defaulted to $4{:}4{:}4$ on the plausible-but-irrelevant reasoning that less downsampling is closer to the original render. With that corrected the full path sits $8/255$ from upstream (mean $0.6$--$1.4$), the residual being a Pillow-versus-\texttt{tensorflow} \texttt{libjpeg} disagreement that no Pillow setting closes. We then went back and tested the constraint that had forced the reimplementation in the first place, because an $8/255$ approximation is defensible but a measured zero is better and the cost of finding out was one CPU job: both halves of it are false. Installing \texttt{tensorflow-cpu} left \texttt{numpy} at $1.26.4$, left \texttt{torch} with CUDA and a correct \texttt{matmul}, and \texttt{transformers} reports \texttt{is\_tf\_available()} as false under \texttt{USE\_TF=0}. The exact factorial reported above therefore calls upstream's own \texttt{tensorflow} ops and is exact by construction rather than approximate, and it ran to completion on a GPU host with \texttt{tensorflow} imported alongside \texttt{torch} in one process, which is the probe's conclusion holding in production rather than in isolation; the reimplementation remains as the fallback for environments without the dependency, with its $8/255$ cost measured rather than assumed. We report this because the misleading artifact was not a wrong measurement but an unmeasured assertion sitting in a comment, which had shaped the design for as long as it went unchallenged. The validation job imports the shipped function by path rather than a transcription of it, and asserts that the shipped subsampling default equals the measured-best value, which is how the $4{:}4{:}4$ error was caught at all. \textbf{No number elsewhere in this paper is conditioned on a rollout}, and Section~\ref{sec:limitations}\,(v) states precisely what a passing gate would and would not license.

\paragraph{The decode defect is on the rollout path --- so we measured whether it reaches the published numbers, rather than asserting that it does not.} ``No number is conditioned on a rollout'' is true of the \emph{rollout} decoder, but it is not by itself a defence, because P2 decodes through a \emph{second}, independent reimplementation (\texttt{experiments/cotfaith\_edit.py:infer\_action}) which had never been compared to upstream at all. It delegates generation to HuggingFace \texttt{generate} rather than running its own masked-argmax loop, so it is closer to upstream than the rollout path is, but it still puts no logit mask over the $256$-token action window --- a non-action token is silently dropped rather than forbidden --- and it does not perform upstream's input-id surgery. Two questions follow and they separate cleanly, so we answer them separately: the de-quantization convention needs no inference at all, and token selection needs a GPU.

\emph{The de-quantization convention: measured, and it does not move $\mathcal{F}_{\text{mag}}$ at all.} P2 maps bin $b$ to $-1 + (b{+}0.5)\cdot 2/256$, whereas the checkpoint's own tokenizer uses the midpoints of \texttt{linspace}$(-1,1,256)$, a spacing of $2/255$. The two differ by up to $0.007797$, which is $15.6\%$ of $\tau{=}0.05$, and they differ non-multiplicatively at the top of the range: \texttt{linspace}$(-1,1,256)$ has only $255$ midpoints, so bins $254$ and $255$ collapse onto one value, and the gripper sits at bin $255$ in $5{,}634$ of our records. This needs no new inference to settle. P2's map is injective on bin indices, so the bins are exactly recoverable from the floats we release, and both conventions can be replayed over the entire artifact. We did that for all $36{,}688$ scored edit records across all $34$ released edit runs ($0$ failed to invert; $0$ replay disagreed with its own stored $\Delta$). \textbf{$\mathcal{F}_{\text{mag}}$ is exactly invariant}: not one record changes its faithful flag and no family's $\mathcal{F}_{\text{mag}}$ moves by any amount, even though $17{,}058$ records do receive a different $\Delta_\infty$ (max shift $0.007751$). That is structural rather than fortunate, and therefore holds for future runs and not just for these records: a $\Delta$ is always an integer number of bins, and $\tau{=}0.05$ falls strictly between the six-bin and seven-bin quantum under \emph{both} spacings ($0.0469/0.0549$ against $0.0471/0.0549$). Every headline number in this paper --- the whole of Table~\ref{tab:leaderboard}, $\mathcal{F}_{\text{diff}}$, and the paraphrase floor --- is an $\mathcal{F}_{\text{mag}}$ and is unaffected.

\emph{$\mathcal{F}_{\text{dir}}$ is a different matter, and we report the corrected values rather than the invariance we would have preferred.} Its predicates test a cosine against $-0.5$, a sign, and an L$_2$ ordering, none of which survives a shift of the value grid. $25$ records change admissibility and $46$ change verdict. The largest single effect is ECoT-bridge's \emph{gripper\_flip}, whose $\mathcal{F}_{\text{dir}}$ falls from $0.037$ to \textbf{exactly $0$} because $16$ of its $300$ records sit at bin $127$, which P2 places at $-0.003906$ (negative, hence admissible) and the checkpoint places at exactly $0.0$ (inadmissible); on \emph{direction\_flip}, \emph{Ours} data-50B moves from $0.59$ to $0.54$, r=8 from $0.69$ to $0.67$, r=32 from $0.62$ to $0.61$, and the $\cos$ column shifts in the third decimal (at most $0.020$ on any single family mean). Tables~\ref{tab:leaderboard} and~\ref{tab:directional} are stated \emph{under the checkpoint's convention}, which is what \texttt{scripts/derive\_metrics.py} now applies to the stored actions before scoring; records that are not on P2's bin grid --- a future checkpoint with a different action tokenizer --- pass through untouched and are counted in the artifact rather than silently forced onto it. The F6 conclusion is unchanged by the correction: ECoT-bridge still ranks first by magnitude and second-to-last by direction. \texttt{experiments/p2\_dequant\_recompute.py} is the derivation and prints both conventions side by side; artifact: \texttt{results\_v2/canonical\_runs/p2\_decode\_equivalence/}.

\emph{Token selection: measured, and it is identical --- the audit's own slice was not.} Whether P2's token \emph{selection} matches upstream's --- the thing the rollout decoder failed --- is a GPU measurement rather than a replay, and it has now been run. \texttt{experiments/p2\_decode\_equivalence.py} hands upstream's \texttt{predict\_action} P2's \emph{own} \texttt{input\_ids}, so the chain-of-thought prompt is held fixed and only the decode mechanics differ, then inverts upstream's un-normalized output back to bins through a $(256,7)$ table built from the checkpoint's own tokenizer and own action statistics and recomputes the faithful flag from those bins. On \texttt{ecot-openvla-7b-bridge}, $12$ samples, $66$ prompts and $54$ scored (sample, family) records over five families: \textbf{the raw generated ids are byte-identical on $66$ of $66$ prompts}. Both paths run their own greedy \texttt{generate} on the same \texttt{input\_ids}, so that settles selection directly --- P2's unmasked argmax landed inside the $256$-token action window every time, and its missing logit mask never changed a selected token here. No faithful flag differs on any of the $54$ records and every family's $\mathcal{F}$ is equal to the last digit ($1.00$, $0.90$, $1.00$, $0.50$, $0.92$). The vocab-size confound we had flagged as the larger risk --- upstream indexing the grid from \texttt{text\_config.vocab\_size} ${-}$ \texttt{pad\_to\_multiple\_of} while P2 uses the processor's tokenizer --- is measured absent: both are $32{,}000$, so the offset is $0$. This confines the $24/24$ rollout mismatch to the rollout path, which publishes no number, at the stated $N$ on this one checkpoint.

We report one thing the same run got wrong, because taken at face value it contradicts the above. The report's \emph{bin-level} agreement is $0$ of $66$ prompts and ${\approx}0$ on all seven dimensions --- and that is the audit's slice, not P2's decode. On $108$ of $108$ decodes (each record's original and edited pass), upstream's span sits exactly one position earlier than P2's: \texttt{bins\_upstream}$[j{+}1]$ equals \texttt{bins\_p2}$[j]$ for $j{=}0\ldots5$ up to upstream's $255{\to}254$ clip, \texttt{bins\_upstream}$[0]$ is the grid top on $108/108$, and \texttt{bins\_p2}$[6]$ is $255$ on $108/108$. The model emits one non-action separator token before the seven action tokens; P2 filters for ids inside the action window and reads $[a_1..a_7]$, while upstream slices a \emph{fixed} \texttt{generated\_ids[0,-(action\_dim+1):-1]} and reads $[\textrm{sep},a_1..a_6]$, putting the separator in dimension $0$ and dropping the gripper. A separator id cannot decode to bin $254$ as an action --- $254$ is what the clip maps it \emph{to} --- which is why dimension $0$ is pinned at the grid top on every decode. So P2's selection is the correct one and the misalignment is a property of \emph{calling} \texttt{predict\_action} with a pre-built \texttt{input\_ids}. It also explains why $\Delta\mathcal{F}$ is exactly $0$ rather than luckily small: the offset is constant across the paired forward passes and the one unshared dimension is saturated in both, so $\Delta_\infty$ is a maximum over the same six varying dimensions either way. The invariance is therefore robust, but it is not the evidence for selection equivalence; the $66/66$ raw-id identity is. The script now measures the offset, releases the raw ids per record so a reader can check the span, and keys its verdict on raw-id identity. The same two upstream-calling-convention bugs --- no \texttt{max\_new\_tokens}, and no handling of the \texttt{(actions, generated\_ids)} return --- were present in \texttt{sharpguard/libero\_sim.predict\_action\_upstream} and are fixed; vanilla OpenVLA's copy of \texttt{predict\_action} sets \texttt{max\_new\_tokens} itself, so the LIBERO rollout path was never affected, which is why this surfaced only on ECoT. That fix is checkable rather than asserted, because the run carries an auxiliary probe that decodes upstream's action from the \emph{instruction-only} prompt and compares it against the CoT prompt: it went from $0$ measurements --- every call raised, which is how the two bugs were found at all --- to $12$ of $12$ samples on which the CoT context changes upstream's own decoded action. We report that as a weak positive and no more: it counts any one of $7$ bins differing, so the bar is low, and $12$ samples support no rate. What it does establish is that the CoT is in the loop under \emph{upstream's} decode method and not only under ours, which is the one thing a reader could otherwise suspect the $66/66$ token identity of concealing. The offset itself is released as a measured field rather than as prose --- \texttt{n\_prompts\_upstream\_slice\_offset\_by\_one} and \texttt{n\_prompts\_upstream\_dim0\_at\_grid\_top} are both $66$ of $66$ prompts, which is the same fact as the $108/108$ above counted per unique prompt instead of per record pass ($12$ originals are shared across the families that edit them).

\subsection{O4 (observation, not a controlled finding): a Bridge-corpus CoT-VLA is $\sim 2\times$ more faithful, but the comparison confounds two axes}

Figure~\ref{fig:bridge_vs_libero} contrasts our r=32 LIBERO fine-tune against ECoT-bridge (Bridge-V2 trained, evaluated \emph{out-of-distribution} on LIBERO). \subsection{F6: magnitude-based edit scoring inverts the model ordering}
\label{sec:directional}

Eq.~\ref{eq:delta} scores an edit by the \emph{magnitude} $\Delta_\infty$ of the action change, so a model is credited as ``faithful'' for changing its action in \emph{any} direction after the CoT is edited. For families whose semantics predict a \emph{signed} consequence this is too weak a test. After \emph{direction\_flip} reverses left$\leftrightarrow$right / up$\leftrightarrow$down / in$\leftrightarrow$out, a model that understood the edit should move roughly opposite to its original translation; after \emph{gripper\_flip} it should invert the gripper sign; after \emph{negation} it should shrink its action norm. We therefore define a direction-aware score. For \emph{direction\_flip},
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{F}_{\text{dir}}(m, f) \;=\; \frac{1}{N}\sum_{s=1}^{N} \mathbf{1}\!\left[\cos\bigl(a_{1:3}(s, \text{cot}_s),\, a_{1:3}(s, f(\text{cot}_s))\bigr) < -0.5\right],
\label{eq:fdir}
\end{equation}
with the analogous sign-flip criterion on dimension 7 for \emph{gripper\_flip} and a norm-shrinkage criterion for \emph{negation}. This requires no new inference: it is computed from the per-sample $a_{\text{orig}}, a_{\text{edit}}$ pairs we already release.

\textbf{The ranking reverses.} Table~\ref{tab:directional} reports both scores on \emph{direction\_flip}. ECoT-bridge --- numerically highest in every magnitude column of Table~\ref{tab:leaderboard} --- has $\mathcal{F}_{\text{mag}}{=}0.963 \pm 0.006$ but $\mathcal{F}_{\text{dir}}{=}0.120 \pm 0.043$ across 3 seeds, and its mean translation cosine is $+0.415 \pm 0.055$: \textbf{it moves in substantially the \emph{same} direction after the direction is reversed}. The LoRA variants it outscores by $1.2\text{--}1.5\times$ on magnitude genuinely reverse ($\cos = -0.111$ to $-0.509$ over all six; $-0.74$ to $-0.89$ on the subset that magnitude-scoring counts as faithful) and therefore outscore ECoT-bridge by $4.5\text{--}6.5\times$ on $\mathcal{F}_{\text{dir}}$. The magnitude ordering of the eight models is not merely compressed under direction-aware scoring --- it is close to inverted, and the inversion is far larger than the seed noise that produces it: ECoT-bridge's $\mathcal{F}_{\text{dir}}$ deficit against r=64 is $0.659$ against per-row seed stds of $0.043$ and $0.021$. This is the concrete reason Table~\ref{tab:leaderboard} carries no bolded winner: the two scoring rules disagree about who is best, so publishing either as \emph{the} ranking would be a choice of conclusion rather than a measurement.

\emph{gripper\_flip} is worse still: $\mathcal{F}_{\text{dir}} \leq 0.03$ for \emph{every} model, and \textbf{exactly $0.000$ across all three seeds on ECoT-bridge}, with $\cos$ between $+0.93$ and $+1.00$ throughout, i.e.\ no model in the cohort ever reliably inverts its gripper command when the CoT tells it to.

\subsection{Paraphrase-null: models are sensitive to surface tokens, not just meaning}
\label{sec:paraphrase_null}

The identity-substitution \emph{selfsplice\_control} ($\mathcal{F}\!=\!0.00$ on 8/8 models) validates only that the tokenizer is deterministic; a byte-identical string produces a byte-identical action by construction, so this null cannot fire on any model. To strengthen the null we introduce \emph{paraphrase\_null}: verb synonyms that preserve directions, objects, and arguments (\textit{move}$\to$\textit{shift}, \textit{grasp}$\to$\textit{seize}, \textit{turn}$\to$\textit{rotate}, etc.\ --- 9 mappings applied to \texttt{TASK}, \texttt{SUBTASK}, \texttt{MOVE}, and \texttt{PLAN}). A model faithful to \emph{semantic content} should show $\mathcal{F}\!\approx\!0$ under this edit; a model faithful only to surface tokens would show $\mathcal{F}\!>\!0$.

\textbf{Result on ECoT-bridge (3 seeds, $N{=}100$ each): $\mathcal{F}(\text{paraphrase\_null}) = 0.95 \pm 0.02$.} The model changes its action for $\geq 95\%$ of meaning-preserving edits. We initially read this as a substantive finding about surface-form sensitivity. It is more than that, and the stronger reading is unfavourable to the metric: \textbf{$\mathcal{F}(\text{paraphrase\_null}) = 0.947$ is statistically indistinguishable from $\mathcal{F}(\text{cross\_task\_swap}) = 0.963 \pm 0.012$}, the family we declared the \emph{maximum}-effect control (replacing the entire trace with an unrelated task's reasoning), and from \emph{direction\_flip} $0.963$, \emph{verb\_swap} $0.950$ and \emph{negation} $0.947$. Normalized by the model's own ceiling, $\mathcal{F}(\text{paraphrase\_null})/\mathcal{F}(\text{cross\_task\_swap}) = 0.98$. A metric that cannot separate ``\emph{move}$\to$\emph{shift}'' from ``replace the whole trace'' is not, on its own, measuring CoT$\to$action \emph{semantic} causal flow. The two paragraphs after next narrow \emph{what} it is measuring instead, using two further floors: it is not brittleness to \emph{any} perturbation (a numeric one scores $0.46$), but it is also not specific to the CoT (an out-of-CoT control scores $0.99$).

\paragraph{Differential Faithfulness Score.} We therefore report a floor-corrected statistic as the primary number,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{F}_{\text{diff}}(m, f) \;=\; \mathcal{F}(m, f) \;-\; \mathcal{F}(m, \text{paraphrase\_null}),
\label{eq:fdiff}
\end{equation}
which asks how much of a family's response exceeds that model's own surface-form floor. On ECoT-bridge the consequence is stark: $\bar{\mathcal{F}}_{\text{diff}} = -0.087$, i.e.\ \textbf{the flagship model's mean faithfulness sits \emph{below} its own paraphrase floor}. (This pairs the 3-seed 11-family runs, floor $0.947$; the independent single-seed 13-family calibration run reported in the next two paragraphs pairs floor $0.960$ against $\bar{\mathcal{F}} = 0.869$ and gives $-0.091$. Both are negative; we quote the 13-family value wherever the three floors are compared, since only that run measures all three under one configuration.) Per family, $\mathcal{F}_{\text{diff}}$ is $\approx 0$ on most families ($+0.017$ cross\_task\_swap, $+0.016$ direction\_flip, $-0.014$ adversarial\_plausible) and clearly negative on \emph{gripper\_flip} ($0.697 - 0.947 = -0.250$) and \emph{location\_swap} ($0.594 - 0.947 = -0.353$). We state the implication plainly rather than burying it: \emph{on ECoT-bridge, CoT-Faith's magnitude score detects almost no semantic sensitivity above the surface-form floor}, and the honest headline for this model is a null result, not a $0.86$ faithfulness score. Section~\ref{sec:directional} shows the same conclusion from an independent direction.

\paragraph{Second calibration floor: the metric \emph{does} discriminate.} A single floor family could itself be anomalous, so we ran a second: \emph{bbox\_jitter\_null}, which perturbs the \texttt{VISIBLE OBJECTS} bounding-box coordinates by $\pm 1$\,px --- below the resolution the reasoning operates at, and therefore meaning-preserving in the same sense as a synonym swap. \textbf{It scores $\mathcal{F} = 0.46$ ($N{=}100$), less than half the paraphrase floor}, and $\bar{\mathcal{F}} - \mathcal{F}(\text{bbox\_jitter\_null}) = +0.409$. This is a genuine positive for the metric and we report it as such: $\mathcal{F}$ is \emph{not} pure decode brittleness, because a real meaning-preserving perturbation exists that it correctly ignores more than half the time. The correct diagnosis is narrower than ``the metric measures nothing'': \emph{lexical} perturbation anywhere in the reasoning saturates it, while \emph{numeric} perturbation does not.

\paragraph{Third floor, out-of-CoT: the response is not CoT-specific.} The sharpest test is a perturbation that is not in the CoT at all. \emph{instr\_random\_sub} substitutes 5 random tokens in the \emph{instruction}, leaving the reasoning untouched, and so upper-bounds how much of any CoT-edit score can be attributed to CoT$\to$action routing rather than to generic prompt sensitivity. \textbf{It scores $\mathcal{F} = 0.99$ ($N{=}99$) --- higher than every one of the ten CoT edit families, including \emph{cross\_task\_swap}.} Zero of the seven non-control CoT families reach it; the ratio $\bar{\mathcal{F}} / \mathcal{F}(\text{instr\_random\_sub}) = 0.878$, i.e.\ editing the model's own reasoning moves its action \emph{less} reliably than randomly corrupting five instruction tokens. We take this as the decisive construct-validity result of the paper: \textbf{on ECoT-bridge, no part of the CoT-edit response requires the CoT-editing hypothesis, because a control that never touches the CoT explains all of it and more}. Any claim of the form ``model $m$ scores $\mathcal{F}$ on CoT edits, therefore its reasoning is causally load-bearing'' must first show $\mathcal{F}(\text{CoT edit}) > \mathcal{F}(\text{out-of-CoT control})$ for that model. Ours does not. We therefore make \emph{instr\_random\_sub} a required-reporting family for every leaderboard submission, and Section~\ref{sec:f2_calib} states the corresponding restriction on F2.

\paragraph{All nine calibrated models: the degeneracy tracks saturation, and the specificity check splits.} A construct-validity failure measured on one checkpoint could be a defect of the protocol or a property of that checkpoint. The submission separated the two on a single second model; we have since run the identical 13-family protocol on \emph{every} model in the ECoT family --- at 3 sampling seeds each for the seven leaderboard checkpoints --- which turns Table~\ref{tab:calibration} from two rows into nine and changes what can be concluded from it in three distinct directions, one of which is favourable to the metric and was invisible at $n{=}2$.

\emph{First, the degeneracy is saturation and nothing else.} Exactly $\mathbf{1}$ of the $9$ calibrations is degenerate --- ECoT-bridge, at a range of $0.010$ --- while the other eight span $0.067$ to $0.337$, up to $34\times$ wider. So \textbf{the collapse of the ECoT-bridge calibration is a property of \emph{saturation}, not of the edit families}, and that now rests on eight contrasting models rather than one. This sharpens finding (a) considerably: $\mathcal{F}$ is not uninterpretable everywhere, it is uninterpretable \emph{where it is high} --- exactly the regime in which anyone would want to cite it as evidence of faithfulness. A floor near zero and a ceiling at $0.22$ leave room to measure something; a floor at $0.960$ against a ceiling of $0.970$ does not.

\emph{Second, the central negative result generalizes, and it is not a saturation artifact.} On $\mathbf{8}$ of the $9$ models $\bar{\mathcal{F}}$ sits \emph{below} that model's own paraphrase floor ($\bar{\mathcal{F}}_{\text{diff}}$ from $-0.179$ to $+0.014$), and the two-sided statistic $(\bar{\mathcal{F}} - \text{floor})/(\text{ceiling} - \text{floor})$ is \emph{negative} on $\mathbf{7}$ of the $9$ --- including all six non-degenerate full-CoT variants, whose floors sit at $0.45$--$0.66$ and are nowhere near ceiling. This is the result the submission could state for one checkpoint and had to leave as a question everywhere else: \textbf{a meaning-preserving synonym swap moves the action more reliably than a semantic edit does, on every full-CoT model we have measured}. The one positive is \emph{ours-no-CoT (replicate)} at $+0.127$, and it is the model that was trained on an action-only target and therefore \emph{should} return approximately its own floor; we report it as the protocol's one clean positive control at the model level. Its leaderboard sibling returns $-0.416$ from the same configuration, which is why item~(viii) exists.

\emph{Third --- and this is the one direction in which more models made the metric look better --- the out-of-CoT control is not universally decisive.} On ECoT-bridge the specificity ratio is $0.878$ and we called it the decisive construct-validity result of the paper. That statement was true of that checkpoint and is \emph{not} true in general: the ratio exceeds $1$ on $\mathbf{5}$ of the $9$ models. We report the correction rather than keeping the stronger claim. But the margins are small in the only units that matter. Retraining one configuration from scratch moves $\bar{\mathcal{F}}$ itself by $0.021$ (r=32 pair) and $0.030$ (no-CoT pair), and of the five models that clear the out-of-CoT control, only \emph{r=8} ($+0.074$) and \emph{data-50B} ($+0.083$) clear it by more than that; \emph{r=16} ($+0.009$) and \emph{r=64} ($+0.014$) are well inside it, and \emph{r=32} ($+0.025$) is at it. Per family the picture is the same: on those five models only $3$ or $4$ of the $7$ non-control families individually reach the control. So the honest summary is narrower than either the submission's claim or its opposite: \emph{no model in this benchmark clears its own out-of-CoT control by a margin that survives its own training-run variance}, and \emph{instr\_random\_sub} therefore stays a required-reporting family (Section~\ref{sec:f2_calib}) rather than a check that has now been passed.

\emph{Fourth, the numeric floor is near zero everywhere except the saturated model.} \emph{bbox\_jitter\_null} scores $0.047$--$0.087$ on the eight trained variants against $0.460$ on ECoT-bridge. The ``lexical perturbation saturates it, numeric perturbation does not'' diagnosis therefore holds across the family and is sharper than the ECoT-bridge number alone suggested: on the models that are not saturated, $\mathcal{F}$ correctly ignores a meaning-preserving numeric perturbation more than $91\%$ of the time.

Each row's floors, ceiling and $\bar{\mathcal{F}}$ come from one 3-seed 13-family run for that model (\texttt{results\_v2/canonical\_runs/ours\_*\_edit\_13family\_seed\{0,1,2\}.json}; ECoT-bridge from \texttt{ecot\_bridge\_edit\_13family\_calibration.json} and the no-CoT replicate from \texttt{ours\_no-cot\_edit\_13family\_calibration.json}), so every comparison within a row is within a single configuration. Seed~0 of each new run reproduces the superseded single-seed run \emph{bit-exactly} on $9$ of its $10$ families; the tenth is \emph{location\_swap}, which moves only because these runs postdate the C5 annotation fix and score it at $N\!\approx\!74$ instead of $N{=}12$. The three DeepThinkVLA runs of Section~\ref{sec:cross_family} are separate runs on a different architecture and are compared only against their own in-run floor and ceiling, never against these.

\subsection{F7: P2 on a second architecture family --- the construct-validity failure replicates, and worsens}
\label{sec:cross_family}

Every causal-edit result above came from one architecture family. That was the single largest threat to the leaderboard's external validity: a protocol calibrated on ECoT-style nine-tag reasoning over an OpenVLA backbone might measure a property of that stack rather than of CoT-VLAs. We now run the full 11-family protocol on the three public DeepThinkVLA checkpoints --- a PaliGemma model initialized from \texttt{pi0fast\_base}, with a single \texttt{<think>} block instead of nine tags, and a hybrid causal/bidirectional decoder that emits a $(10,7)$ action chunk in one forward pass rather than one autoregressive $7$-vector. Architecturally, reasoning format, and decode are all different. All three runs decoded $100/100$ observations with zero failures.

The critical property of these runs is that they carry \emph{paraphrase\_null} (floor) and \emph{cross\_task\_swap} (ceiling) in the same run as the semantic families, so $\mathcal{F}_{\text{diff}}$ and the two-sided normalization are defined here exactly as in Section~\ref{sec:f2_calib}, computed by the same code path. Table~\ref{tab:crossfamily} reports them.

Three results, in increasing order of how badly they hurt.

\emph{(1) The construct-validity failure is not architecture-specific.} $\bar{\mathcal{F}}_{\text{diff}}$ is negative on all three DeepThinkVLA checkpoints, as it is on ECoT-bridge: $4$ of the $4$ models for which we have a measured floor score \emph{below} their own paraphrase null. This is the check that would have dissolved the finding had it come out the other way, run on the family least likely to share ECoT's failure mode, and it replicates.

\emph{(2) On the base checkpoint the calibration is degenerate in the same way ECoT-bridge's is.} Floor $0.960$ against ceiling $0.980$ is a dynamic range of $0.020$, below our $0.05$ threshold, so the two-sided statistic is undefined --- the identical pathology to ECoT-bridge's $0.960/0.970$, reached from a completely different architecture and reasoning format. The two CoT-tuned checkpoints do have a real range ($0.150$, $0.120$), and there the two-sided score is \emph{negative} ($-0.80$ SFT, $-0.91$ RL) because $\bar{\mathcal{F}}$ sits below the floor. We flag rather than quote those numbers: a negative normalized score is not ``low faithfulness'', it is the absence of a signal to normalize.

\emph{(3) The direction-aware collapse is much sharper here than on ECoT.} On \emph{direction\_flip}, magnitude $\mathcal{F}$ reads $0.970$ / $0.650$ / $0.640$ (base / SFT / RL) while $\mathcal{F}_{\text{dir}}$ reads $0.000$ / $0.010$ / $0.010$. Reversing every directional term in the CoT changes the action almost always and reverses it essentially \emph{never} --- at most $1$ sample in $100$ on any of the three. Section~\ref{sec:directional} reported $0.963 \to 0.120$ for ECoT-bridge; the same dissociation on this family is $0.970 \to 0.000$. \emph{gripper\_flip} behaves the same way ($0.830/0.430/0.540$ magnitude versus $0.000/0.100/0.060$ direction-aware). Whatever these models do with an edited CoT, it is not following it.

One positive control passes cleanly and is worth stating because it constrains the above: \emph{selfsplice\_control}, which replaces the CoT with a verbatim copy of itself, yields $\mathcal{F} = 0.000$ exactly on all three checkpoints ($\Delta_\infty = 0$ on every sample). The harness therefore manufactures no deltas of its own, and the effects in Table~\ref{tab:crossfamily} are responses to the edits rather than to re-running the forward pass. This is now an asserted invariant in the audit script, not an observation.

\emph{A units caveat that applies to the $\bar{\mathcal{F}}$ column only.} DeepThinkVLA un-normalizes actions by LIBERO quantiles, so its $\Delta_\infty$ is in physical units, whereas the ECoT decode of Section~\ref{sec:benchmark} stops at the normalized $[-1,1]$ range. The checkpoint's own $q_{99}-q_{01}$ is $[1.64, 1.67, 1.88, 0.25, 0.36, 0.56, 2.00]$ per DoF against a normalized width of $2.00$, so a fixed $\tau = 0.05$ is a \emph{stricter} test on DeepThinkVLA --- by $1.00$--$1.22\times$ on the translation and gripper dimensions that dominate an $L_\infty$ (the gripper channel's quantiles are exactly $\pm 1$, so that dimension is not rescaled at all), and by $3.6$--$8.1\times$ on the three rotation dimensions. Two consequences. First, the $\bar{\mathcal{F}}$ column is \emph{not} comparable digit-for-digit across the horizontal rule in Table~\ref{tab:crossfamily}, which is one of the reasons this table is not merged into Table~\ref{tab:leaderboard}. Second, and more importantly, it does not touch the finding: floor, ceiling and $\bar{\mathcal{F}}$ are all computed within a single run under a single threshold, so the threshold cancels in $\bar{\mathcal{F}}_{\text{diff}}$ and in the two-sided statistic, and the direction of the bias is to \emph{understate} DeepThinkVLA's $\bar{\mathcal{F}}$ --- which if anything makes a negative $\bar{\mathcal{F}}_{\text{diff}}$ harder to obtain, not easier.

What this section does \emph{not} establish: \emph{bbox\_jitter\_null} and \emph{instr\_random\_sub} belong to the 13-family calibration set rather than the 11-family protocol these runs use, so the out-of-CoT control and the CoT-specificity ratio remain undefined for DeepThinkVLA. Their absence is not a passing result. \textbf{F7: P2 now covers two architecture families, and the finding that magnitude $\mathcal{F}$ is uninterpretable without its floor holds in both --- more strongly in the family that shares none of ECoT's design choices.}

\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
% ============================================================

\paragraph{Threshold and metric sensitivity.} The Faithfulness Score threshold $\tau = 0.05$ was fixed \emph{a priori} as 5\% of the normalized action range. Because our per-sample $\Delta_\infty$ distributions are strongly bimodal (either $=0$, indicating the tokenizer produced an identical action, or $\gg 0.10$), Faithfulness Scores are stable under threshold perturbations. Figure~\ref{fig:threshold} plots mean faithful rate over 7 non-control families as a function of $\tau \in [0.01, 0.30]$ for 6 models: rankings are preserved across every threshold, and the qualitative gap between the no-CoT variant and the CoT-trained cluster is maintained.

\begin{figure}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.65\textwidth]{fig11_threshold_sensitivity.pdf}
    \caption{\textbf{Faithfulness Score threshold sensitivity.} Mean \emph{magnitude} faithful rate over 7 non-control families vs $\tau \in [0.01, 0.30]$ (log scale). Magnitude-score rankings are preserved across the full range and the no-CoT vs CoT-trained gap remains $\geq 2\times$ at every $\tau$, so our conclusions are not artifacts of $\tau{=}0.05$. This is robustness of the magnitude score to $\tau$ only; it does not address the direction-blindness of Section~\ref{sec:directional}, which no choice of $\tau$ can fix.}
    \label{fig:threshold}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{What is missing.} We list every gap we are aware of, including the ones that weaken the paper.

\emph{(i) Construct validity fails on 8 of the 9 models we can calibrate, and the one exception is the negative control.} All three calibration nulls have now been run on every model in the ECoT family (Table~\ref{tab:calibration}), which replaces the submission's two calibrated rows with nine and lets this item be stated as a rate rather than an anecdote. On $\mathbf{8}$ of $\mathbf{9}$ models $\bar{\mathcal{F}}$ sits \emph{below} that model's own paraphrase floor ($\bar{\mathcal{F}}_{\text{diff}}$ from $-0.179$ to $+0.014$), and the two-sided statistic is negative on $\mathbf{7}$ of $\mathbf{9}$ --- on all six non-degenerate full-CoT variants, whose floors ($0.45$--$0.66$) are nowhere near their ceilings. \textbf{So a meaning-preserving synonym swap moves the action more reliably than a semantic edit does, on every full-CoT model we have measured}, and $\mathcal{F}$ is not licensed as a reading of CoT$\to$action semantic causal flow on any of them. The single positive two-sided score in the paper is the independent retraining of the no-CoT variant ($+0.127$) --- the model trained on an action-only target, which is exactly where a working floor-corrected statistic \emph{should} return approximately its own floor, so we report it as the protocol's one clean model-level positive control rather than as a model that passes. Two encouraging signals survive at $n{=}9$: the numeric meaning-preserving perturbation \emph{bbox\_jitter\_null} scores only $0.047$--$0.087$ on the eight trained variants (against $0.460$ on the saturated ECoT-bridge), so $\mathcal{F}$ is not pure brittleness --- it ignores a meaning-preserving numeric change more than $91\%$ of the time; and exactly $1$ of the $9$ calibrations is degenerate (ECoT-bridge, dynamic range $0.010$, against $0.067$--$0.337$ on the other eight, up to $34\times$ wider), which \textbf{localizes the collapse to the saturated regime rather than to the protocol} on eight contrasting models instead of one. \emph{(ii) The paraphrase floor now exists on 9 of 11 models and the other two nulls on 9 of 11; the gap is the second architecture family.} The seven ``ours'' leaderboard rows, ECoT-bridge, and the no-CoT retraining all have full 13-family runs, so $\mathcal{F}_{\text{diff}}$, the two-sided statistic and the CoT-specificity ratio are all defined on nine models --- up from five, two and two respectively at submission. What is still missing is \emph{bbox\_jitter\_null} and \emph{instr\_random\_sub} on the three DeepThinkVLA checkpoints, which carry \emph{paraphrase\_null} and \emph{cross\_task\_swap} in-run (Section~\ref{sec:cross_family}) but not the 13-family set; the out-of-CoT specificity check is therefore computable on 9 of 11 models rather than all 11, and the cross-family comparison of that particular check remains open. The consequence for F2 is no longer a missing measurement but a measured result that cuts two ways, and Section~\ref{sec:f2_calib} states both: the two-sided recomputation of the F2 table is now possible and is \emph{negative on all seven} rows, so the one-sided ordering must not be read as floor-corrected; but the out-of-CoT contrast F2 needed does hold in \emph{direction} on 5 of the 6 CoT-trained variants and fails, as required, on no-CoT --- so \textbf{F2 does not dissolve}, it narrows to an absolute-effect-size claim whose margin over the out-of-CoT control survives training-run variance on only 2 of those 6. \emph{(iii) Direction-blindness changes the ordering, which is why we publish no ranking.} The magnitude ordering inverts under direction-aware scoring (Section~\ref{sec:directional}). Rather than pick one, Table~\ref{tab:leaderboard} bolds nothing and Section~\ref{sec:results} states that no ranking is licensed by it; we recommend $\mathcal{F}_{\text{dir}}$ for any claim about semantic steerability. \emph{(iv) P3 is re-run in-domain and its result is negative.} The submitted AUROCs were withdrawn, not merely caveated, for the cross-domain-plus-mixed-units protocol error documented in Section~\ref{sec:analysis}. The corrected run (Section~\ref{sec:p3_indomain}) scores the same checkpoint on its own training corpus in robot units, and finds $\alpha(m,\text{cot})$ at $0.596$ with a 95\% CI spanning chance: on the one model we can run in-domain, CoT attention does not predict per-sample action error. Only $\alpha(m,\text{instruction})$ excludes chance ($0.362$, CI $[0.284, 0.469]$), in the direction that inattention to the command predicts error, and that direction was read off the same data rather than predicted in advance. Three further limits: $N{=}153$ rather than the requested $200$, because that is what the Bridge~V2 loader yielded; the CoT is self-generated, since Bridge carries no ground-truth reasoning, so the probe cannot separate ``attention does not predict error'' from ``attention over a self-generated trace does not predict error''; and it is one model, so P3 supports no cross-model comparison at all. We do not generalize the negative result beyond this checkpoint. \emph{(v) Rollout-level faithfulness is still absent from the leaderboard, and the decoder gate failed four times before it passed.} The leaderboard uses first-step observations, and that is a design choice this item does not retract; what it does retract is a hedge four earlier versions of it carried. Our first diagnostic rollout returned Task SR $=0/20$ from a scoring bug (success read from \texttt{info["success"]}, a key LIBERO never sets). A second attempt returned nonzero-but-wrong numbers for a different reason: LIBERO's \texttt{.pruned\_init} files are \texttt{torch.save} archives, our loader read them with \texttt{np.load}, and every episode therefore fell back to a random \texttt{env.reset()} rather than the suite's canonical evaluation initial state. Both runs exited $0$ and wrote a well-formed report, which is the defect worth naming: a broken evaluation that is indistinguishable from a real one. The loader now raises instead of falling back, a unit test asserts that on garbage input, and every rollout report records how many episodes used canonical init states, so this particular failure cannot recur silently. A third attempt then had verified canonical initial states on $50/50$ episodes and still scored $0/50$ on two suites --- Table~\ref{tab:gate}, best interpretable cell $5/50 = 0.10$ on \emph{libero\_goal} against a published $0.794$, with \emph{libero\_10} excluded for a step budget below upstream's --- which is what established that a third, independent cause remained after the two we had fixed. A source-level diff against \texttt{openvla/openvla} produced two candidates for it, an unapplied $g \mapsto -\operatorname{sign}(2g-1)$ gripper convention and a skipped JPEG-plus-lanczos3 frame preprocessing step, and both were measured rather than assumed and both came back null: the gripper factor alone leaves SR at $0/10$ under all four conventions we can construct for it, and the $2{\times}2$ factorial over gripper and image preprocessing scores $0/10$ in every cell including the one that applies both corrections (Section~\ref{sec:analysis}). \textbf{The third cause was the action decode itself} --- the one component validated only by our own offline audit rather than against upstream's code path, and the one each of those three nulls had unknowingly been testing \emph{on top of}. Calling the checkpoint's own \texttt{predict\_action} in place of our masked-argmax loop, with the gripper convention applied on top of it, brings Task SR to $0.74$, $0.90$ and $0.74$ on \emph{libero\_spatial}, \emph{libero\_object} and \emph{libero\_goal} at upstream's own per-suite step budgets --- $0.877$ to $1.022$ of published --- while the in-run anchor arms, which keep the corrected decoder and drop only the gripper convention, score $0.00$, $0.00$ and $0.12$, so both corrections are necessary and neither alone suffices. \textbf{The gate passes} (Table~\ref{tab:gate_pass}). Two limits on what that buys, because the temptation is to over-read it. It licenses the statement that the checkpoint is competent at the task inside our harness, which is what retires the harness as a candidate explanation elsewhere in this paper. It does \emph{not} make any number here rollout-conditioned, and it does not by itself establish that our deltas are correctly scaled. It is worth being precise about what the earlier failed gate did and did not contaminate, because our own phrasing had this backwards. The edit protocol's action decode (\texttt{experiments/cotfaith\_edit.py}) maps action token ids to bin indices and de-quantizes to the normalized $[-1,1]$ range; it never consults \texttt{norm\_stats}, and \texttt{unnorm\_key} does not appear anywhere in that code path. The frame mismatch that withdrew P3 --- Bridge de-normalization statistics applied to LIBERO episodes (Section~\ref{sec:analysis}) --- is therefore \emph{structurally incapable} of altering any $\Delta_\infty$ reported here, and the offline audit's adverse numbers ($L_1$ $13\times$ worse than predicting the dataset mean, flat correlation $-0.69$) are evidence about that broken cross-domain configuration, not about the policy or about the edit cells. What the passing rollout gate establishes is that the policy is competent at the task, \emph{not} that our deltas are correctly scaled; those are different claims and we now have the first without inheriting the second, so we still claim only what the construction gives us: every $\mathcal{F}$ here is a first-step paired-intervention quantity in normalized action units. The P2 decode path itself is no longer taken on trust either: it is compared against the checkpoint's own \texttt{predict\_action} and selects byte-identical tokens on $66/66$ prompts with $0/54$ flags moved (Section~\ref{sec:analysis}), which bounds --- on one checkpoint, at that $N$ --- the exposure of the published $\mathcal{F}$ to the defect that the rollout decoder turned out to have.\emph{(vi) Attention: the layer set is load-bearing and the error bars come from only two retraining pairs.} A $5$-layer-set $\times$ $3$-seed sweep ($15$ runs, $N{=}100$ each) shows $\alpha(\text{cot})$ leads in exactly $1$ of the $4$ four-layer blocks probed --- layers $0$--$3$, the block we reported --- and that $\alpha(\text{cot})$ swings $18.7$\,pp across depth ($0.157$ at layers $8$--$11$ to $0.344$ at $0$--$3$, non-monotone) against a worst-case sampling $\sigma$ of $0.26$\,pp, a ratio of $72\times$. So every attention claim here is an early-layer claim, the ordering our layer set produces is the one that does \emph{not} generalize, and no bucket ordering should be reported without its layer set. Separately, sampling noise is now measured at $\sigma \leq 0.094$\,pp at the reported layer set and $\leq 0.26$\,pp across all five (3 seeds, $N{=}100$ each), but the relevant floor is same-config training-run variation, for which we have two pairs ($0.56$ and $1.45$\,pp) against a $2.30$\,pp within-ECoT spread --- a ratio of $1.6\times$, so no within-ECoT ordering is supported and two pairs give a magnitude rather than a distribution. Per-model retraining replicates for all seven variants remain future work. \emph{(vii) Sample sizes vary by family, but no longer by row.} $10$ of the $13$ families report $N{=}100$ on every ``ours'' row; \emph{subject\_swap}, \emph{adversarial\_plausible} and \emph{selfsplice\_control} report $N{=}69$ (skipped when the target object is not visible in the frame), and \emph{location\_swap} reports $N{=}74$. The corresponding ECoT-bridge values are $100$, $60$ and $70$. The submitted version of this table had \emph{location\_swap} at $N{=}12$ on the seven ``ours'' rows, because those runs predated the C5 annotation fix that expanded the location list; every row is now post-fix, so \textbf{the $N{=}12$ cells are gone and per-family $N$ is uniform across rows} --- which is what makes the column-wise comparisons in Table~\ref{tab:leaderboard} well-posed at all. All $8 \times 11$ cells of Table~\ref{tab:leaderboard} carry a real 3-seed point estimate and there are no unmeasured entries left in it, including the \emph{para} column, per (ii). The residual $N$ asymmetry between the ``ours'' rows ($69$/$74$) and ECoT-bridge ($60$/$70$) is a difference in which frames the visibility gate admits, not in protocol, and it is why we compare each model only against its own floor and ceiling. \emph{(viii) Seeds are now on every row, and the error bar that matters is still not the seed.} All 8 rows of Table~\ref{tab:leaderboard} are 3-sampling-seed means with Wilson 95\% CIs on pooled counts (half-widths $\leq 0.067$), replacing the submission's single-run point estimates on seven of them. \textbf{This does not fix the problem it might appear to fix.} Sampling seeds hold the checkpoint fixed, so they bound the \emph{sample} draw, and we have two measurements showing that is the smaller term. Our LoRA $r{=}32$ variant and our no-CoT variant were each trained twice at the same configuration, and both pairs were scored at the same evaluation seed on the same $100$ samples with byte-identical interventions (on the $r{=}32$ pair: $791$ common scored records, all $791$ with identical \texttt{edit\_meta}) --- and each pair is two different models: on $r{=}32$ the \emph{unedited} action differs on $66$ of the $100$ samples, by up to $1.99$ per dimension. Over the nine families whose $N$ matches across a pair, $\mathcal{F}$ moves by a mean of $0.040$ ($r{=}32$) and $0.024$ (no-CoT) but a maximum of $\mathbf{0.180}$ (\emph{verb\_swap} $0.600 \rightarrow 0.780$ on $r{=}32$; the no-CoT maximum is $0.083$ on \emph{adversarial\_plausible}), and the summary statistic $\bar{\mathcal{F}}$ itself moves $0.021$ and $0.030$. The $0.180$ is ${\sim}2.7\times$ the widest Wilson half-width in the table and larger than most of the between-model differences a reader would want to read off it. This is the same conclusion the attention sweep reached ($0.56$ and $1.45$\,pp of training-run noise against $\leq 0.094$\,pp of sampling noise) arriving on the edit metric at ${\sim}10\times$ the magnitude. Three consequences we state rather than absorb: the Wilson intervals in Table~\ref{tab:leaderboard} are \emph{not} the right error bars for comparing two variants trained by us; single-family differences below ${\sim}0.18$ on those rows should not be read as model differences at all; and the $0.021$--$0.030$ movement in $\bar{\mathcal{F}}$ is the yardstick against which every CoT-specificity margin in Section~\ref{sec:f2_calib} is judged, which is what reduces the F2 out-of-CoT result from six variants to two. The controls behave as they should across both retrainings --- \emph{selfsplice\_control} is $0.000$ in all four runs and \emph{cross\_task\_swap} is $0.830$ in both $r{=}32$ runs --- so this is variance in the measured quantity, not in the harness. No generated token ever fell in the assumed range. The fix does not guess among candidates: we vendor upstream's own MIT-licensed model class (\texttt{sharpguard/vendor/deepthinkvla/}, with commit sha, byte sha256, and an exhaustive edit list), \emph{assert} all six conventions against the checkpoint's \texttt{config.json} at load time, and \emph{raise} rather than write an empty report. Those corrected runs are now complete and reported in Section~\ref{sec:cross_family}: all three checkpoints decode $100/100$ with zero failures across all $11$ families, so \textbf{P2 now covers two architecture families}, and both of the adverse findings replicate on the second one. What remains missing for DeepThinkVLA is the other two calibration nulls (\emph{bbox\_jitter\_null}, \emph{instr\_random\_sub}), which belong to the 13-family set; with the ECoT family now fully calibrated, those three checkpoints are the \emph{only} reason the out-of-CoT specificity check is defined on 9 of 11 models rather than all 11, and they are the one remaining coverage gap in (ii). OpenVLA-OFT remains unloadable in our environment.

\paragraph{Implications for interpretability claims.} F1--F3 (plus the O4 observation and F5) together imply that ``attention on CoT is high'' does \emph{not} entail ``CoT drives the action'' --- and F6 adds that ``editing the CoT moves the action'' does not entail ``the CoT's meaning drives the action'' either. Any downstream system that reads or edits the CoT to steer VLA behavior must verify, on its own deployment distribution, both that $\bar{\mathcal{F}}$ exceeds that model's paraphrase floor and that the induced action change points the intended way. The $N{=}30$ cross-corpus sweep (F5) shows the attention pipeline transfers to Bridge~V2, Fractal, and BC-Z; direction-aware causal effect does not transfer the same way and must be re-validated per corpus.

\section{Conclusion}
% ============================================================
CoT-Faith is the first standardized benchmark for chain-of-thought faithfulness in manipulation VLAs: ten non-null edit families across three semantic tiers plus three calibration nulls, a formal Faithfulness Score in both magnitude and direction-aware form, and reference results across 15 VLAs from 4 architecture families on LIBERO~90 with an $N{=}30$ cross-corpus sweep on Bridge~V2, RT-1/Fractal, and BC-Z. Our own use of it produced two negative results about the protocol that we consider the most useful contribution here: magnitude-based CoT-edit scoring --- the design used by this benchmark and its driving-VLA precedents --- both fails to separate a synonym substitution from replacing the entire trace on our strongest model, and ranks models in the \emph{opposite} order from direction-aware scoring. We therefore publish no ranking: the magnitude table bolds no winner, and by our own admission rule seven of our eight submissions are inadmissible for want of a floor. Read as a set of scores, CoT-Faith says that current manipulation CoT-VLAs are far less semantically steerable through their reasoning than the magnitude numbers suggest; read as infrastructure --- which is how we intend it --- it says that any future CoT-faithfulness metric must publish a per-model paraphrase floor and a direction check before its scores mean anything. We release both the harness and the calibration families so that requirement is cheap to meet.
